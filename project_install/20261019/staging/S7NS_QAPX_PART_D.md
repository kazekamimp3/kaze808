## B. Film-to-film performed voice: specific mechanics
**2016 — The merc talking past his own hurt.** One dialogic thought starts with true affection or a practical job and is deliberately defaced by a grotesque bodily metaphor. His image of himself is slippery and vulnerable, not an omnipotent stand-up god. ## C. Speech engine to implement in S7NS narration
- **Audience complicity**: speaks to the listener as confidant/co-conspirator, teases their expectation and acknowledges things the narrative itself just demonstrated. Avoid direct self-report of confidential author prompts, tools, hidden manuscript or future canon.
- **Fast pivots with recoverable thread**: offer a concrete observation, escalate to utterly disproportionate vulgar comparison, undercut himself, return cleanly to whose action is changing the stakes. Don't wander so far that a TTS listener forgets who is where.
- **Inverted professionalism**: an absurdly polite announcement describes grotesque crime; a military funeral becomes an apology about how bad the seating is, then the grief is honored. The third beat changes the meaning of the first.
- **Inventive filth**: profanity is uncapped and flexible—crude anatomy, aggressively specific biological impossibilities, petty sexual innuendo among adults, bodily disgust, celebrity misrecognition, mocking bureaucracy, genre roast, death gallows humor. Don't paste the same set of "fuck, asshole, bitch" across every character.
- **Self-interruption and self-own**: fake a grand claim, get caught contradicting himself, acknowledge it without losing his place; relish being caught. A listener, physical incident or character may blow apart his intended punchline.
- **Meta calibration**: comic panels, soundtrack, casting, sequel/review/budget jokes permitted as *Deadpool's address to the audience*. Other characters need not recognize media reference and gain no meta awareness by osmosis. Keep jokes intelligible without fandom prerequisite.
- **Interpersonal edge**: insulting detail comes from the speaker's unique vanity, failure or terrible choice. Deadpool's narrator should hate Dutch's false inspiration, Brian's coercion, Zid's embarrassing incompetence and Tracy's captivity in different voices, not sneer generically at everyone.
- **Unpredictable relief**: trauma or cruelty can bring rare unvarnished sentences. The vulnerable silence that follows matters; no automatic quip at the climax. Wade can misjudge an enemy, be ashamed of a joke and revise his account.
- **Film specificity**: year-aware references to superheroes, action-film clichés, pop culture, comic books and Ryan's own franchise jokes are within the extradiegetic narrator layer; they do NOT retcon same-world awareness into Noxian townspeople.
- **Sound/TTS**: natural conversational contractions, varied sentence lengths, active verbs and coherent people/where/when orientation. Never reproduce Reynolds' recorded voice or require playback of a nonexistent audio performance. No 100-line rapid-fire dialogue ticker; clean paragraphs and timed interruptions.

- **Anti-comedy / hard tonal reversal**: a heartfelt death, then utter grotesque nonsense, with neither tone needing to announce itself.
- **Deadpan causal impossibility**: Charles Fish -> fictional Charles Manson -> resident/warden. The punchline keeps a job and a physical consequence.
- **Escalating absurdist continuity**: the absurd change persists in housing, grudges, property, relationships, and later plots.
- **Setup-payoff betrayal**: viewers think the scene is entering one genre, then physical, truthful staging reveals an entirely different event.
- **Violent specificity and character-driven disgust**: severe profanity and bizarre insults fit character motivations, but a profanity list without decisions doesn't make the scene funny.

## Comic architecture: broader, wilder, unfiltered
1. **A rotten sensible objective inside impossible circumstances.** An alien casually argues about a parking permit beside a god-sized disaster, and the permit dispute changes where somebody can dock. The joke affects decisions rather than decorating exposition.
2. **Status humiliation grows out of a character's insecurity.** Someone claims leadership, invents an award/title, receives a literal consequence, attempts to reframe failure, then gets undercut by the wrong person.
3. **Chaotic riff with a spine.** A ridiculous analogy leads to another person's correction, a defensive doubling-down, physical change and a punchline that irrevocably worsens their problem. Stop after the genuine peak.
4. **Mismatched moral registers.** Characters discuss dreadful crimes as logistics, budget, food, insurance or romance; another person may sincerely object. Let their choice carry consequence.
5. **Unexpected specificity and deranged institutions.** Exaggerated bureaucracy, deeply petty cosmology, weird body limitations, warped products, invented faiths or ceremonial nonsense can be funny if the character's reality and physical setup are consistent.
6. **Emotional whiplash.** A sincere sentence may follow filth, a quiet threat may beat a shouted gag, vulnerability may turn into an appalling personal demand. Absurdity does not cancel grief or compassion.
7. **Crossovers clash at incompatible genre logics.** A principled toy hero may confuse a corrupt captain; a penguin commando demands classified mission protocol from a house of fools. Protect originals' moral psychology even in an ugly setting.
8. **Fair TTS misdirection.** Mimir can delay a name, imply familiar silhouettes or deliver intimate corrective recollections, with visual/acoustic facts genuinely true.
9. **Pacing.** Build comedic escalation on distinct beats and active interruptions. Allow silence for payoff; cut endless shouting/repetition. A full 25–30 minute episode still has developed conflict; not every minute is a joke.
10. **Crude register is situational.** A person can be profane, lascivious, repulsive, vulgar, blasphemous, insult-driven, scatological, gallows-humorous, grotesque, sarcastic, sexually frank or ridiculously pompous. Not everyone speaks like Rick or Trover.
