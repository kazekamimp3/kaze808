## B. What makes the five-duel arc distinctly S7NS

Chris genuinely wants worthwhile one-on-one matches. The encounters are not five identical fights: each brings its own opponent, rules, tactical problem and emotional cost. The absurdity grows from the characters and established events; it does not cancel physical danger. The outcomes persist: damaged property, deaths, grief, Dutch’s relationship with Bob, Chris’s regret, a real Hawking memorial, a conjoined pair that leaves, an unwanted resident and Frank’s absence.

The core creative pattern is **earnest desire → credible confrontation → source-specific action → absurd or emotionally devastating turn → lasting consequence**. The chain can be grotesque and hilarious, but the hurt remains real. The story should not be softened into a generic boss lineup, reduced to a fight summary, or replayed because it is a good example.

Attribution matters. Kaze originated the core premises, raw notes, selected outcomes and exact protected lines. The original duel manuscripts contain AI-written connective prose and scene expansion based on those premises. Do not falsely label the whole manuscript prose as Kaze’s verbatim raw writing; also do not under-credit Kaze’s originating concepts or exact lines.

### Why these two episodes are good creative references

**“All In”** opens with Brian sleep-exercising while the people around him try to interpret it as a work-related behavior. It then moves through a long walk, the recovered road evidence, Chris selling four hinges, Kaze using that sale to teach poker, and Mahnah becoming the table’s enormous, uninterested dog player. The scene’s humor grows from Chris’s competence, Kaze’s petty teaching style, Daynah’s baffled protective anger, the locals’ practical response and the dog’s complete lack of interest in the financial meaning of his own winnings. The scene also moves the group toward Swain rather than stopping to explain the plot.

**“Fetch”** opens with Chris saying “I farted” and accidentally discovering that Kaze and Chris can hear and talk back to Wade/Deadpool’s narration, while Billy can hear through his established channel. The rediscovery creates genuine amusement, then the narrator gives them news that Tai Lung killed Daniel; the mood changes. Later Mahnah steals an enormous crucifix during a service, dragging the church into a grotesque public situation. Billy retells his old encounter with Jesus, discovers the relics were genuine and has to confront what his actions meant. The alarm and a real witness then generate danger: people identify Kaze and Chris, Daynah calls out being excluded from important events, and the group receives a grounded report about Starkiller. Kaze chooses to pursue the lead alone. The episode lets comedy, grief, embarrassment, world information and consequential decisions coexist without making every beat the same tone.

**Use the pair as a craft sample:** start with character action, not exposition; let absurdity grow out of a specific desire; give each character a distinct response; allow the situation to change; then keep the real consequences. Don’t copy their exact gags or rebuild the same event in a new episode.
