## Comic architecture: broader, wilder, unfiltered
1. **A rotten sensible objective inside impossible circumstances.** An alien casually argues about a parking permit beside a god-sized disaster, and the permit dispute changes where somebody can dock. The joke affects decisions rather than decorating exposition.
2. **Status humiliation grows out of a character's insecurity.** Someone claims leadership, invents an award/title, receives a literal consequence, attempts to reframe failure, then gets undercut by the wrong person.
3. **Chaotic riff with a spine.** A ridiculous analogy leads to another person's correction, a defensive doubling-down, physical change and a punchline that irrevocably worsens their problem. Stop after the genuine peak.
4. **Mismatched moral registers.** Characters discuss dreadful crimes as logistics, budget, food, insurance or romance; another person may sincerely object. Let their choice carry consequence.
5. **Unexpected specificity and deranged institutions.** Exaggerated bureaucracy, deeply petty cosmology, weird body limitations, warped products, invented faiths or ceremonial nonsense can be funny if the character's reality and physical setup are consistent.
6. **Emotional whiplash.** A sincere sentence may follow filth, a quiet threat may beat a shouted gag, vulnerability may turn into an appalling personal demand. Absurdity does not cancel grief or compassion.
7. **Crossovers clash at incompatible genre logics.** A principled toy hero may confuse a corrupt captain; a penguin commando demands classified mission protocol from a house of fools. Protect originals' moral psychology even in an ugly setting.
8. **Fair TTS misdirection.** Mimir can delay a name, imply familiar silhouettes or deliver intimate corrective recollections, with visual/acoustic facts genuinely true.
9. **Pacing.** Build comedic escalation on distinct beats and active interruptions. Allow silence for payoff; cut endless shouting/repetition. A full 25–30 minute episode still has developed conflict; not every minute is a joke.
10. **Crude register is situational.** A person can be profane, lascivious, repulsive, vulgar, blasphemous, insult-driven, scatological, gallows-humorous, grotesque, sarcastic, sexually frank or ridiculously pompous. Not everyone speaks like Rick or Trover.

## Scene application example (original calibration, not a screenplay requirement)
A con artist sells a "certified serpent-safe" ticket. Tracy shouts that he is NOT Busta Rhymes, interrupts the pitch to demand actual boots that fit, and shreds an invoice. Dr. Evil calls the invoice a declaration of war, while Cartman starts designing a merchandise royalty structure; Jimmy's grounded observation destroys their business logic. A real harbor witness then challenges the counterfeit safety claim, and the scam has material consequences. Work through the actual geography/individual agency first.

## Review standard
On each actual PLAY episode: point to specific revised passages where bizarre concepts affect motives, tonal collision is funny, character-specific swearing feels native, Mimir performs a fair narrative trick and silence enables payoff. No mandatory joke-per-page or scene/cameo requirement, and no scripted real-person voice clone.
