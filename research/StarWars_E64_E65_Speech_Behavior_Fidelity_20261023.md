## Jar Jar — speech, emotion, movement and decisions

Evidence: the official Databank gives the distinct forms “How wude!”, “Desa rescuin' is hard jobbin'!” and “Dellow felegates!” [S1]. The official film-quote collection also records his plain “Steady. Steady.” [S4]. Therefore an occasional plain short sentence is not itself a fidelity error.

Ahmed Best describes direct, unguarded expression and a character concerned with what is actually happening to others, alongside movement-led performance [S2]. Rob Coleman describes playful mock humility that collapses when Jar Jar is made general, plus a sensitive, thinking person within the slapstick [S3]. Supply Lines supports deliberate distraction in service of aid; Bombad Jedi supports courageous improvisation rather than real Jedi power [S5,S6].

Writing application, inferred from those references:
- Preserve a readable Gungan Basic cadence: mesa/meesa and yousa forms where appropriate, altered demonstratives, contractions and distinctive emphatic word choices. Match a real grammatical function; do not paste -sa onto random English words. This pass does not establish a complete grammar or mandatory spelling for every sound.
- Write thought and emotion before dialect. He may react quickly, protest disrespect, ask an inconveniently direct question, worry, become excited, attempt dignity, or improvise. Compassion is active and sometimes inconvenient.
- Vary register between public representative, flustered social participant, practical companion and genuine alarm. Do not make him an endlessly serene adviser, an infant, an omniscient fool or a hidden Sith.
- His long stride, balance, ears, hands and misjudged reach can alter an exchange. A physical joke needs an actual setup and consequence; no scheduled fall every scene or compulsory incompetence.
- Give him a choice toward his own stated purpose. Helping Brian can complicate that purpose, and he may set a boundary. He need not know Brian murdered Shake or understand the crossed-out lyric without actually seeing/hearing enough.

Specific64–65 repair: “No hurry. Small drinks,” “Working,” “Breakfast,” “Now,” the meals, cups and quiet guidance cumulatively narrow him to caregiving. None of these individual short lines is automatically forbidden. Replace repeated caretaker function with specific curiosity, indignation, purposeful action and physical consequences. His confrontation over the rap must come from what Brian actually exposes, with an independent response rather than a compulsory patient smile.

## Jabba — language as power, not a generic trader

Official history shows coercive bargaining, possession of offered gifts without agreement, enjoyment of humiliating captives and vindictive punishment [S7]. Burtt's first-person account describes Huttese built around the sound/rhythm of Quechua and Larry Ward's performance, then deepened for a heavy voice [S8]. The official Hutt history supports a cultural preference for Huttese and ability to use Basic [S9]. These references do not provide a complete vocabulary for newly invented lines.

Writing application:
- Huttese is the normal spoken language. Brian's lack of comprehension remains real. Important selected Basic exchanges are available; the earlier R6 choice is retained, not retroactively called an error.
- In an English story, mark a translation as audience-facing when used. A readable translated line does not mean Brian or Jar Jar suddenly understood its words. Alternatively show a comprehensible demand through pointing, a displayed object and a brief chosen Basic phrase.
- Give his translated/Basic meaning a specific threat, indulgence, entitlement or price. He can sound amused and patient because he believes he controls the encounter. Do not reduce him to one-word commands followed by laughter.
- He may value Brian's actual information while regarding the performance as grotesque entertainment. A small useful bargain is not respect for Brian's artistry. No automatic confiscation is required by fidelity: the selected informant cultivation and retained saber stand.
- Slow, weighted delivery and guttural laughter are useful contrasts, not a requirement to spell pages of fake Huttese, repeat ho-ho-ho, or place a laugh after every line.
- Do not import Ziro's distinct performance, other Hutts' identities, or a modern generic mafia speech into Jabba.

Specific64–65 repair: “Show me,” “Where?”, “People,” and “Move them” are possible words for him, but the overall role is too thin. Rebuild the conversational leverage and what he actually wants to obtain from the saber/information/entertainment. Preserve Brian's pretend comprehension being exposed. Keep Jar Jar's explicit lack of Huttese comprehension. Do not add a translator character to repair a writing problem.

## Jawas — fast trader speech and materially specific reactions

The Databank establishes scavenging, droid capture and resale [S10]. The official Jawa feature distinguishes outsider-facing Trader Talk from fuller communication among Jawas, including scent, and describes the accelerated Zulu-based sound construction [S11]. Utinni is attested; it is not a universal English translation or sufficient dialogue for every intention.

Writing application:
- Use brief, quick vocal exchanges with pointing, inspection, offering, taking back or physically testing an object. Not every encounter needs a bargaining scene.
- Avoid invented lengthy Jawaese with claimed precise translation. Describe an unknown call when exact wording is not sourced.
- Their replies need not answer Brian's English argument. Observable attention, hesitation and action can expose that mismatch. A repeated sound can be copied without understanding its meaning.
- They are adult participants with work and interests. Let one keep working or reject Brian's interference; no new named foreman, biography or crowd subplot.
- Scent is evidence for their communication, not permission for Wade to decode every sniff or explain a complete secret negotiation.

Specific64–65 preservation/repair: tool-versus-saber interest, visible inspection, shared beat and imperfect imitation of B. Moo are usable nonverbal communication. “One Jawa said something” repeatedly wastes distinct sound/behavior opportunities. Add select concrete vocal rhythm and purposeful action without expanding their screen time. Do not make all Jawas a unified cheering audience who understand the rap.

## Tuskens / Sand People — vocal and signed communication

The Databanks support a nomadic people with disciplined community life, craft and dangerous capability [S12,S13]. The official account of the Mandalorian encounter confirms meaningful sign negotiation [S15]. Kotsur explains designing clear signing around masks, gloves and the inability to rely on facial expressions [S14]. The Boba episode discussion supplies further evidence of training, mourning and social acceptance rather than a single permanent aggression setting [S16].

Writing application:
- Keep their calls and signed/gestured exchanges distinct from Jawa chatter. Render the visible rhythm, posture and hands instead of a generic roar for every response.
- Exact signed words require an established knowledgeable observer or clearly audience-facing translation. Brian does not know a language merely because he sees hand movements. Plain pointing/pantomime can communicate a simple action without full fluency.
- Do not invent exact traditional signs and present them as verified canon, or assume Jar Jar interprets them. No claim that modern ASL and Tusken signing are identical.
- Reserve deliberate stillness, coordinated movement, warning, amusement and refusal as different responses. These people can be skilled and funny; their speech is not proof of stupidity.
- The local scouts can find Brian's involuntary motion funny and elaborate it. They do not require a new named leader or a side story to have agency.

Protected author exception: the adult imitation, Magic Mike clothing moves and ass-cheek flashing are explicitly selected. Keep them. Do not “correct” away the gag because ordinary source portrayal is covered, and do not call it an ancient rite or infer generalized species anatomy. Masks need not come off. Later-series evidence informs communication portrayal; it grants these displaced scouts no unestablished personal memories of Boba or Din.

Specific64–65 preservation/repair: their pointing toward shelter, moving watchers from a real blade, dancing and choosing to sit already communicate without fluent Basic. Preserve those successes. Differentiate their calls/hand responses from the Jawas and retain nonparticipation where appropriate. Narration such as “Nobody understood why” asserts unobserved internal comprehension; show the observable mismatch instead.
