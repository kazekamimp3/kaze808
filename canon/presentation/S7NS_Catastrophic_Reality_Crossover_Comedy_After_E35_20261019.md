## Editorial high standard

**Do not confuse madness with noise.** A stranger shouting obscenities is not a comic engine. A random genre mash-up is not a reveal. Another “funny” ledger deal isn't escalation just because the gang curses more loudly. A monstrous killing with no new choice is not automatically shocking. Same joke spoken by another cast member is not a new bit. Naming an influence does not implement it.

The best S7NS material:
1. Respects deep source fidelity enough to make the absurd intersection only possible *with those exact identities*, bodies, fears, weapons, customs, memories and relationships.
2. Places a fair but initially under-interpreted clue, original person-specific desire or ordinary action before an enormous recontextualization; actor's false theory may be honestly believed.
3. Lets multiple fully autonomous people refuse/cooperate/manipulate/counter in ways that change action, instead of a waiting room of jokes.
4. Shows real material effect: recoil, wound, spoilage, property, screen privacy, hostage consent, new friend, genuine heartbreak, criminal proceeds, institutional response with actual messenger, etc. Consequences persist across the three source episodes and later installments.
5. Includes an unexpectedly sincere observation or silence when warranted; Wade's fourth-wall contempt and Rick-adversarial verbal flourishes may fall quiet for real pain. Neither he nor anyone else gets to falsify past canon.
6. Varies surprise: mistaken source identity, true relation, shocking competence, contradictory moral response, horror->anti-comedy, fake miracle that exploits knowledge of a real miracle, genre change, betrayal from pride, ridiculous literalization of a pragmatic act, small stakes beating cosmic stakes or excellent character victory. Do not require all categories per feature.
7. **Escalates across a whole feature**, not just a vignette: early ordinary choice impacts a later unrelated desire and persistent relationships; funny outcomes from part one become dangerous or affectionate from another angle in part three.
