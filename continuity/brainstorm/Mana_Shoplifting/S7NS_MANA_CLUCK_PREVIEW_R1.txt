Cluck Around and Find Out

Daynuh woke up with one hand resting on a warm rock.

He looked at it.

Looked at the empty place beside it.

Then looked back at the rock, which had apparently been promoted to dog sometime during his nap.

“Mahnuh?”

Across camp, Chris was scraping the bottom of a pan.

“He was there a minute ago.”

“How many minutes?”

Chris inspected the pan as though the answer might be stuck beneath the onions.

“I wasn't timing the fucking dog.”

Daynuh stood.

Now, a missing pit bull is usually a neighborhood problem. A pit bull five feet seven inches at the shoulder is a neighborhood entering witness protection.

I'm Wade. I'll be handling the narration while these men discover that loving an animal and accounting for its whereabouts are two entirely separate qualifications.

Daynuh checked the road. No barking. No crashing. Nothing on fire.

That last part was reassuring until he remembered Mahnuh had never needed fire to make a problem.

“I'll look toward town.”

“I'll check the stream,” Chris said.

He put the pan down, wiped his hands and started along the bank.

Inside BELLY, Catfish Billy watched through the live view from Chris's headlamp. His broad face leaned closer to the second monitor. The first screen's game had been left alone.

“Christopher,” his voice came from the banjo at Chris's side, “your search would benefit from looking somewhere other than your shoes.”

Chris raised his head.

“Checking tracks.”

“You are following a trail of your own feet.”

“Then we've eliminated one suspect.”

Chris hadn't reached the first bend when Daynuh called him back. They found Mahnuh before either search developed into an achievement.

A town watchman came up the road, stopped well short of Daynuh and pointed behind himself.

“Your dog?”

“Where?”

“Cluck Around and Find Out.”

Chris looked up.

“What?”

“The poultry shop.”

Daynuh was already moving.

“Outside?”

The watchman shook his head.

“Inside.”

Daynuh stopped.

“Inside the store?”

“He's made himself fairly central to it.”

The shop had a wide loading entrance, a painted chicken over the door and a roast turning above coals by the front window. Mahnuh couldn't read a fucking word of the sign. Fortunately, the chicken had spent the morning leaking its address into the air.

By the time they arrived, four customers were outside pretending to study a neighboring wall.

The butcher stood behind his counter holding a wooden paddle.

Mahnuh stood on the customer side with his head over the counter.

An empty roasting tray sat beneath his nose.

“There he is,” Chris said.

The butcher looked at him.

“Yes. We've established his location.”

“Mahnuh.” Daynuh pointed toward the door.

The dog's ears shifted. He looked at Daynuh, then at the butcher's hand.

The butcher put the paddle down very carefully.

Mahnuh waited.

“Don't give him anything,” Daynuh said.

“I haven't been.”

Chris glanced at the tray.

Billy's voice rolled out of the banjo.

“An admirably successful policy, sir.”

The butcher stared at the instrument.

“Which one of you said that?”

“Leave it,” Chris told him. “You'll lose the rest of your day.”

Daynuh came beside Mahnuh and touched his neck.

“Come on. You've had enough.”

Mahnuh followed him out.

The butcher let out a breath.

Then Mahnuh turned back.

Everybody stopped breathing again.

He picked up a fallen scrap beside the doorway and left.

Chris watched him go.

“Thorough.”

Daynuh settled the cost of the first missing roast from his own pouch, then led Mahnuh back toward camp.

It could have ended there.

Nobody involved was qualified to let that happen.

The next morning, Chris found Mahnuh coming up the road with a wrapped duck hanging from his mouth.

The dog stopped.

Chris stopped.

The duck swung gently between them.

Inside BELLY, Billy sat forward.

“Well.”

“Quiet.”

“I have scarcely begun.”

Chris looked toward camp. Daynuh sat behind a fallen trunk, repairing the split handle of their cooking pan.

He looked back at Mahnuh.

“Where'd you get that?”

Mahnuh lowered his head.

The duck touched the ground beside Chris's boot.

Chris crouched.

“No.”

He smelled it.

“Oh, that son of a bitch knows what he's doing.”

“The dog?” Billy asked.

“The cook. That's juniper.”

Mahnuh wagged once.

Chris looked toward the fallen trunk again.

“Did you bring this for me?”

The dog's gaze stayed on the duck.

“He's awaiting a ruling on the duck, Christopher. I would not burden the occasion with a wedding.”

Chris tore a small piece from the breast, checked it for bone and held it out. Mahnuh took it gently.

Chris ate a piece himself.

For a moment, they crouched together in the perfect silence of two creatures finding a practical agreement.

Then Chris took off the wrapping.

Billy made a soft, delighted sound.

“There it is.”

“What?”

“The point at which a witness begins disposing of evidence.”

“I'm taking the string off. He'll eat the fucking string.”

“And you have taken the duck into protective custody.”

Chris tapped his thigh twice. Mahnuh came close enough for him to wipe grease from the dog's chin.

“Go on. Act regular.”

Mahnuh walked around the trunk and lay beside Daynuh.

Daynuh looked down.

“Where've you been?”

The dog put his head on his paws.

Chris remained behind the tree with half a duck.

“You say one fucking word—”

“Bring me a proper portion when next you come inside.”

“You're blackmailing me over a duck?”

“I am helping you understand the cost of discretion.”

By the third visit, the butcher had moved the cooked birds away from the front counter.

This worked.

Mahnuh came in, found nothing within easy reach and stood beside the empty tray.

The butcher crossed his arms.

“Not today.”

Mahnuh waited.

A customer entered behind him carrying a basket.

Saw the dog.

Left the basket.

Left the shop.

Mahnuh turned his head.

The butcher closed his eyes.

Outside, Chris arrived in time to see Mahnuh emerge carrying the basket by its handle. A string of smoked meat trailed from it, bouncing against his leg.

“Jesus fucking Christ.”

“An improvement in packaging,” Billy observed through the banjo.

“I'm taking it back.”

Chris caught up and took the handle. Mahnuh released it without a struggle, then sat.

Chris looked inside.

Eggs. Bread. Meat. A small pot of rendered fat.

Mahnuh looked at his hand.

“You're not getting a reward.”

The butcher appeared in the doorway.

“You with him?”

Chris straightened.

“In what sense?”

The butcher stared.

Chris handed over the basket.

“There. All back.”

“That's a customer's shopping.”

“Well, he'll be fucking thrilled. We found it.”

“Your dog keeps stealing my food.”

“Not my dog.”

Mahnuh leaned against him.

Chris braced one leg against the enormous affectionate weight.

The butcher looked down at the dog, then back at Chris.

From the banjo, Billy sighed.

“The defense has acquired a structural difficulty.”

Chris took Mahnuh home without giving him anything.

Halfway there, he stopped beside a water trough and checked the dog's mouth for string.

Mahnuh waited patiently.

“You can't keep going in there.”

Billy chuckled.

“You have made his returns rather pleasant.”

“I fed him once.”

“And washed his face. Removed the inconvenient paper. Welcomed his contribution to supper.”

Chris looked at the banjo.

“You requested a contribution.”

“I recognized an emerging trade.”

Billy moved closer to the monitor as Mahnuh began sniffing the road again.

“Turn your head, Christopher. Toward the dog.”

Chris did.

Mahnuh stopped beside a wheel rut, nose down, then followed it toward town.

“He's following the deliveries,” Billy said.

“How do you know?”

“I don't. Follow him and we'll both improve.”

Chris stood there.

Billy cleared his throat loudly through the banjo.

“Or remain here explaining your superior judgment to a banjo.”

Mahnuh nudged Chris's empty hand.

Chris held it open.

“See? Nothing.”

Mahnuh sniffed it, then turned toward town.

Chris caught his attention before he could start.

“No. This way.”

The dog followed.

Inside BELLY, Billy smiled at the screen.

“He has correctly identified the supply problem.”

By afternoon, Daynuh had noticed three things.

Mahnuh was less interested in supper.

Chris had become unusually interested in washing the dog's face.

And whenever Daynuh asked where either of them had been, Chris answered first.

“He went down by the water,” Chris said.

“Didn't ask you.”

“He can't answer.”

“I know what my fucking dog can do.”

Mahnuh lay between them with his eyes closed.

One ear remained upright.

Daynuh looked at it.

Then looked at Chris.

Chris busied himself with a perfectly clean spoon.

That evening, Daynuh went to town without announcing it.

The next morning, the butcher's old delivery cart was gone.

So was most of his stock.

He hadn't closed. He had joined four other merchants in paying for guarded storage near the harbor. Food would come to the shops in smaller loads. The back room would stay locked. People carrying baskets would be advised to stop abandoning them beside the fucking animal.

Competent measures. Real improvement.

Unfortunately, food continued to smell like food.

Mahnuh found the new delivery route before Chris found Mahnuh.

Chris caught up beside a broad warehouse entrance, where two armed guards watched workers bring crates from a vessel at the surviving berth.

The dog stood outside a wooden rail.

On the other side, the butcher was arguing with a bald man in a high gray collar.

Dutch van der Linde stood beside the bald man.

Chris stopped so abruptly that the headlamp's view lurched.

Inside BELLY, Billy leaned back.

“Oh, Christopher. Your little friend has found an expensive neighborhood.”

Brian stood on a crate near a table, miniature, furious and trying to be included in a conversation happening above him.

“I'm saying the name should be visible from the street.”

Dr. Evil looked toward the street.

“It's a secure storage operation. Why would I want people to know where we've stored everything?”

“Because we're selling a service.”

“Yes. To people who already know where it is.”

Dutch saw Chris.

His expression closed.

“Well.”

Chris looked at the dog.

“We were walking.”

Mahnuh put his nose against the rail.

The butcher pointed.

“That's him.”

Dr. Evil examined Chris, then the dog.

“Which one?”

“Both.”

Billy's voice purred out of the banjo.

“An unfortunate development for the walking explanation.”

Dutch raised a hand toward the guards. They kept their weapons ready but did not lift them.

“What do you want, Chris?”

“My morning back.”

“You followed our suppliers.”

“I followed him.”

Mahnuh breathed deeply through his nose.

From somewhere beyond the rail came the sound of a roasting lid being lifted.

The dog's head turned.

So did Chris's.

The butcher spread his hands.

“There. Look at them.”

Dr. Evil drew Dutch a pace aside, lowering his voice without lowering it enough.

“Is this the organization you mentioned?”

“One of them.”

“And this is how they make contact?”

“I wouldn't presume to tell you what he thinks he's doing.”

Chris pointed at Dutch.

“Smartest fucking thing you've said all morning.”

Dr. Evil turned back.

“If this is an attempt to interfere with my new business, I think you'll find we have made some improvements.”

Mahnuh sat down.

Dr. Evil waited.

The dog looked past him.

Dr. Evil looked behind himself.

A worker was carrying half a roast on a board toward the guards' table.

“Put that inside.”

The worker changed direction.

Mahnuh stood.

Dr. Evil watched him track the board.

“Oh.”

Brian threw both hands up.

“Yes. Oh. It's a dog.”

“I'm aware that it's a dog, Brian.”

“Then stop giving it a criminal organization.”

“I haven't given it anything.”

“You were negotiating with it.”

“I was addressing the man.”

Chris had begun easing Mahnuh away from the rail.

“And the man is leaving. Good meeting.”

“Chris.”

Daynuh stood at the far end of the loading yard.

Chris stopped.

The butcher folded his arms with the weary satisfaction of a man who had successfully brought everyone's father to work.

“You said he'd come back,” he told Daynuh.

“He's a dog. Food didn't stop being food because you moved it.”

Daynuh approached. Mahnuh's tail moved once, then stopped.

Chris spread his hands.

“I came to get him.”

“I can see that.”

“Then we're good.”

“We ain't finished looking.”

Billy's voice emerged, rich and courteous.

“A sensible distinction.”

Chris looked down at the banjo.

“You shut the fuck up.”

Daynuh looked at it too.

“You knew?”

“I became aware through Christopher's excellent picture service.”

“And didn't say anything?”

Billy considered this.

“My judgment was compromised.”

“By what?”

“A portion of duck I have yet to receive.”

Silence moved around the loading yard.

Chris looked at the instrument as though it had just handed somebody his internal organs.

“You hadn't even got it.”

“And already it has cost me dear.”

Mahnuh lowered his head and backed toward Chris.

Chris, distracted, tapped his thigh twice.

The dog moved into place beside him and lifted his chin for cleaning.

Daynuh watched the practiced little arrangement.

Chris's hand stopped halfway to the dog's mouth.

Nobody spoke.

Daynuh nodded slowly.

“How long you two been doing that?”

“He had something on his face.”

“Yeah. Your fucking fingerprints.”

The butcher made a noise that might have been a laugh if it hadn't spent three days becoming something worse.

Daynuh stood in front of Mahnuh.

“You know you're supposed to come back when I call.”

The dog looked up at him.

“And you.” He turned to Chris. “You knew I was looking.”

Chris's expression changed.

“First time, I didn't.”

“After that.”

Chris glanced at Mahnuh.

“After that, yeah.”

Daynuh let the answer sit.

Dr. Evil cleared his throat.

“While this is clearly a touching family matter, the merchants have paid for protection from this particular—”

“And they got it,” Daynuh said. “He's outside your rail. Leave it there.”

Dutch watched Chris's hands, then the guards.

“That's sufficient.”

Dr. Evil looked at him.

Dutch kept his voice agreeable.

“We kept the goods. Customer can see that. No need to give him a battle to remember instead.”

The bald man considered the butcher, the intact crates and the enormous dog who still hadn't entered his warehouse.

“Yes,” he said. “Exactly. A demonstration.”

Brian looked down at the board beneath his feet.

He had been trying to get that much attention for twenty minutes.

Daynuh turned toward the road.

“Mahnuh. Come.”

The dog followed.

Chris followed too.

“Not you,” Daynuh said.

Chris stopped.

Daynuh pointed at the butcher.

“You were helping. Help finish it.”

The butcher picked up an empty basket and held it out.

“These need carrying to the shop.”

Chris looked at the guarded crates, then at the road.

“You're making me deliver the fucking food?”

“You know the route.”

Inside BELLY, Billy began to laugh.

Chris lowered his voice.

“You want to come out and carry it?”

The laughter stopped.

“I would never deprive a young man of honest work.”

Chris took the basket.

By midday he had carried three loads to Cluck Around and Find Out. He put each one down behind the counter, waited while the butcher checked it and returned for another. Nobody shot anybody. The food arrived. The guards kept their goods. An ordinary job was being completed successfully, which somehow made Chris angrier.

On the last trip, Mahnuh appeared beside Daynuh at the shop entrance.

The dog sat outside.

Stayed there.

The butcher looked at Daynuh, then went behind the counter.

He returned with one plain piece of cooked meat.

“For staying out.”

“Ask him to wait,” Daynuh said.

The butcher did.

Mahnuh waited.

Then Daynuh gave the word, and the dog took the meat gently.

Chris watched from behind the counter, sweating through his clothes.

“Unbelievable.”

Billy hummed through the banjo.

“Look at that, Christopher.”

“I fucking see it.”

“The dog has retained his supplier.”

Chris lifted the last crate onto the shelf.

“And?”

“You have acquired a job.”

Mahnuh chewed in the doorway while Chris stood inside the poultry shop, doing unpaid inventory with a supernatural catfish laughing at him from his own belt.

Then the butcher opened the next crate.

Mahnuh's chewing stopped.

His nose rose.

Daynuh saw it.

“Don't even fucking think about it.”

The dog looked away.

Slowly.
