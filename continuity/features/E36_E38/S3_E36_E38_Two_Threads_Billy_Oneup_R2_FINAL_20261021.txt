“Pre-condiment heat. Before you could say medium or mild and have the universe respect either one. Every dumbass who ever went 'how hot can it be'—all their answers in one place. In me. While the planet's carrying on like it's the fucking victim.”

He struck the wall once with the heel of his hand.

“I was right there.”

The words returned, smaller.

Right there.

Inside, Meatwad looked up at Billy.

“Did Chris do something to them?”

“No.”

“Then why?”

Billy rested his hand against the floor.

“I do not know.”

Outside, Chris pulled the cistern chain.

The stored water flushed. The broken supply pipe did not refill it.

He waited.

Nothing.

“Now the fucking toilet's on skim.”

Kaze's heel slid in the dust. Daynuh caught his shoulder before he hit the bathroom wall again.

“Get him out of there,” Arthur called. “People are moving around the front.”

“I'm aware of movement,” Chris shouted.

At the Immortal Bastion, Orra reached the upper passage with dust in her hair and a guard leaning on her shoulder.

Swain waited beside the broken stair.

She gave him what she had: occupied rooms, failed masonry, a severed channel and an old metal door exposed behind the fallen wall. No cause. No name.

“How many still below?” he asked.

“We don't know.”

“Then keep looking.”

“The lower walk needs shoring.”

She told him. He turned to the nearest officer and gave the order.

Only then did he look down toward the exposed arch.

“Find where it began.”

Chris came out carrying the jar from beside the annex. He washed properly at the damaged basin while Arthur watched the passage. Inside BELLY, Frylock turned the exterior monitor back toward the room.

Daynuh watched him dry his hands.

“You done?”

“With the bathroom? I fucking hope so.”

“With the story.”

“One more.”

Kaze looked toward the darkening sky.

“What's bigger than a planet?”

“The Sun. It pissed on me.”

Arthur looked back from the passage.

“Accidentally,” Chris added.

“Wasn't part of it. Wasn't aiming. Just happened to go where I was. After everything else, I get cleaned off because the Sun wasn't looking where it was pissing.”

Chris spread his hands.

“All of it. Pellets. Rings. Hypatia in my eyes. Tadmor's little fucking family tree. Arion's heat. Washed off.”

K.K glanced toward the empty jar.

“A fortunate accident.”

“Yeah. Don't ask me to feel dignified about it.”

For a few seconds Chris looked at the drying water on his hands.

“Skin was clean. Head still knew.”

He put the jar down.

“Then food.”

Arthur waited.

“Learning it, I mean. Once I found out milk had whole versions, I had to know what else somebody hadn't fucking mentioned. Butter. Cream. Cheese. Cow could've furnished a whole apartment and nobody told me.”

“Cheese ain't furniture,” Arthur said.

“Big enough block. Small enough apartment.”

“Wouldn't last.”

“Neither did my first couch.”

K.K opened his mouth.

“Comparison,” Chris said, pointing at him. “Before you ask which planet upholstered it.”

The wooden mouth shut.

“Then I hear 'mother sauces.' Great. The gravy's got a parent. Somebody in the kitchen's being supervised. But you learn what it means. Learn why a sauce splits. Why bread rises. Why something burns outside and stays raw inside. There's an answer, and if the guy teaching you's full of shit, you can try it and find out.”

“I liked that. You could get good. Couldn't go back and unknow the fucking planets, but I could know whether dinner was right.”

“You liked feeding people too,” Billy said through the banjo.

Chris glanced down.

“Yeah. I did. Still do.”

Inside, Meatwad looked toward the kitchen.

Billy let Chris continue.

“I learned knife work. Stock. Sauces. Meat. How to keep food safe. Worked at it until I could run a kitchen. Not just shout near one. Anybody can do that. A guy can stand beside an oven and say 'chef' till he dies. Doesn't mean he knows when the chicken's done.”

Kaze picked a pale chip out of his shoe.

“The chef knew,” Kaze said.

Chris looked through the ruined hatch.

“Yeah. I knew he didn't need the advice.”

Arthur beckoned from the passage.

Chris checked the sling one last time and got up.

“That's where the cooking started. Me wanting to know.”

“Tell the next part walking,” Arthur said.

“That was the end.”

“Good.”

Daynuh had already found a way along the retaining wall. Mahnuh tested it first, stopped where the earth had slipped and chose the firmer stones instead. K.K picked up his stick. Two fingers had splintered against the falling frame; fine new wood was closing the shallow damage.

Kaze watched him look back at the restaurant.

“What?”

“You could've missed my fingers,” K.K said.

Kaze looked at the healing wood. “I did.”

“Woman would've got out.”

“Yes.”

Kaze waited.

K.K leaned down until his long face was close enough to make the answer private.

“Next time I put my hand somewhere, remember that it's mine.”

Then he straightened and stepped past him.

Kaze watched the tree go. The little smile he'd been preparing went unused.

They followed Arthur through the rear passage. He drew both UNSHAKEN revolvers and pressed them together. Dark connective seams drew tight; receivers turned and locked.

HIGH NOON settled across his hands, short and brutal, oversized shotgun barrels above the compact body.

Chris glanced at it.

“Now that's a useful fucking argument.”

A shot struck the wall beyond Arthur's shoulder.

He dropped behind the corner. Across the broken canal, a road-watch marksman was reloading behind a timber shield on a raised stone terrace.

Arthur's red artificial eye tightened around its central lens. It found the firing position and the clear stone behind the man.

HIGH NOON moved in his grip. The same metal lengthened into THE COUNT; the stock settled against his rebuilt shoulder.

He leaned out and fired once.

The physical round punched through the timber and the marksman, striking pale fragments from the stone beyond. The rifle fell over the terrace edge.

Arthur worked the action and caught the spent casing.

“Go.”

They crossed while he covered the opening.

Under the next wall he took the long weapon in both hands and ripped it apart. Each half turned and closed into one of the original revolvers. He checked the cylinders and holstered them.

“You keeping that?”

“Yes.”

“Was just asking.”

“I know. That's how you start.”

They left the Golden Heron with its bill unpaid and its kitchen silent.

On the intact shelf beside the courtyard door, Madam Lin’s order book lay beneath a fallen panel. The dust had covered the prices. Chris’s signature remained visible where the panel had stopped short.

Beyond the town, the road no longer led where it had led that afternoon.

They retraced the damaged road toward the stream. At a broken culvert Daynuh laid a fallen trunk across the gap. Mahnuh tested it before the others followed; K.K stepped over on his long legs.

By the time they reached the camp, the sky had gone purple.

Daniel's branch was empty.

Chris looked down the lower road.

“Motherfucker really is solo gallivanting.”

“He told us,” Arthur said.

“Yeah. Usually when somebody says they're doing their own thing, they mean watching you do yours from slightly farther away.”

Kaze called Daniel's name once. The stream answered by continuing to be a stream.

“No leftovers,” Chris said, toward the distant lights. “You chose an excellent day to be disappointed.”

They had not arranged a return hour. His own gear and money had gone with him. There was no message waiting.

“Let him have his evening,” Arthur said.

K.K inspected the pear tree. It still stood, leaves powdered with pale dust. He brushed one leaf clean, then left the rest.

“Now you look like you've been places,” he murmured.

The empty tub stayed above the bank. He took his stick.

They climbed to the rise overlooking the town.

Kaze stopped beside K.K, one hand in the pocket of his samurai pants. His black shoes were powdered with the restaurant’s pale stone. The skin of his sword hand had closed, leaving dried black blood at the crease of his palm.

He took out the folded fortune.

Chris looked at the little piece of paper.

“You gonna kill that too?”

Kaze’s mouth moved toward a smile.

“Thinking about it.”

“Take five minutes,” Arthur said. “See if anything survives.”

Chris laughed. Daynuh shook his head. K.K watched the paper.

Kaze folded the fortune carefully and put it back in his pocket.

The paper had survived the restaurant.
