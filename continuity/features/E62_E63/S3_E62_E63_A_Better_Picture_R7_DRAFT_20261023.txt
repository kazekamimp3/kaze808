Sand burst over him.

The water container split against a buried stone.

Starkiller lowered his hand.

The squealing cart wheel passed him again.

He turned toward the trader who had brought it.

“The northern landing.”

The man pointed.

Starkiller walked that way.

Behind the dune, Brian spat sand out of his mouth, checked the inscription and found it intact.

He shut the journal.

“Strong initial assessment.”

On the coast, Chris set the first boiled egg behind a rock.

Meatwad was inside Billy, waiting to be let out for the hunt. Chris had spent two silver on the eggs and convinced everyone that a search for objects smaller than a clenched fist could, for once, be an ordinary fucking activity.

Four seconds later, holy light shone through cracks in the shell.

Chris turned around.

A white ray passed across his cheek.

He watched the egg split.

“No.”

The light widened.

“No, we're closed.”

A figure rose through it.

Bare feet touched the earth. Robes settled. Jesus looked around the camp.

Chris put the basket down.

“I can't fucking enjoy anything, can I? I just wanna be an ordinary moose knuckle menace, man. I boil something, I hide it. That's the whole job.”

Kaze sat up.

“Oh, shit.”

Arthur set his cup down.

Daynah put a hand on Mahnah's collar before the dog could investigate the steaming shell.

From Chris's headgear, Billy said, “Cheap entrance.”

Jesus looked at the mask.

Then at Chris.

“Billy?”

Chris smiled despite himself.

“Oh, he knows you.”

“There are men at fairs,” Billy said, “who learn a name before they approach.”

“You ate him.”

“I ate an impersonator.”

“The priest checked his stuff.”

“And clergy have never made a mistake.”

Jesus watched the little speaker.

“You thought I was pretending.”

“I continue to think so.”

Chris laughed.

“Listen to him. He knows damn well.”

Billy's voice grew warmer.

“Christopher, would you mind asking our guest to go scam somebody else's household?”

“Come out and tell him yourself.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“I have responsibilities.”

Inside, Meatwad stood beside Billy's full-sized interior self with an empty bowl, waiting to be released for Easter.

Billy gently moved him away from the exit route.

“Extensive responsibilities.”

Chris folded his arms.

“You told us you owed Jesus an apology. Here he is.”

“A convenient coincidence for a criminal.”

“You're a criminal.”

“Then I recognize the trade.”

Jesus lowered himself onto the rock beside the broken shell.

“You can talk to me.”

“I am,” Billy said. “Through suitable protective equipment.”

Chris shook his head.

“Fuck it. Sorry about my roommate. You need anything? Help getting somewhere? Something to wear on your feet?”

Jesus looked relieved.

“My cross. And the crown.”

Chris stopped.

Kaze leaned forward.

There it was. The exact moment religious tolerance ran into the display cabinet.

Jesus continued.

“You kept them.”

Chris picked up the basket.

“According to who?”

“I remember them.”

“Everybody knows what a cross looks like.”

“The ones Billy swallowed with me.”

Chris looked at Arthur.

Arthur lifted both eyebrows.

“Sounds like he knows.”

“Or somebody told him.”

Billy's voice returned at once.

“Research.”

Chris nodded.

“Targeted.”

“I asked for my things,” Jesus said.

“Your alleged things.”

Jesus looked toward the mask again.

“You said the priest checked them.”

Chris's face changed.

“Why you listening so carefully?”

“You're talking to me.”

“You hear how he talks? Everything's a fucking riddle with this guy. I ask where you're from, I'm from the light. I ask whose cross, I am the receipt.”

“I didn't say either of those.”

“That's how it starts.”

Kaze held his stomach.

“Let him see the crown, man.”

“Why?”

“So he can identify it.”

“That is exactly how theft happens. You bring the thing out to establish what it is, now it's out. Step one accomplished.”

Arthur nodded.

“Dangerous. Man gets to looking at his own belongings, who knows where it'll end.”

Jesus held up his empty hands.

“I won't take them from you.”

Chris immediately looked at the hands.

“The holes aren't proof.”

Kaze went still for a second.

Then his laugh came back worse.

“You were gonna use the fucking holes?”

“I was eliminating a line of argument before it wastes our time.”

Billy murmured through the headgear.

“Experienced operators anticipate verification.”

Chris pointed at the mask.

“Thank you.”

He did not look at Kaze, because Kaze was close to falling off the log.

“Christopher,” Billy said, with genuine satisfaction, “I am so pleased you've remained open-minded.”

Kaze started laughing.

“You was just telling him to apologize.”

Chris pointed at Jesus.

“Before he started casing my fucking house.”

Jesus looked at the basket.

“I came from the egg.”

“Exactly. No appointment. Just straight into the yard. And first thing is where's my cross, where's my crown. You don't ask if I'm all right after a fucking man hatches out of my groceries.”

“You asked what I needed.”

“A ride. A drink of water. Not the museum.”

Arthur turned his cup in his hands.

“Borrowing your own cross. Bold sort of scam.”

Chris looked at him.

“You helping?”

“Trying to understand the crime.”

Billy took the opportunity.

“Do you still claim that your mother is still virgin? Or do you want to admit that she was a dirty whore! She cheated on your father, with the neighbor! Love thy neighbor? She went out there and pulled a Judas, way before Judas.”

Jesus stared at the speaker.

“That's my mother.”

“I've identified the subject correctly.”

“You don't know her.”

“I know she had you.”

Chris held up one hand.

“Billy. Jesus Christ.”

“Yes?” Jesus said.

Billy pounced.

“There. Why did you yell your own name when I swallowed you, if you're actually Jesus?”

“I was frightened.”

“Of what?”

“You.”

Billy paused.

“Well, that part stands up.”

Chris gave the mask a sharp look.

Billy recovered.

“A convincing impersonator would be frightened too.”

“You swallowed the cross with me,” Jesus said.

“Lengthwise,” Billy corrected.

Silence.

Kaze lowered his face into both hands.

Chris looked up at his own headgear as though he could glare through the wiring.

Billy continued.

“A detail the fraud could have acquired from a witness.”

“You were the witness,” Arthur said.

“And I remain unconvinced.”

Jesus closed his eyes.

I have seen a man negotiate with the police while wearing the stolen trousers. I have never seen the trousers ask for proof they had legs.

Chris put the basket under his arm.

“Look. You want an apology for what happened? Talk to him. I just wanted to do Easter for Meatwad.”

“Then do it,” Jesus said.

“With you standing there? He finds one of these and now I gotta explain whether he's meant to put it in the bowl or baptize the fucking thing.”

“I don't need to stay.”

“You need my cross.”

“My cross.”

“See? Keeps coming back to the valuables.”

Jesus stood.

“I didn't ask you to collect them.”

“Everybody always says I didn't ask. Nobody asks. That's the problem.”

Chris set the basket down again, harder.

“I try cereal. Normal bowl-of-cereal behavior. A fucking Eyehole mascot turns up to kill me over breakfast. Not even the shop. The man on the box wants me dead.”

Kaze held up one finger.

“Good cereal, though.”

“That's not the point.”

“Just saying.”

“Then there's Mr. Nimbus. I got a grown man who looks like he sleeps varnished telling me he's some kind of ocean authority. I'm not running an aquarium. I'm trying to survive eating fucking cereal.”

Jesus stayed quiet.

Chris had found the whole drawer.

“Then Micah Bell. Which ought to be simple. Shoot the rat, go home. Except Bob Ross is living in him. Dutch's twin brother. I'm in a gunfight and suddenly the bullet's got a fucking art-school scholarship attached to it.”

Arthur looked down.

“You knew Bob was there,” he said.

“I know. That's why it's complicated. It isn't five normal bastards and then lunch. It's one bastard with somebody else's family tragedy riding around in his fucking meat.”

Billy made an approving sound.

“Choice phrasing.”

“Then Jack. Adult Jack. Clifford. The kid I couldn't be bothered playing as turns up grown and remembers all the shit we did. So now my game has fucking custody evidence.”

“You pushed him off,” Arthur said.

“You helped with the name.”

“I did.”

“Thank you. Shared responsibility. Look at us growing.”

Chris pointed toward the road without looking at it.

“Then Colm O'Driscoll. Except he's not Colm. He's Stephen Hawking. Don't look at me like that. I found out after. And this one”—he tapped the headgear—“went to prehistoric school with him.”

“We were acquainted.”

“You mourned the chair.”

“It was a beautiful machine.”

“You ate it.”

“Grief is individual.”

Chris spread his hands at Jesus.

“That. That's what I live with. Other people have an annoying roommate that leaves a pan in the sink. I have a Jurassic fucking youth pastor eating his class reunion.”

Daynah laughed once.

Chris kept going.

“My fish turns into Charles Manson. I go to sit down, and the decoration's got fucking opinions and wants to live in my house. Meatwad moves in. Then the milkshake and the floating fries, and one of them fucks off to France because apparently I'm the one who has to be reasonable about that.”

His voice caught on the missing milkshake.

He looked toward the submarine, then away.

“And the tree. Keke. You think you get a plant, maybe some shade. No. Family. Another place for somebody to fucking hurt us.”

Nobody interrupted that part.

Chris looked at the cracked shell beside Jesus's foot.

“So I think, eggs. That's got to be manageable. No guns. No goddamn disguise. Not somebody else's dead brother talking through a man I already hate. Boiled eggs. A little hunt.”

He pointed at Jesus.

“And then you lay this white dog shit on me? I mean, what the hell Jesus? If that's even your real name.”

Jesus looked down at the shell.

Then back up.

“It is.”

“That's exactly what you'd say.”

Billy sighed through the speaker.

“Christopher, I tried to warn you.”

Kaze fell backward laughing.

“You was on his fucking side for thirty seconds!”

“New information.”

“He asked for his shit!”

“Allegedly.”

Jesus took one step toward Chris.

Chris lifted the basket between them.

Not a weapon. A barrier made of the last surviving part of his afternoon.

Jesus stopped.

He waited until Chris lowered it.

“Can you at least let Meatwad out?”

Chris looked toward the mask.

Billy had gone quiet.

At last he said, “I suppose an egg hunt needn't suffer because a stranger's conduct has been disappointing.”

Chris inverted. Billy took the exterior, allowed Meatwad down, and yielded back to Chris with the basket.

Meatwad surveyed the scene.

He saw the cracked shell.

“You found one?”

Jesus knelt.

“I suppose I did.”

“That one's mine.”

Chris closed his eyes.

Meatwad picked up half the shell, inspected the empty interior and held it toward him.

“Can you hide it again?”

“Yeah. Sure.”

Meatwad handed it over and turned away to give him a fair chance.

Chris looked at the half shell.

Then at Jesus, who had wisely not asked again for the cross.

He tucked it behind the rock.

For several seconds, nothing happened.

Meatwad found it.

He put it in his bowl.

Chris watched until the next egg stayed an egg.

Across the desert, Brian got up with sand in his shoes, his water sinking into the dune and a glowing endorsement safe against his chest.

He looked toward the yard.

The broken container dripped once.

Brian shut his mouth on the speech he had been preparing and started walking.
