Arthur watched it from behind the field wall. The Count rested across his knees. Beside him, Chris had finally stopped trying to eat yesterday's bread quietly.

Every bite sounded like a horse walking over roof tiles.

“Swallow it or marry it,” Arthur whispered.

Chris pushed the last piece into his mouth.

Kaze lay farther along the wall with his chin on his forearm. Daniel's spear had been entrusted to Billy's storage before they left the hollow. Nothing belonging to Daniel was being volunteered for this morning's experiment.

Daynah stepped into his path.

“Representing Tampon Pandemic Productions, in an exclusive cross-cultural martial activation—”

“His name,” Dutch said.

“Tai Lung. The Jade Juggernaut. The Angry Kitten.”

The handbill dropped down the platform's front.

A childishly drawn paw print followed it.

Tai Lung looked over his shoulder.

Brian, who had just said everything on the card, gave him a professional nod.

Varr struck.

He went for the exposed back of Tai Lung's knee, then reversed the staff toward his head before the first sweep finished. Tai Lung jumped the low strike, turned in the air and caught the descending shaft with both hands.

Varr let it slide instead of wrestling for it.

The iron cap drove toward Tai Lung's throat.

Tai Lung folded away from the point. It missed his muzzle by an inch. His heel hit Varr's breastplate hard enough to shove him across the platform, but Varr kept the staff and dropped behind the far rail.

A lesser fighter might have followed the retreat.

Tai Lung saw the left foot brace.

He stopped.

Varr's thrust came through the rail where Tai Lung's chest would have been. The iron cap punched out a slat. Tai Lung caught the shaft against the broken wood, stamped its middle down and brought Varr over the rail by the leverage.

The human released one hand, rolled across his shoulder and came up with a short knife.

The audience woke up.

So did Harl.

“That isn't part of the exhibition!”

Varr went for the inside of Tai Lung's arm.

Tai Lung let the knife enter reach, turned the wrist with the flat of his palm and drove two knuckles into the man's upper arm. Varr's grip opened. The knife fell. Tai Lung caught it by the hilt and pinned the hem of Varr's coat to the platform.

Varr tore free without the hem.

He came in low, using his armored shoulder. Tai Lung slid past him, caught the back of the breastplate and redirected the charge into one of the thick canopy posts.

The post cracked.

Varr turned with a broken length of timber in both hands.

Tai Lung jumped onto it.

For one suspended instant he stood on the end of Varr's weapon, one foot above the man's face.

Then his other heel came down.

The timber burst against the platform. Varr's helmet hit the boards. Tai Lung landed astride him and struck once beneath the rim of his breastplate, cutting off the breath he needed to get up.

Varr reached for the fallen staff.

Tai Lung put his foot on the reaching hand.

“Finished?”

The man tried to breathe.

Tai Lung increased the pressure.

Varr slapped the boards twice.

A real win. Earned with observation, timing and several violent revisions of where another man's joints were allowed to be.

Brian sounded the horn.

“Give it up for the kitten!”

A pair of hired attendants pulled a bright sash across the platform.

BEST IN SHOW.

One reached for Tai Lung's neck.

He caught the ribbon.

The yard became very quiet.

Dr. Evil stepped up beside the horn.

“We are celebrating you.”

Tai Lung pulled until the sash tore down the middle.

“Then say what I am.”

The doctor glanced at the paw-print banner.

“Extremely difficult to merchandise.”

Tai Lung struck the horn stand.

The brass mouth flew into the drinks table. Spirit sprayed over Harl's newly finished platform. A lamp fell.

Flame traveled across the varnish.

Brian saw the cable running under the far edge.

“Off the rail! It's live!”

He pulled the feed from its connector before a dockworker grabbed the metal horn.

Dr. Evil saw him do it.

Then the platform's burning canopy dropped between Tai Lung and the crowd.

Tai Lung drove through the falling cloth instead of letting it wrap him. A strip caught his shoulder; he ripped it away and kicked the burning post into the empty center of the platform.

Harl pointed at Dr. Evil.

“You brought this!”

“An impressive performance,” the doctor said, backing toward the basin. “Which I would be happy to repeat at a different venue.”

Dutch and Clifford were already at the instrument shed.

The demonstration had given them access. The fire had used up their discretion.

Clifford ignited his real saber against the water gate's exposed chain. One link ran red, parted and dropped. Dutch pushed through with the launch while Brian came down the steps carrying the covers.

A watchman ran from the shed.

Dutch shot him before he could raise his pistol.

Clifford dragged three instrument cases onto the landing. They loaded them low, covered them and cinched the cloth. The small fourth case was still in the upper store.

“Leave it,” Dutch said.

Brian looked surprised.

Dutch pointed at the fire, the watching crowd, the man running for the patrol boat.

“Three we can carry.”

A blue-coated boatman appeared beside the gate with a hooked pole. Clifford caught his coat before he could retreat.

“Which way out?”

“Right. Main basin.”

Dutch hauled him into the launch.

“Name?”

“Merrit.”

“Then sit down, Merrit.”

On the quay, Daynah had taken the loose end of Harl's audience barrier and pulled it clear of the fire, opening a route out for the crowd.

Then he lifted the heavy post at the barrier's other end.

It was brass. Good brass. Hollow enough to carry, heavy enough to sell.

Chris saw it through the monitor.

“You taking that?”

Daynah grinned.

“You busy?”

“Billy. Make room.”

Two guards tried to force their way against the escaping crowd toward the instrument shed.

Daynah laid the post across the service-stair entrance.

The first guard hit it at the knees and went over. The second stopped, saw Mahnah beside Daynah and chose a different route.

Daynah let him choose.

He took hold of the fallen man's sleeve and pulled him out from under the pushing crowd.

Then he pulled the purse off his belt.

The guard looked up.

Daynah looked at the purse.

“Bad afternoon.”

Mahnah's nose touched the man's boot. He froze until Daynah called the dog back.

Kaze watched the whole exchange.

“Thought you was helping him.”

“I did help him.”

Daynah held up the purse, then tucked it away separately for the common count.

“Don't mean he's helping me for free.”

The purse held eight silver. He gave it to Arthur before moving the brass post into Billy's reach.

Chris's voice came through Billy's banjo.

“Look at that brass sign. Nobody's watching the sign.”

“People are burning,” Daynah said.

“That's why nobody's watching.”

Billy scooped the entire bronze admission sign off its stand, inspected the mouth-sized gap beneath it, then swallowed it into storage.

“The lettering appealed to me,” he said.

Kaze watched Tai Lung clear the last of the burning canopy.

The snow leopard saw him watching.

Kaze ran a thumb along his throat.

Tai Lung stepped off the platform toward him.

Arthur moved his weapon into his hands.

Then Harl shouted an order to the men by the launch.

“Stop them! The cases!”

Dr. Evil had not reached his escape boat.

He was still on the pier beside Kaze.

Kaze looked at him, then at Tai Lung.

“Oh, this is gonna fuck your afternoon up.”

Dr. Evil spread his hands.

“I did request a professional event.”

Tai Lung lunged for the doctor.

Kaze drove his shoulder into Dr. Evil, shoving him bodily onto the descending service steps. Tai Lung's fist shattered the post where the bald head had been.

Kaze caught the leopard's forearm.

Tai Lung turned with the contact, planted a foot against the broken post and ripped free before the grip settled.

Kaze grinned.

“After you.”

He kicked the burning platform support sideways.

Not at Tai Lung's body. At the narrow strip of pier leading to the stairs.

It dropped through with a crash, taking the path away.

Tai Lung sprang back onto the sound section. He had room to cross the gap. Kaze could see it. So could Arthur.

Kaze looked at his friend.

For one second he stayed.

“Another time, cat.”

Tai Lung did not chase into Arthur's waiting gun and the unfamiliar giant body beside him. He turned toward Harl, who had locked the upper walkway to keep people out of his store.

Tai Lung hit the lock with the remaining half of the ceremonial sash wrapped around his fist.

The door went inward with the lock still attached.

Dr. Evil reached the launch. Dutch opened the throttle.

Merrit pointed right.

“Inside turn. Slow.”

The canopy did not.

Clifford caught Dutch by his coat before his face entered it.
