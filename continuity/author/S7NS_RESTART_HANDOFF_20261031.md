## 3. Dutch's intentional hypocrisy callback

The two conversations must remain separate and preserve their deliberate contrast:

**Earlier:** Brian wants revenge against the Sins. Dutch tells him, “Revenge is a fool's game.”

**Later:** Dutch lists his own grievances against Brian, including money, Tahiti, and Brian's betrayal of the gang, then begs Dr. Evil to pursue revenge against Brian.

The later request exposes Dutch's hypocrisy. He condemns revenge when Brian wants it, then seeks revenge when he feels personally wronged. Kaze explicitly confirmed this is character-faithful Dutch behavior.

Do not remove, soften, explain away, or accidentally resolve the contradiction. Do not make Dutch conveniently acknowledge his hypocrisy unless Kaze directs it.

## 4. Dr. Evil fidelity grounding and limits

The connected-source review previously surfaced excerpts from the logical owner:
`canon/characters/Dr_Evil_Fidelity_and_TPP_Leadership_20261021.md`.

**Verification limitation:** The complete owner was not retrieved in that review. The points below are excerpt-based and do not constitute a full-owner audit.

The surfaced material characterizes Dr. Evil as theatrical, controlling, self-important, resource-conscious, approval-seeking, and capable of calculated manipulation. He expects people to appreciate his plans, titles, displays, jokes, and menace; their reactions can affect his next line. His delivery can be composed and deliberate, with formal emphasis, short corrections, impatient restarts, failed bids for approval, direct intimidation, oddly intimate concern, self-congratulation, or an embarrassed attempt to continue.

Do not reduce him to pinky gestures, evil laughs, catchphrases, or repetitive shushing.

The excerpts also describe a complicated Brian dynamic: Brian copies Dr. Evil's posture and pinky behavior; Dr. Evil notices and adjusts his own gesture to make Brian wrong. Dr. Evil can give grudging acknowledgment when Brian earns it, while also manipulating Brian's ego and sense of belonging. The “daddy/Scotty” dynamic remains part of their relationship; Dr. Evil chooses whether to call Brian Mini-Me.

Dutch is described as Dr. Evil's right-hand man. Their dynamic can involve Dutch validating broad ambition while reshaping implementation, competing over credit, withholding information, or presenting objections as agreement. Dutch does not automatically control Dr. Evil's decisions.
