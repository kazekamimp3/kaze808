## Vaas research — source-supported traits
Ubisoft's official description of Vaas emphasizes a volatile pirate leader who torments, toys with and threatens Jason, sometimes appearing more interested in testing how far Jason will go than in simply killing him. The official Game Makers Podcast page and its transcript describe Michael Mando's performance as emotionally intense and improvisational; Mando discusses torment, heartbreak and finding a state of flow. In an interview with Far Cry 3 executive producer Dan Hay, Vaas is described as a bully who gets too close and makes the other person feel like a victim. These support the following observable craft traits:
- Sudden, motivated changes in conversational focus, intensity and physical energy.
- Intrusive proximity and attention that make an interaction feel personally targeted.
- Taunting and testing that are about eliciting a reaction, not merely delivering insults.
- A volatile mix of charm, playfulness, cruelty, curiosity and genuine emotional charge.
- Conversational spirals that develop from the immediate person or object rather than polished generic villain speeches.
- Physical performance that carries the thought: gaze, distance, hands, posture, movement and timing change with the exchange.
- A bully's use of social pressure and uncertainty, without assuming every target is helpless or every threat succeeds.

## Application to Kaze — adaptation, not imitation
The S39 owner already contains the committed forty-part Vaas-derived architecture. Use this new direction to activate those existing mechanisms more often and less predictably in the relevant future period, especially:
- Chosen chaos and controlled chaos: he can know a behavior will unsettle someone and choose it anyway.
- Live cognition, spiral talk and recursive phrasing: a small real detail can hijack a conversation, mutate into a personal question, then abruptly land on a practical point.
- Psychological question chains and interpretive testing: he can study how an enemy answers, change the question, challenge their self-image or expose a pretext. His read remains fallible.
- Play-state menace and improvised games: he may make an encounter feel like a game to him while the danger and consequences remain real to the other person.
- Contradictory signals and sudden sincerity: laughter, curiosity, warmth, threat, annoyance or silence can overlap without a mandatory mood-switch formula.
- Physical proximity and action-speech overlap: he may approach, inspect, interrupt himself, change position or act during a sentence when mechanics and circumstances permit.
- Self-aware bad faith: he can recognize that he is choosing the unreasonable option and still do it because amusement, curiosity, intensity, pride or a tactical purpose wins in that moment.
