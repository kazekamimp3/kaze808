S7NS — Season 3, Episode 59: Family Business

Chris put the duck lid down one last time.

Clunk.

Daniel opened one pale eye.

“If that thing lays an egg, I'm leaving.”

“Cast iron doesn't lay eggs.”

“Then quit checking.”

Late afternoon stretched across the dry shelf above the inland road. Below them, a wagon crawled between two dusty bends. Beyond it, broken masonry interrupted what had previously been a perfectly serviceable aqueduct.

Kaze had improved the view considerably, provided your preferred landscape was an insurance claim.

He sat beside Daniel's rock, pulling a stiff, bloody fold of his pants away from his knee. Arthur worked a cloth along the Count. Daynah had settled where his shadow covered Mahnah's resting forepaw.

Daniel drank from his frosted bottle.

“Actually,” he said, “I am leaving.”

Chris looked up.

“Where?”

“Spirit realm. Gonna get wasted with Master Oogway.”

Arthur's cleaning cloth stopped.

Kaze turned his head slowly.

“You're going where?”

“Spirit realm.”

“You just got here.”

“I know.” Daniel looked at the fallen aqueduct. “Busy visit.”

There are announcements that give a room time to prepare. A suitcase beside the door. A carefully worded email. Your dad suddenly describing a waitress as somebody who really understands motorcycles.

Daniel had gone with turtle alcoholism.

Chris rested both hands on the duck.

“Oogway the turtle?”

“Yeah.”

“I didn't know he drank.”

“I'm about to find out.”

“He's got that long neck,” Chris said. “First shot's gonna spend a while getting down there. You might think he's handling it good. Then everything arrives.”

Daniel laughed, shoulders shaking before the sound came out.

“Fuck. Imagine he gives me some wisdom halfway through and just falls backward.”

Kaze wasn't laughing yet.

“You serious?”

Daniel sat up. His tail slipped off the rock and curled loosely beside his bare heel.

“Yeah, man.”

No enemy climbed the slope. No blade opened him. Nothing reached out of the sky to collect a debt.

He was telling them where he wanted to go.

Kaze looked down the road, then back at him.

“Right now?”

“In a minute. I gotta say bye, don't I?”

Kaze stood, took three steps toward the edge of the shelf, and stopped. He turned back with both hands open.

“You could've said something before we sat down.”

“Then you would've been weird the whole walk.”

“I'm not being weird.”

Daniel looked at the three steps he'd taken to stand nowhere in particular.

Kaze looked down at his own feet.

“Fuck you.”

“See? Saved you a whole walk.”

Kaze came back and sat beside him again. Their shoulders touched.

Daniel didn't move away.

From Chris's headgear came Billy's voice, softer than his earlier complaints about the pot.

“Well, you certainly aren't departing my company with a wave from across the damn yard.”

Daniel smiled.

“Wasn't planning to.”

He reached for the spear hooked beneath his foot and offered it to Kaze, shaft first.

Kaze didn't take it immediately.

“Again?”

“Yeah. Keep it for me.”

“You taking the bottle, though.”

Daniel tucked the bottle against his chest.

“I said I was leaving. I didn't say I had a fucking problem.”

That got Kaze. One short laugh, unwilling and real.

He took the spear.

“Still your shit.”

“I know.”

Daniel pulled the small purse from his clothes and held it toward Arthur.

“Same with this. Hundred and ten. Keep it separate.”

Arthur set the Count safely aside before taking the purse.

“You expecting me to charge a storage fee?”

“You'd tell me the price after.”

“Only way to keep a customer.”

Arthur tucked Daniel's money away apart from the common funds. Daniel watched him do it, nodded, and stood.

For a moment he seemed about to make another joke.

Instead, he looked around the shelf.

“I love you guys.”

Chris's thumb stopped rubbing the duck's iron wing.

Daniel swallowed, then breathed through his nose.

“I know we spend most of our time calling each other stupid. And a lot of the time, we got evidence.”

His eyes moved from Arthur to Daynah, from Chris to Kaze.

“But you made me feel like I had a place. Like I could show up all fucked up, say some dumb shit, and somebody would still move over.”

Kaze shifted the spear across his knees.

“Thank you for being my family,” Daniel said.

Nobody helped the sentence by explaining it.

Daniel went to Arthur first. Arthur stood, and the hug began with the familiar shoulder grip of two men who both intended to keep it brief.

It didn't stay brief.

Arthur pressed a hand between Daniel's shoulders.

“Look after yourself, son.”

“You too, old man.”

“I'll make a moderate effort.”

Daynah was already getting up.

“Come here, you drunken little shit.”

Daynah bent and gathered him close, carefully enough that Daniel could keep breathing. Daniel's face disappeared against his shoulder.

“There it is,” he said, muffled. “Whole fucking rib cage just got tucked in.”

“You've been rattling around loose.”

Daynah set him down. His hand stayed on the back of Daniel's head for another second before he let go.

Mahnah lifted his head from his paws.

Daniel crouched beside the resting dog. Mahnah's nose worked over his face. Then his tongue caught Daniel's cheek.

“Yeah, I love you too.”

Daniel wrapped both arms around Mahnah's neck and rubbed the broad muscle there. The dog's tail beat the dirt. Daniel held on until Mahnah decided his ear required cleaning.

“Okay. That's inside my head now.”

Mahnah shoved his forehead beneath Daniel's chin.

Chris stood as Daniel approached. For once, neither of them put a joke in front of the hug.

Chris gripped him hard.

“Take care of yourself, dude.”

“You too.”

Daniel drew back just enough to look at him.

“And actually use the duck.”

“I'm going to.”

“You better. You got it wrapped up like the pope's kidney.”

Chris looked down at the silver-thread cloth beneath it.

“That kidney would've made the trip.”

Daniel laughed against his shoulder.

Then Chris stepped clear of the pot and the others. His body folded through the continuous, deeply impolite inversion that brought Billy outside and carried Chris into their shared interior.

Billy straightened to his full height.

An enormous amount of goodbye available on him.

He crouched and opened his arms.

“Come on, then.”

Daniel stepped in. Billy held him with both hands broad across his back, his usual grand performance reduced to the care he took with the pressure.

“You were welcome in my house,” Billy said. “I want you to remember that.”

“I will.”

Billy released him slowly.

“The others ought to have their chance.”

Billy opened a safe passage from the living space and let Charles, Meatwad, and Shake climb out onto the shelf. Chris stayed inside.

Meatwad came straight to Daniel's ankle.

“You going somewhere?”

Daniel crouched again.

“Yeah, buddy.”

“Well, I ain't going. I got stuff in there.”

“You don't have to.”

“Okay. Then bye.”

He pressed against Daniel, who gathered him carefully for a brief, warm squeeze. Meatwad pulled back and looked at him.

“You smell cold.”

“Been working on that.”

Charles accepted his hug with a firm pat and a searching look, then stepped aside. Shake held his little arms out as though he had personally arranged all of this and found the reception adequate.

“All right. Let's not make it weird.”

Daniel bent to hug him.

Shake patted him once.

“There. That's a good one. You're welcome.”

“Thanks, man.”

The three remained nearby while Daniel returned to Kaze.

Kaze had stood up. The spear was planted beside his foot, his hand around its shaft.

Daniel looked at it, then at him.

“You gonna stand there holding that the whole time?”

Kaze leaned it against the rock.

“Come here, nigga.”

They caught each other hard enough to knock Daniel half a step sideways. Kaze's hand struck his back once, then stayed there.

Daniel shut his eyes.

“Love you, bro.”

“Love you too.”

Neither moved right away.

When they separated, Kaze kept a hand on Daniel's shoulder.

“Go have fun.”

Daniel nodded.

“That's the plan.”

White light began along the edges of his dark blue clothes. It gathered around his bare feet without burning the dirt, outlining his tail and the hand holding the bottle.

Mahnah lifted his head.

Daniel looked at every one of them he could see.

“Thank you,” he said again.

He raised the bottle a little.

Then the light softened through him. Face, shoulders, clothes, tail, bottle: all of him faded together until the shelf was visible where he had stood.

The white glow lingered for a breath.

Then that, too, was gone.

Billy bowed his head. Arthur looked at the empty patch of ground. Daynah rested his hand on Mahnah's back.

Kaze stood beside the spear.

For a while, the only sound was a wagon wheel turning somewhere below.

And there he goes.

Beautiful goodbye. Everybody got a hug. He meant it. They meant it.

Now that he's gone—Kaze, you fucking fraud.

Kaze looked up.

“What?”

Not you, shirtless. Real-life Kaze. The author. The man operating this emotional forklift without a license.

You were going to kill Daniel again.

Then you realized you'd just brought him back, and killing him twice would make you look like a toddler testing whether the same goldfish can survive a second microwave. So suddenly there's a vacancy at Oogway's assisted-living facility for dead alcoholics.

“He's going to the spirit realm.”

Motherfucker, you fired him with a sunset.

From Billy's banjo, Chris said, “He did just get back.”

Exactly! We hadn't even finished processing the return. The man still had resurrection packaging stuck to his ass. Now he's getting a farewell card everybody signed while you stood over them holding a pen.

And I know why the turtle's involved. A regular bar would've left him geographically available. You needed a drinking establishment outside the jurisdiction of a follow-up question.

“Where's Daniel?”

“Having a great time.”

“Can we see him?”

“Absolutely not. Very spiritual.”

Kaze rubbed his face.

“You finished?”

No. Your real-world counterpart found a loophole in grief and stuffed a kung fu pensioner into it.

I respect the hug. I respect Daniel choosing to go. I do not respect the author backing a U-Haul up to the afterlife and pretending the turtle requested a roommate.

Billy glanced toward the empty patch of ground.

“I believe the gentleman said his piece.”

Daniel did. I'm addressing the criminal behind the gentleman.

But fine.

The goodbye stays a goodbye.

Wade Wilson, demonstrating restraint. Somebody write that down before I ruin it by comparing heaven to a retirement-home prostate exam.

Kaze picked up the spear.

“You already did.”

God damn it.

Billy admitted the household back into the living space. Meatwad went first, followed by Charles, with Shake complaining that the dirt outside had been poorly maintained.

Once they were safely inside, Billy inverted. Chris emerged, planted both feet, and stood looking at Daniel's rock.

Then he picked up the duck.

“We should eat.”

Daynah nodded toward the road.

“That cart's stopped twice. Something's wrong with a wheel.”

Arthur stood where he could see around the brush. His red eye narrowed on the slow wagon below.

“Rear left. Man keeps getting down to look at it.”

Kaze glanced toward the distant broken aqueduct.

“Road still open?”

“This part.”

Chris tucked the pot securely under one arm.

“If he's got onions, I'm buying some.”

They took the sloping path down from the shelf. Mahnah walked beside Daynah at an easy pace; nobody dragged him into a hurry. Kaze carried Daniel's spear rather than leaving it on the rock.

The wagon stood beneath a crooked roadside tree. One rear wheel had sunk into a rut, tilting its load of baskets, sacks, and covered wooden trays. A man with a graying beard knelt beside it, holding a broken length of wood and staring at the place where he had hoped it would become a lever.

He looked up at the approaching company.

Then he looked farther up at Daynah.

“Evening.”

“Got onions?” Chris asked.

The man blinked.

“Yes.”

“Thank Christ.”

Billy made a low, displeased sound through the headgear.

Chris ignored him.

The seller's name was Pell. He was taking vegetables, bread, and cooked sausages to the freight yard farther along the road. “Every other evening for eleven years,” Pell said. Today's route had acquired a great deal of unexpected stone.

“Aqueduct's down,” he said.

Kaze looked past him.

“Yeah. Saw that.”

Pell studied the blood on his pants, the spear, Arthur's weapon, and the giant man beside the dog.

Whatever question he had considered asking, he put it away.

Chris crouched by the wheel.

“Axle looks straight. You've just buried the rim.”

“I know what's wrong with it.”

“Then why are you threatening it with half a stick?”

Pell looked at the wood in his hand.

Daynah put his fingers under the rear corner of the wagon.

“Move your foot.”

Pell moved it.

Daynah lifted until the wheel cleared the rut. Arthur rolled a flat stone beneath the low side, and Daynah eased the wagon onto it. The load settled. Nothing snapped.

Pell stood very still.

“Well,” he said. “Thank you.”

“Sell the man an onion,” Daynah said. “He's been suffering.”

Chris bought enough bread, sausages, and onions for the exterior group, with a little extra bread set aside. Arthur paid six silver from the common purse. Daniel's smaller purse stayed closed.

Pell wrapped the food while Chris inspected an onion, turning it once in his fingers.

Kaze leaned closer.

“You gonna apologize to it first?”

“I'm checking for soft spots.”

“Looks nervous.”

“So would you if somebody picked you up by your ass and started squeezing.”

“No diddy,” Kaze said.

Chris pointed at him with the onion.

“You were the one watching.”

Pell made the mistake of trying to be hospitable.

“You can sit under the tree. Stove's still warm. Used it at my last stop.”

On the back of the wagon, a small iron stove held a bed of coals. Chris looked at it, then at the duck.

“That'll work.”

Within minutes he had the pot heating and sausage cut into thick pieces. He browned the meat, reserved a portion to cool before it touched any onion, then added onion to the rendered fat. He worked without fuss, moving the pot to control the heat rather than blaming the stove. The sharp smell softened and turned sweet.

Kaze hovered.

Chris moved the knife to his other side.

“Wait.”

“I didn't do anything.”

“You're looking at that sausage like you came to identify it at the morgue.”

“Maybe I knew him.”

“Then respect the family.”

Pell sat on the wagon's front board. He watched Chris lift the lid, stir once, and put it back.

Steam began to leave the duck's bill.

Chris smiled.

Not a demonstration smile. Not a victory over anybody.

The stupid thing worked.

Kaze noticed. He looked at the bill, then at Chris, and left the lid alone.

Arthur stood near the road, watching a second wagon approach from the direction they had come. It carried crates under a rope net. The driver slowed to ask Pell whether the freight yard was still accepting traffic.

“Accepting it?” Pell said. “They're stacking it on top of itself. Everybody trying to get coastward before the soldiers close another road.”

“Upper bridge?”

“Gone.”

The driver swore with the weary precision of a man whose entire working day had just been put in a sack and beaten against a wall.

Kaze watched him go.

Arthur watched Kaze.

“Busy visit,” Kaze muttered.

He looked down at Daniel's spear.

Then Chris lifted the lid, and everybody moved toward the food.

Pell supplied wooden picks from his serving box. Daynah checked the reserved meat's temperature with his fingers and let Mahnah take it from his palm. Chris folded the onions through the rest.

For several minutes they ate.

The bread was rough. The sausage had more pepper than it needed. The onions were good.

Nobody turned dinner into a memorial speech.

Pell watched Arthur use a piece of bread to collect the fat from his share.

“You looking for work?” he asked.

Arthur raised his eyes.

“Depends who needs doing.”

“Guard work.”

“Ah. The slow kind.”

Pell reached beneath the driver's seat and produced a folded freight notice, stained along one edge where cooking oil had soaked through the wood.

“They've put this out at every stop. Need escorts for the coastal export run. More now, I'd imagine.”

Arthur took it.

The notice offered payment to armed escorts presenting themselves at the inland freight yard before dawn. A temporary convoy would take the lower road toward the coastal depots. The listed cargo included medicinal spirits, perfume concentrates, dyed silk, and locked consignments of coin.

Several shippers had pooled their loads after the damage to the bridges.

Arthur read it twice.

Chris leaned over his shoulder with a piece of bread.

“Medicinal spirits.”

“Alcohol,” Pell said.

“I know. They put medicinal on it so a man can wake up in a ditch and say the treatment's working.”

Kaze held out his hand for the paper.

Arthur gave it to him.

Pell pointed down the road.

“Yard's beyond the next rise. White gateposts. Can't miss it.”

“How many guards?” Kaze asked.

Pell's expression changed slightly.

“Enough, I expect.”

“Then why hire more?”

“Because of the roads.”

Kaze glanced at Arthur.

Arthur tore off another piece of bread.

“Roads have been acting awful mean.”

Pell stood.

“I should get these there before dark.”

Kaze passed the notice back to Arthur.

“You should,” Arthur agreed, returning it to Pell.

Chris moved the duck off the stove and set it on bare ground to cool. Pell checked his wheels, climbed onto the seat, and urged the wagon forward.

It rolled freely.

He looked back once.

None of them waved him over again.

When the cart had passed the bend, Kaze turned to Arthur.

“Fuck the guard job.”

Arthur wiped his fingers on the last heel of bread.

“Wasn't polishing my references.”

Chris looked between them.

“We're robbing it?”

“I wanna see it first,” Kaze said.

Daynah picked up the cooled pot by its handles.

“Good. Because the paper didn't mention where they keep the horses, how many men sleep there, or which way we leave with anything heavy.”

“Lower road,” Arthur said. “Convoy has to use it. We don't have to meet them at the gate.”

Billy's voice emerged beside Chris's jaw.

“I do enjoy a business that gathers its valuables in one place. Considerate people. Makes you almost sorry.”

“Almost?” Chris asked.

“Well, I wouldn't want to lie over supper.”

They walked far enough to see the freight yard from the rising shoulder of the road. Arthur chose a position behind a broken length of field wall. No one climbed onto the skyline.

Below, two white gateposts framed a congested yard. Wagons stood wheel to wheel. Men shifted crates beneath hanging lamps while a foreman directed the loads with both arms. Guards watched the gate and the road, not the fields alone.

Arthur studied the visible layout through his red eye.

Kaze knelt beside him, the spear laid flat on the ground.

Chris crouched on Arthur's other side. Daynah stayed farther back with Mahnah, keeping the dog off the loose stones.

A loaded wagon tried to turn across the yard. Another blocked it. Two drivers began shouting. The foreman squeezed between them, pointed toward the lower exit, and ordered the front wagon out into a short holding lane.

Beyond that lane, the road narrowed between a drainage ditch and a low stone retaining wall.

Kaze followed it with his eyes.

“There.”

Arthur nodded.

“Can't turn a loaded wagon around in that.”

“Don't need the whole yard,” Chris said. “Need the front one to stop.”

Billy hummed thoughtfully.

They watched long enough to see the lane fill, the gate open twice, and two guards take positions beside the waiting wagons. There were more armed men inside. Their exact number remained hidden by the load stacks and buildings.

One guard walked the outside of a wagon, tapping its wheel hubs with the butt of his spear. Another checked the seals on a locked box before waving its driver forward.

Chris leaned close enough that only Arthur and Kaze needed to hear him.

“They check what's coming out.”

“They ought to,” Arthur said.

“Could get in as guards.”

Arthur looked at him.

“Your friend down there gets nervous and describes us, that's a mighty short interview.”

“He didn't seem nervous.”

Kaze looked over.

“He started packing while you were still chewing.”

Chris considered that.

“Thought he respected my time.”

Below them, a handler led two fresh horses toward the waiting wagons. The animal nearest the ditch balked at a flapping piece of canvas. The handler took hold near its cheek, brought it around, and tried again.

Arthur watched until the team stood square.

“Take a wheel off, you make yourself a job hauling what's in it. Keep the wagon whole.”

Kaze nodded without looking away.

“I can stop the front.”

“You can. I want to see what comes behind it first.”

A second armed man joined the guard near the holding lane. They spoke briefly, pointing back toward the yard. Nothing in the exchange reached the wall clearly enough to make out.

Kaze settled lower.

“Fine. We watch.”

Chris scraped dirt from his thumbnail.

“What are we doing with perfume, anyway?”

“Selling it,” Arthur said.

“To who?”

“Somebody who smells expensive.”

Kaze glanced at Chris.

“You can smell expensive and still look broke. That's you after this.”

“I'm not putting that shit on. You'll be smelling flowers for a week every time Billy turns inside out.”

Billy's quiet answer came through the headgear.

“You say that like my entrance couldn't benefit from a seasonal fragrance.”

Chris pressed his lips together and looked down at the wall until the urge to laugh passed.

Arthur waited for both of them to settle before looking back through the gap. The guards were still where they had been. No one had come searching the rise.

Arthur lowered his head behind the wall.

“We come back before dawn. Watch what actually leaves.”

Kaze's fingers closed around Daniel's spear.

“Then take the shit we want.”

Chris looked down toward the crates being loaded under lamplight.

“Billy, can you keep that stuff away from the food?”

“I am capable of arranging a room, Christopher.”

“I'm asking because I don't want my bread tasting like a woman who drowned at the mall.”

“Then don't eat the perfume.”

“Glad we had the meeting.”

They withdrew before the light left the ridge, choosing a hollow back from the road where the freight lamps could not pick out their camp. The duck was cool enough to wrap. Mahnah settled beside Daynah. Arthur checked the approach before sitting.

Kaze laid Daniel's spear within reach, parallel to his own leg.

Chris passed him the remaining bread.

He took it.

For tonight, that was enough.
