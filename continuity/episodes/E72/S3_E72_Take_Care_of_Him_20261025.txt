Season 3, Episode 72 — Take Care of Him

“There's still one behind the wagon.”

Daynah moved Arthur's aim past the overturned trunk. On the screen, a man in a dark coat came out from behind the stagecoach with his revolver pointed at a fleeing passenger.

Daynah shot him before he fired.

The passenger kept running. Arthur lowered his gun.

Chris leaned back into the couch.

“Now go get the first three.”

“They're dead.”

“Exactly. Finished all their appointments. Wide open afternoon.”

Daynah guided Arthur toward the coach. He checked the far side before turning back down the road. The last attacker lay across a wheel rut. The passenger had disappeared into the trees.

“You nearly let him get shot,” Daynah said.

“I told you there was one.”

“After telling me to leave.”

“I revised my position when more crime became available.”

Daynah made Arthur holster his weapon.

Chris pointed at a body on the near shoulder.

“That one's got a good coat.”

“Can't take his coat.”

“I know you can't take the coat. I'm assessing the man. A fella don't buy a coat like that and keep lint in every pocket.”

Daynah mounted the horse.

Chris's hand rose toward the controller, stopped, and became a pointing finger instead.

“There. Right there. You can still turn.”

The horse turned.

Chris brightened.

Then Daynah steered around a fallen branch and continued toward the road.

“That's cruel,” Chris said.

Daynah smiled without looking at him.

The split at his lip had closed. The bruised swelling beneath it had not. He sat with his back slightly clear of the cushion, the cloth at his side no longer soaking through as quickly.

At the next clear stretch, he paused and took off Kahuhu. He set both gauntlets on the floor beside his feet, within reach, then picked the controller up again. Without them, his hands looked merely large enough to strangle a washing machine.

Chris watched Arthur ride past the last corpse.

“Three wallets,” he said.

“Four.”

Chris turned slowly.

“You counted?”

“I shot them.”

“And you're leaving all four.”

“Yes.”

Chris looked at the screen as if the grass itself had become financially irresponsible.

Daynah reached the main road. Behind him, the stagecoach remained upright, its surviving passengers alive. He had done what he went back to do.

Chris couldn't dispute that. He tried taking a different route.

“When we run out of bullets, I'm telling everybody about your little charity for dead men.”

“It's a game.”

“Then why are you being so fucking polite in it?”

That was a fair question from a man whose real home contained stolen property, a prison, and Charles Manson.

Meatwad was sitting farther down the living floor, sorting the cereal from his open box into a small pile and a smaller pile.

Chris noticed.

“What're you doing?”

“These ones got broke.”

“They still taste the same.”

“Then you can have 'em.”

Chris opened his mouth, reconsidered the terms and held out his hand.

Meatwad poured the broken pieces into it.

Daynah glanced over.

“There's your loot.”

Chris ate a piece.

“At least somebody's collectin' it.”

He watched Daynah adjust the cloth at his side.

“That kid you mentioned.”

Daynah kept his eyes on the game.

“Not mine.”

“You said you got a kid.”

“It said I was its dad.”

Chris lowered his handful of cereal.

“How old?”

“A few minutes.”

“Already makin' accusations.”

Daynah steered past a wagon.

“Came off Hulk's shoulder.”

Chris waited for the rest. Daynah supplied none.

“Was Hulk there for that?”

“It was his fucking shoulder.”

“Yeah, but was he paying attention? If a baby fell off me, I'd want to see the replay before I started pointing at guests.”

Daynah's mouth pulled at its newly closed split.

“He was paying attention.”

“And he still thinks it's yours?”

“He's got it now.”

Chris considered that outcome. He finished his cereal.

“Good. We ain't got a little controller.”

Daynah laughed once, held his side and shook his head.

Chris moved the bowl on the low table closer so he could put the bloody cloth down without reaching. Then he pointed back at the game.

“Road bends.”

“I see it.”

On the second monitor, the afternoon outside was thinning toward evening. Billy's view moved past a damaged warehouse wall. A workman was waving people away from the cracked stone above its doorway.

The sound was still low from when Daynah had turned it down.

Chris raised it enough to hear Arthur.

“Other side,” the cowboy said.

Billy crossed the lane. The view swung with him, showing the damaged Subaru beyond the cleared space. Arthur walked alongside, cigar in one hand, looking up at the roof rather than waiting to be told what might fall.

Chris used the banjo's outward voice route.

“Car all right?”

“Same as you left it,” Arthur answered. “If that qualifies.”

“Don't stand it under that wall.”

“It ain't under the wall.”

“I'm just saying.”

“You generally are.”

Billy settled on a broad stone at the far edge of the ropewalk. His own banjo lay across his lap, its strings quiet beneath Chris's voice.

“We have found a spot where the architecture appears less eager to join us.”

“Thank you,” Chris said.

He closed the outward route and turned back to Daynah.

Arthur's horse was approaching Saint Denis.

They were far enough into this save that the city ahead meant something neither of them had wanted to think about. The cough had already been there. So had the tired way Arthur carried himself when Daynah let him stand still.

The traffic thickened. Wagons passed beneath the elevated brickwork. Daynah eased off the speed and kept to the street instead of forcing the horse between two carriages.

Chris brushed cereal crumbs off his palm.

Then Arthur coughed.

Chris's hand stopped.

The cough came again, harder. On the screen, the ride gave way to a man trying to stay upright in a city that continued moving around him.

“Ah,” Chris said quietly. “This bit.”

Daynah took his thumb off the stick.

They both knew what was coming.

Knowing did nothing useful.

The stranger helped Arthur toward the doctor's office. Daynah sat forward as the scene took control away from him. He wasn't trying to press through it. The controller rested across his palms.

Chris moved his foot off the low table.

In the examination room, the doctor listened. Arthur tried to make the symptoms sound like something smaller than they were.

Then the illness had a name.

Tuberculosis.

No ambush. Nobody to shoot before he finished saying it. No enemy health bar with a satisfying little red portion left to remove.

Daynah stared at the screen.

Chris looked down at Daynah's motionless hands, then back at Arthur.

Neither spoke over the rest of the examination.

Outside the office, the city sounded distant. Arthur moved through it carrying news that had changed nothing visible and everything he could expect from the time ahead.

Daynah's fingers closed around the controller again.

He did not move him yet.

Chris swallowed.

“Hate this part.”

Daynah nodded.

On the other monitor, their Arthur stood alive beside Billy. A little red light showed beneath the brim of his hat when he turned toward a sound down the lane. He was brushing ash off his cuff.

Daynah looked from one screen to the other.

Chris followed his glance.

For a moment, having him outside made the scene harder to watch.

Then Arthur reached across Billy's field of view and flicked a fallen speck off the banjo's rim.

The ordinary movement helped.

Daynah let out a slow breath.

“He was still working,” he said.

Chris nodded once.

The next marker waited on the game map. Neither of them opened it.

Meatwad gathered his remaining cereal back into the box. He looked at the two men, then at the screen, and carried the box away without offering another trade.

Daynah finally moved Arthur off the pavement and away from the traffic.

“Not doing another job yet.”

“No,” Chris said. “We got time.”

He reached for the pause button, looked at Daynah and waited.

Daynah handed him the controller.

Chris opened the save menu. His thumb hovered over an older file, then moved to a separate slot. He checked it before confirming.

Daynah watched him do it.

“Keep the other one.”

“Yeah.”

Chris passed the controller back.

Daynah made Arthur eat. He checked the horse, left the city at an easy pace and took the road into open country. Chris pointed out a quieter turn without making it an argument.

For a while, they let the ride be a ride.

Daynah pulled off the road and made a small camp. A pot stood beside the fire. Arthur settled into the light, his face different from the one Chris had started the game with.

Chris leaned forward when Daynah opened the food supplies.

“Got better than that.”

Daynah moved past the tins to the meat.

“There.”

“I know.”

“Cook it.”

Daynah did.

Chris watched the little strip darken over the fire. Nobody was shooting at them. For several seconds, the most important thing happening in the room was a man waiting for another man who didn't exist to finish his dinner.

Then Daynah selected another piece.

“Easy,” Chris said.

“You said feed him.”

“I said better. Didn't say build a second Arthur out of pork.”

Daynah stopped on the menu.

Chris glanced at the real Arthur on the other monitor, then back at the campfire.

“We've already got two.”

Daynah's mouth shifted, but he put the menu away.

The fire snapped. Arthur sat with his hands near the warmth.

Chris stayed forward, elbows on his knees. Normally, this was where he'd start finding something else they could do while they were in the area. A robbery. A stranger whose horse he liked. Somebody minding his own business suspiciously close to a cliff.

He reached for his own drink instead.

Daynah watched the fire until his shoulders came down.

“Could stay here.”

“Yeah.”

“For a bit.”

Chris put his drink back within reach and settled against the couch.

They stayed.

When Daynah finally broke camp, he brushed the horse before getting on. Chris caught himself studying the tree line for men who weren't there.

“We get trouble, ride out of it.”

Daynah looked at him.

“You telling me that?”

“I can have a sensible thought. Don't make a whole fucking event out of it.”

Daynah turned back to the screen.

“Taking care of him.”

“That's right.”

Inside Billy, no cart rattled past their knees. No stranger demanded a repair. The pressure against Daynah's ribs eased as the skin beneath his cloth continued closing. Chris leaned back, his hands resting empty in his lap.

They reached a line of trees.

“Take it easy through here,” Chris said.

“I am.”

“I know. Just—there's a rock by that bend.”

Daynah steered wide of the rock.

The horse cleared it.

Chris pointed farther right.

“Other side of—”

Daynah corrected at the same moment the horse's shoulder clipped the tree.

The horse stopped.

Arthur did not.

He left the saddle belly first, folded around the trunk and dropped behind it with both legs briefly visible above his head.

The controller remained absolutely still in Daynah's hands.

Chris leaned forward.

On the screen, Arthur lay face down in the leaves.

“Fuck,” Daynah said.

Chris put a hand over his mouth.

Neither laughed.

Then the horse wandered into view and stood on the other side of the tree, looking down at him.

Chris made a short, strangled noise through his fingers.

Daynah turned his head.

“Don't.”

“I'm not.”

Arthur's boot twitched.

Chris bent forward so abruptly that his forehead nearly struck his own knee.

Daynah's shoulders began moving.

“We were taking care of him,” Chris managed.

“You said right.”

“I said other side.”

“Tree's got two.”

“You picked the inside.”

Daynah tried to breathe through his nose. It hurt, which made him angrier, which made Chris laugh harder.

On the screen, Arthur slowly pushed himself upright.

Both men straightened.

“There,” Chris said, wiping his eyes. “He's up.”

Daynah guided him toward the horse.

“Walk.”

“Yeah.”

Arthur walked.

They watched him take several perfectly uneventful steps.

Chris's mouth started moving again.

“Fella got the worst news of his life, and we immediately mailed him to a tree.”

Daynah pressed pause before the next laugh could make him steer.

Outside, Billy heard Chris's chuckle only when Chris deliberately opened the outward route to answer him.

“Everything satisfactory in there?” Billy asked.

“We had a little riding incident.”

Arthur looked toward the banjo.

“Whose riding?”

“Don't worry about it.”

Arthur took a slow pull on his cigar.

“That bad, huh?”

Daynah waited until Chris closed the route before speaking.

“You were about to tell him.”

“He asked.”

“Billy asked.”

“Arthur became involved.”

Daynah resumed the game.

They got Arthur back onto the horse without further assistance from the tree.

By the time lamps were being lit along the real harbor, Brian and Jar Jar had left the drinking stand above the landings.

Kratos and Mimir were already gone with Brian's information. The signatures remained in his journal. He had checked them twice since the two men left, as if either might try to crawl off the page when unsupervised.

Jar Jar carried their bag along the upper lane.

Brian walked beside him with the wrapped saber secured at his side and the journal tucked against his chest.

“It's different when people at that level recognize you,” Brian said.

Jar Jar stepped around a patch of fallen roof tile.

“Him recognized you before the song.”

“Artistically.”

“Him remembered yousa.”

“You're separating things that are interconnected.”

Jar Jar stopped at the next junction. The way toward the landing office had been roped off beneath a damaged wall. A porter pointed them around by the ropewalk.

Jar Jar thanked him and took the open lane.

Brian followed, climbing over a piece of masonry that barely reached Jar Jar's ankle.

The next piece rolled beneath his foot. Jar Jar caught the back of his clothing before his face reached the road.

Brian clutched the journal with both hands. The wrapped saber knocked against his leg.

“Watch the book.”

Jar Jar set him on the other side.

“Book wasn't falling by itself.”

Brian checked its corners anyway.

Jar Jar adjusted the bag and went on. He had been told there might be a room above the office. He intended to find out while somebody was still there to answer. Brian had spent the walk explaining how their evening would be remembered by people who had already left it.

“We're going the wrong way.”

“Other way has wall falling.”

“I understand the wall. I'm talking about momentum.”

Jar Jar looked back at him.

Brian patted the journal.

“Career momentum.”

“Yousa can carry it this way.”

Ahead, Billy sat with his banjo across his lap. Arthur stood beside him, facing the lower road. Neither had noticed Brian yet.

Brian slowed.

Inside, Daynah had left the trees and was approaching a roadside camp when armed men stepped out to stop Arthur's horse.

He dismounted into cover. The first shot struck the trunk beside him. Daynah moved the aim toward the man firing from the camp's left edge.

The image caught.

For an instant, Arthur's coat hung motionless halfway through a turn. Then the screen jumped. The shooter had moved. Daynah's shot struck the space where he'd been.

Another burst came from behind a wagon.

Daynah pressed to move.

Arthur remained in the open for a fraction too long.

The game caught up all at once.

Gunfire. A stumbling step. The familiar darkening screen.

Dead.

Daynah stared at it.

“I pressed that before he shot me.”

Chris was already leaning forward.

“Saw it.”

“Whole picture stopped.”

“I saw.”

Daynah held up the controller.

“Fucking Wii U. B. Moo console. Beat's already gone and the little bastard's still getting ready.”

Chris barked a laugh, then opened his outward voice route.

“Billy. Daynah just called this a B. Moo console. Says the beat's gone and the little bastard's still gettin' ready.”

The words came out of the banjo just as Brian reached the edge of the ropewalk.

Brian stopped.

His face changed before anyone addressed him.

“That's not what offbeat means.”

Arthur turned.

Billy looked down.

Inside, Chris turned up the exterior monitor.

Brian stood in the lamp glow with his journal pressed beneath one arm.

Chris looked at Daynah.

Daynah looked at the ceiling.

“Of course.”

Billy's smile spread slowly.

“Well. Look what the road has provided.”

Brian pointed at the banjo.

“Chris, tell him there's a difference between being behind the beat and not understanding the beat.”

Chris answered through it.

“We didn't order you, Brian.”

“I was walking past.”

“That's how deliveries work.”

Jar Jar came alongside Brian and looked up at Billy, then at the instrument.

“Somebody in there?”

“Christopher,” Billy said. “Has been for some time. Difficult habit to break.”

Jar Jar leaned toward the strings.

“Hello?”

“Hey,” Chris said through the banjo.

Jar Jar straightened with visible satisfaction. He had established which object to address. Brian was still fighting a review nobody had offered him directly.

“I'm saying it can be intentional.”

Arthur studied him.

“Can be.”

“Mine is.”

“That's convenient.”

Brian opened the journal.

“Starkiller liked my wordplay.”

Chris glanced at Daynah.

“He said anything about the parts between the words?”

Daynah rested the controller on his thigh.

“Tell him fix those.”

Chris relayed it.

Brian raised his chin.

“Those are pauses.”

“We got those,” Chris answered. “That's what killed Arthur.”

The real Arthur slowly turned his head toward the banjo.

“Excuse me?”

“Game Arthur.”

“You could lead with that.”

“You were standing right there. I figured you'd eliminate yourself.”

Arthur looked at Brian again.

“I'm beginning to see the appeal.”

Brian put one hand on his hip.

“A delayed response is not a musical comparison.”

Billy plucked a single low note.

Brian stopped talking.

Billy waited, watching him.

Then he plucked another.

“There,” Brian said. “That's space.”

“I was waitin' for you to finish.”

“You made my point.”

“I fear you've brought enough for everyone.”

Jar Jar shifted the bag to his other shoulder.

“Mesa asking about the office.”

Brian held up a finger without looking at him.

Jar Jar walked around the finger and addressed Arthur.

“Landing office. Open still?”

Arthur pointed down the lane.

“Light's on. Door's open. Reckon that's your best chance.”

“Thank you.”

Brian snapped the journal shut.

“I'm coming.”

Then Chris said, “Hang on. Do it on time once.”

Brian turned back immediately.

Jar Jar exhaled through his nose.

“Mesa go ask.”

He left them to it.

Brian watched him walk toward the lighted doorway, then glanced back at Billy.

“This'll take a second.”

Arthur stepped out of the way as two workers passed carrying a bent shutter. One end scraped the road. He caught it before it hooked Brian behind the knees, lifted it clear and let the men keep going.

Brian hadn't seen it coming. His journal was already open again.

“You're welcome,” Arthur said.

“For what?”

Arthur looked at the departing shutter.

“Keep talking. Might come back.”

Billy watched the workers carry on, then rested one large hand across his strings.

“What, precisely, are we testin'?”

“Nothing,” Brian said. “I'm not auditioning.”

He moved closer to the banjo.

Billy raised an eyebrow.

Brian adjusted his stance.

“Just keep it consistent.”

Billy began a simple, steady pattern.

Brian counted beneath it, then tapped his little palm against the journal on the next beat.

He kept time.

No stumble. No accidental new rhythm. The hand landed where it was supposed to land, and he gave himself enough room to do it again.

Inside, Chris listened.

Daynah nodded once.

Brian stopped after several clean bars.

“There.”

Billy let the last note ring.

“There indeed.”

Brian looked from Arthur to the banjo.

“So.”

Chris answered.

“That was on time.”

Brian's shoulders rose.

“Thank you.”

“Keep that part.”

“That's not a part. That's the underlying—”

He stopped himself.

For once, an exit stood directly in front of him and he appeared capable of recognizing it.

Then he opened the journal again.

“You know, Kratos and Mimir also—”

“You had it,” Chris said.

“Had what?”

“You were walking away with a compliment. Clean. No witnesses injured. Then you turned around to put a little shed on it.”

Daynah covered his mouth.

Brian stared at the banjo.

“I don't need to walk away.”

“Evidently.”

Arthur crouched enough to see the page Brian was presenting.

The signatures were real. The upper part of the paper remained folded back.

Arthur did not touch it.

“They sign everybody's?”

“No.”

“Then you've got something.”

Brian looked at him, briefly without an answer ready.

Arthur straightened.

“Try not to talk it out of being something.”

Brian lowered the book.

Inside, Chris closed the outward voice route and turned away from the exterior monitor as Daynah selected the game's restart.

The camp returned. Arthur stood alive again near the approach.

Daynah made him take cover before the shooting started. He waited through a small hitch in the picture, then moved when it cleared.

The first attacker fell.

Chris watched the left edge of the screen.

“One moving round.”

“Got him.”

Daynah shifted. The second man ran into the shot. The third retreated behind the wagon and fired through its spokes.

Daynah circled away from the gap, waited for the reload and stepped out from a different angle.

The man dropped.

This time Arthur stayed alive.

Chris pointed at a body.

Daynah pointed at him without looking away from the screen.

Chris lowered his hand.

Outside, Brian had heard the banjo fall quiet. He looked up at Billy.

“He still there?”

“Very much so.”

“Tell him I understand latency. I've worked with sound.”

Billy looked at the instrument.

“Christopher?”

Chris reopened the route.

“Yeah?”

Brian continued before Billy could repeat him.

“If the whole picture froze, it isn't just him pressing late. Did the animation stop too?”

Chris glanced at Daynah.

“Yeah. Whole thing.”

“Then that's the machine. You can't perform around something that changes the delay.”

Chris nodded.

“That's what he said.”

Brian waited.

“And?”

“And he was right.”

Brian's expression tightened.

“You could've just said that at the beginning.”

“We did. You came in arguing about music.”

Billy's fingers settled gently across the strings.

Brian looked down the lane after Jar Jar.

For a moment, he seemed ready to explain why that wasn't the same thing.

Then he tucked the journal safely beneath his arm.

“I have somewhere to be.”

“Go be there,” Chris said.

Brian walked away.

He got three paces before turning.

“That wasn't an audition.”

Arthur tipped his hat.

“Then you got paid about right.”

Brian kept walking this time.

Inside, Daynah guided Arthur back onto the road. Chris closed the outward voice route and sat beside him.

The game still had its sickness, its debts and its waiting missions. The next marker remained where it had been.

Daynah stopped the horse beside a stretch of open grass.

Chris looked at him.

“What're we doing?”

“Nothing a minute.”

Chris leaned back.

Arthur's horse lowered its head.

They let it.
