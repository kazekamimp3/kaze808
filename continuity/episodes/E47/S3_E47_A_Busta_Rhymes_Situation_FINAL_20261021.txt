Season 3, Episode 47: “A Busta Rhymes Situation”

“The tide is going out,” Dutch said.

Brian looked toward the water.

“Busta Rhymes could make a tide chart sound fucking dangerous.”

Dr. Evil turned slowly.

“Would he improve the tide?”

“I'm talking about delivery.”

“So was Dutch. Until you introduced a recording artist to the ocean.”

They stood on the quay beside the submarine the morning after Brian had submitted his shortened demands for recognition. The five silver from the poultry run had survived the discussion. The founding dispute had survived it too.

Dr. Evil had come outside to inspect the berth and discover why a vessel this impressive did not make everyone nearby look sufficiently impressed.

Brian had come because nobody had told him not to.

One of the crewmen called up a question about moving a rope.

Dutch answered him.

Brian watched the man lean into the work.

“That's what I'm saying. Presence. Some people can just stand somewhere and the whole fucking environment starts anticipating them. Busta walks into a room, you don't need a sign saying the room's different. You feel the air change.”

Dr. Evil looked at the crewman.

“Was that the rope, or did you feel the air change?”

The man wisely continued working.

“You don't get it,” Brian said.

“I'm receiving it in considerable detail.”

Brian stepped onto a low mooring block so he could see past the stacked rope.

“I'm describing command.”

Dr. Evil regarded him from above.

“Then perhaps describe mine.”

Brian looked at him.

There was a tempting opening. A normal person might have stepped through it, complimented the man providing the accommodation, and enjoyed a quiet morning.

Brian said, “It's a different kind.”

Dutch looked out to sea.

“How different?” the doctor asked.

“You're more controlled.”

That helped.

“Calculated.”

That helped more.

“Busta has that too, but then there's the explosive factor. Like a building that knows when to become a fucking drum.”

Dr. Evil's mouth closed.

“I see. I am the building before the interesting thing happens.”

“That's not what I said.”

“No. You used more words.”

He turned to a crewman near the gangway.

“Have the horn sounded. Once.”

The man went aboard. A short interval passed while he reached the control.

Then the submarine's horn rolled across the harbor.

Ropes trembled against metal. Men farther along the quay looked up. Dr. Evil stood still, allowing the sound to supply the weight beneath his next sentence.

“That,” he said, “is presence.”

Brian's face lit.

“Busta would absolutely murder that sample.”

The doctor looked toward the gangway.

“We're finished with the horn.”

It hadn't been scheduled to sound again.

He still wanted someone to know why.

Upriver, the previous morning, Arthur had unfolded Maul's torn survey map beside the stream.

K.K.'s walking stick lay across the fresh earth near the pear tree. Chris passed it carrying his rolled gear and looked toward the water instead.

Arthur put a finger on the works. Then on the road connecting them to the lower valley.

“This gets us past the broken section. I know the way to the port from there.”

Kaze stood over the map.

“And that's where the doctor is.”

“Last I heard.”

Chris looked at the wrapped hilt beside Arthur's hand.

“Clifford gets a laser sword. Next thing a different guy turns up with two.”

“One handle,” Billy said through the banjo.

“Thank you. We can all sleep now.”

Arthur kept his finger on the road.

“Having one doesn't mean that doctor knows everybody carrying one.”

Kaze looked up.

“Then he'll have an easy answer.”

He wasn't claiming he'd solved anything. He wanted to look at the man who gave Clifford a weapon like that, hear what he knew, and decide how much he enjoyed the answer.

Maul had come looking for him. K.K. had been the person standing in the way.

That particular fact had survived the night.

Daynuh crouched beside Mahnuh and checked one paw. The dog pulled it back, then permitted the indignity when Daynuh took it again.

“Long road?” Daynuh asked.

“We'll stop when we need to,” Arthur said.

“Good. Daynuh ain't racing a submarine.”

“It's tied up.”

“Then Daynuh's winning.”

Chris bent over the map.

Billy asked him to turn slightly so the headlamp could give him the whole chart.

“That's a local survey,” the fish said. “Don't let its neat little lines persuade you the rest of the country has agreed to them.”

Arthur folded it along the existing creases.

“I won't.”

He secured the wrapped saber where the switch stayed protected. The weapon had no new title, no oath attached to it. It was a dangerous thing he'd picked up beside a dead man, and he meant to keep it from becoming somebody else's accident.

They left the pear tree standing.

By late the following morning they had reached the coast, following surviving road around the damaged ground. They came into the harbor on foot, dusty and hungry, while Dr. Evil was still considering whether a second horn blast would look needy.

One of his crew recognized Arthur from the visit to the camp.

The man spoke to Dutch before anybody began treating the arrivals as customers.

Dutch turned.

“Arthur.”

Arthur lifted a hand.

“Dutch.”

Dr. Evil studied the group approaching along the quay. The enormous dog. The man carrying a banjo. The tattooed, bare-chested man who looked at the submarine without offering it the smallest facial reward.

Dutch glanced toward him.

“Kaze.”

“Yes,” the doctor said. “I had begun to suspect.”

The name across Kaze's stomach saved everyone a round of introductions.

Brian hopped down from the block.

Chris saw him.

“Shit. Somebody left the founder outside.”

Brian looked at the banjo, then at Chris.

“I can go outside.”

“We've been finding that out.”

Dr. Evil stepped forward before the conversation could settle permanently around Brian's ankles.

“Welcome. I understand you've already met several of my associates.”

Kaze looked at Clifford, who had come out through the gangway doorway and was moving toward the open space on the quay in his chair.

The gifted hilt remained fastened to its frame.

“Hard to miss some of them.”

Clifford stopped beside Dutch.

His mask stayed on.

He looked at Chris for a long second. Then turned toward Arthur.

“How's the camp?”

Arthur's expression changed.

“We lost somebody.”

Dutch moved closer.

“Who?”

“K.K. The tree.”

The two crewmen who had visited the camp went quiet. They knew who that meant. Dr. Evil had only their descriptions.

Brian opened his mouth.

Nothing came out before Kaze spoke.

“Guy with horns. Red face. Two ends on one of those.”

He nodded toward Clifford's weapon.

Arthur drew the wrapped hilt from his gear, keeping the control covered.

“Called himself Maul.”

Dr. Evil looked at it without reaching.

“And you believe I know him.”

“I'm asking,” Kaze said.

“I don't.”

Kaze watched his face.

The doctor let the answer stand.

Arthur put the wrapped weapon on a clear section of an empty crate between them.

“He was asking about the broken ground. Wanted Kaze.”

Dutch looked from Arthur to the dark stains still worked into the seams of Kaze's pants.

“Where is he now?”

“Upper road,” Kaze said.

“Is he coming here?”

“Not together.”

A gull screamed over the berth.

Dr. Evil looked at Kaze again.

That answer had given him rather more information than the first one.

Brian stepped beside the crate.

“The important distinction is that matching presentation doesn't establish common manufacture. It's like when people hear an aggressive vocal and immediately say Busta Rhymes. No. There's breath placement. There's the architecture of the whole fucking delivery. You can't just hear a man shout and call it Busta.”

Chris turned toward him.

“We brought a murder weapon, Brian.”

“I'm explaining attribution.”

“You found a way to make a dead tree about your fucking playlist.”

“He made a relevant distinction,” Dr. Evil said.

Brian looked up.

The doctor continued, “Shortly before losing custody of it.”

Kaze's mouth moved at one corner.

Arthur drew back the cloth enough to expose the hilt, leaving his thumb beside the protected control.

Dr. Evil examined its length and construction. Then looked at the weapon attached to Clifford's chair.

“That one came with me. This one did not.”

“You make yours?” Kaze asked.

“I employ people.”

“Wasn't what I asked.”

“No. I did not personally manufacture it.”

“You got a way back to where it came from?”

Dr. Evil paused.

The question mattered enough that Dutch stopped watching Brian.

“Not at present,” the doctor said.

Kaze looked past him at the submarine.

“That's a lot of boat to be lost in.”

“It has been a considerable inconvenience.”

He said it with the irritation of a man whose hotel had misplaced the continent.

Chris rubbed his mouth.

From the banjo, Billy said, “A vessel can be excellent at the wrong sort of departure.”

The doctor looked at it.

Chris tapped the banjo.

“That's Billy.”

“I see.”

“Not from there, you don't,” Billy said.

Brian was already nodding.

“That's projection. Busta doesn't need to stand right in front of you to dominate the entire space.”

Dr. Evil turned toward him.

“Does this man handle all your examples?”

“If the example fits.”

“We were discussing transportation.”

“Movement. Momentum. It's connected.”

Chris stared down at him.

“You'd find Busta Rhymes in a fucking stool sample.”

Brian pointed at him.

“If somebody had eaten a CD, yeah, there'd be a reason to investigate.”

For a moment nobody answered.

I need you to appreciate that he thought he'd defended himself. He'd climbed into that sentence, pulled the lid shut, and was waiting for us to compliment the hinges.

Kaze laughed.

Dr. Evil waited for him to stop.

He didn't stop quickly enough for the doctor's liking.

“Brian is developing our public presentation,” Dr. Evil said.

“Apparently through the colon,” Billy replied.

Brian looked at Chris.

“Do you have to keep doing that?”

Chris lifted both hands.

“That one wasn't me.”

Dutch drew the attention back to the crate.

“If you don't know the man, perhaps one of your people can tell whether the weapons have anything in common.”

Dr. Evil considered it.

“A comparison. Certainly. That is a much more reasonable request than arriving with a corpse's property and asking whether I misplaced the rest of him.”

Kaze looked at Arthur.

Arthur's hand stayed beside the hilt.

“They can look here.”

“Our tools are aboard,” the doctor said.

“Then bring what you need.”

Dr. Evil looked at the crate, the open quay, and the group that had declined to become a tour.

“This is not my usual consultation arrangement.”

“Mine either,” Arthur said.

Dutch stepped in with the smoothness of a man laying a rug over a hole.

“No one needs to move anything sensitive. Have someone familiar with Clifford's weapon make an initial comparison. Then we decide whether there's anything worth pursuing.”

Dr. Evil called to a crewman. Asked him to bring the crewman who'd explained the gift's controls to Clifford.

The man went aboard.

The pause became Brian's almost immediately.

He moved toward the open end of the crate and straightened his little jacket.

Dr. Evil noticed.

“What are you doing?”

“Helping with presentation.”

“I have already presented myself.”

“You said welcome. That's a door mat. It doesn't communicate scale.”

Kaze's eyes moved to him.

Brian mistook attention for an invitation.

“When you're introducing somebody at this level, you've got to understand the room before the room understands what's happening. Give them a reason to anticipate. You don't walk out and say here's a guy.”

Dr. Evil looked toward the group.

“You see the difficulty.”

Brian held up a hand.

“No. Let me finish. You're actually proving my point by resisting the buildup.”

“I shall try to resist more efficiently.”

Brian turned toward Kaze.

“What you're looking at here is a man with infrastructure. People. Reach. Most people say they have an organization, they have a fucking room and somebody owes them twenty dollars. This is an operation.”

Dutch glanced at him.

That part hadn't been bad.

“You don't judge it by whether the man himself is making every sound,” Brian continued. “Busta doesn't personally build the fucking speakers. That's what I'm trying to get across. You put him at the center and everything around him becomes part of the performance.”

Dr. Evil's expression became very carefully blank.

“Am I at the center yet?”

“I'm establishing the analogy.”

“I seem to be behind Mr. Rhymes.”

“You're missing the point.”

Clifford spoke from behind his mask.

“He got introduced first.”

Brian pointed at him.

“No, he didn't. I used him as an example.”

“He got the speakers,” Chris said.

“It's a metaphor.”

Kaze folded his arms.

“Does the metaphor have a submarine?”

Brian hesitated.

Dr. Evil looked at him.

For once, he and the shirtless visitor wanted the same answer.

“Not that I'm aware of,” Brian said.

The doctor spread his hands.

“Finally. A distinction in my favor.”

“You can't reduce the entire comparison to who owns a submarine.”

“I can from this one.”

Brian's mouth stayed open.

Kaze looked at Dr. Evil.

Then laughed through his nose.

The doctor saw it and relaxed by a fraction.

Brian saw that too.

He'd spent a minute trying to provide something impressive. Dr. Evil had picked through it, found the part he wanted, and taken it without asking Brian whether the rhythm was right.

It was infuriating.

It was also, against every useful intention Brian had brought to the morning, something he wanted to get better at.

He stepped back from the crate.

“You know what gets overlooked? Control. Everybody hears speed and thinks that's the whole thing. It isn't. Busta could make silence sound like it's about to punch your landlord.”

Dr. Evil's eyes moved toward him.

“Has he considered using some?”

“Who?”

“You, on his behalf.”

Brian crossed his arms.

“You don't have to like hip-hop.”

“I would like to finish meeting the people on the dock.”

“I was adding context.”

“You have added an entire other man.”

Daynuh put a hand against Mahnuh's chest before the dog could investigate the opening beside the gangway.

“Mahnuh stays here.”

Brian watched the dog settle.

“See, that's loyalty. Busta has that energy. You know if he's in your corner—”

“Does he know?” Kaze asked.

Brian stopped.

“Know what?”

“That you're in his corner. With a fucking shovel. Digging tunnels between every conversation on Earth.”

Chris bent over, laughing into his hand.

Brian looked toward Dutch for something useful.

Dutch was looking at the water.

“I'm a fan,” Brian said.

“Nigga, a fan moves air. You've built that man a church in your throat.”

Dr. Evil's head turned toward Kaze.

He considered the phrasing.

Then looked down at Brian.

“We shall not be funding the church.”

“Nobody asked you to.”

“Good. A productive agreement.”

The crewman returned with the man who knew Clifford's gift. He carried a small tool roll and asked Arthur to keep the weapon switched off.

Arthur folded back the cloth himself.

The man examined what he could see. Asked Clifford for permission to compare the exterior of the other hilt. Clifford brought his chair closer and held the gift where both men could reach it.

The crewman worked without opening either weapon.

Brian leaned toward Chris.

“Busta would've appreciated the precision.”

Chris looked at him.

“You don't know what he's doing.”

“I appreciate precision in general.”

“Then say general.”

The crewman straightened.

“They aren't the same design. I can't tell you who made that one from looking at the outside.”

Dr. Evil nodded.

“There. An answer from a man qualified to be uncertain.”

Kaze studied the crewman.

“Could it take you home?”

The man looked at the hilt.

“I have no reason to think so.”

“Didn't ask what you hoped.”

“Neither did I,” Dr. Evil said.

Kaze looked back at him.

For the first time that morning, the doctor wasn't performing for Brian.

“We arrived here,” he said. “We have a functioning vessel. We are learning where it can go. Those are the facts. I won't invent another because your visitor carried a similar weapon.”

Kaze held his gaze.

Then nodded once.

Arthur rewrapped the saber.

No miraculous address fell out. No little light blinked the answer into a room. They had carried a question to somebody with a reason to know, and his answer had remained no.

That still changed where they would look next.

Dutch touched the edge of the survey map visible in Arthur's open gear.

“He had that?”

“Took it from a driver,” Arthur said. “Upper works. Marked roads.”

“Then the driver may know which direction he came from.”

Kaze looked down at the folded chart.

It was an ordinary suggestion. Good enough that he couldn't enjoy dismissing it.

“Yeah,” he said.

Dutch withdrew his hand.

“I can listen here. If anyone else arrives asking for the man who broke the ground, I'd prefer to know before he starts moving it again.”

“Don't promise him where we are,” Arthur said.

Dutch looked at him.

Then nodded.

“I'll bring the question to you.”

Brian took a breath.

Dr. Evil raised one finger without looking.

Brian closed his mouth.

The doctor's hand remained up.

Kaze saw the exchange.

So did Chris.

Brian saw both of them seeing it and moved his jaw as though the silence had been entirely his own idea.

The crewman packed his tools. Clifford secured his hilt again. Arthur put the other away, retaining it and the map.

Dr. Evil considered the gathering.

“Well. Now that nobody is accusing me of issuing the horned man, perhaps we can introduce ourselves properly.”

“You first,” Kaze said.

The doctor straightened.

“Dr. Evil.”

Kaze waited.

“That's it?”

“It has generally been sufficient.”

“Sufficient for what?” Chris asked. “Getting out of parent-teacher conferences?”

The banjo gave a low, satisfied chuckle.

Brian looked up at his employer.

He knew that expression now. Knew the careful pause before the correction. He was waiting for it with a small, unpleasant excitement.

Dr. Evil looked at Chris.

“I understand you protected my meal.”

“Shut a gate.”

“Yes. Brian supplied a more ambitious description. I thought we'd try yours.”

Chris's smile widened.

Brian's went away.

There. Everybody had received something.

Dutch offered to show Arthur where they could sit out of the traffic and talk. Arthur accepted. Daynuh took Mahnuh toward a patch of shade along the quay. Chris went with him, keeping Billy's view turned toward the group until the stacked rope blocked it.

Arthur and Dutch stopped beside a stack of unused mooring timbers farther along the berth. Dutch sat on one. Arthur stayed on his feet until the wrapped hilt was settled securely beside his gear.

“I was glad to get your letter,” Dutch said.

“Figured.”

“You could have made it less difficult to appreciate.”

Arthur looked at him.

Dutch smiled.

For a moment it almost fit a place neither of them was standing in.

Then Dutch looked back toward the others.

“How's Chris taking it?”

“He buried what we could bring back.”

Dutch nodded slowly.

“And Kaze?”

Arthur followed his gaze.

“You've seen the answer he got.”

“I meant afterward.”

Arthur rested a hand on the timber.

“He came here.”

Dutch looked at him again.

That was an answer too.

Somebody began hammering at a berth farther down the waterfront. The sound traveled over the water and came back thin beneath the hull.

“I haven't heard anything that would help with Daniel,” Dutch said. “A great many stories. None that put the man you want where someone actually saw him.”

“Don't send me a story because it sounds good.”

“I won't.”

Arthur studied his face.

Dutch held the look without adding a speech.

“And if somebody asks where we're staying,” Arthur said, “that includes your doctor.”

“You think I'd sell him your location?”

“I think you like being useful to the man with the boat.”

Dutch's jaw tightened.

“I'm useful to a number of people.”

“I'm asking you to choose how.”

The hammering continued.

Dutch looked down at his hands, then toward the gangway.

“I'll ask before I pass it along.”

Arthur nodded.

They hadn't repaired the world. They had reached one small agreement that would now be somebody's choice to keep.

From beyond the rope stack came Brian's voice explaining that breath control was also a form of leadership.

Dutch shut his eyes.

Arthur looked toward the sound.

“He always do that now?”

“He found a subject nobody here could correct.”

“Sounds like somebody should've let him finish a conversation once.”

Dutch opened his eyes.

“Arthur, we are trying to leave the harbor standing.”

Kaze stayed a moment, looking at the submarine.

Dr. Evil stayed beside him.

“Impressive,” the doctor said.

Kaze glanced at him.

“You asking me or telling me?”

The doctor considered the distinction.

Kaze nodded toward Clifford farther down the quay.

“You give everybody one of those?”

“It was a gift.”

“Yeah. I saw the part where he kept it.”

Dr. Evil's eyes moved toward the secured hilt.

“A particular expression of appreciation.”

“Appreciate me.”

The doctor looked back at him.

Kaze smiled.

“I'm standing right here. Put some feeling into it.”

“You arrived with a weapon of your own.”

“Arthur's carrying that.”

“Then I suggest you discuss your distribution arrangements with Arthur.”

Kaze laughed softly.

He hadn't reached for anything. He'd wanted to see how quickly the generosity found a fence.

Dr. Evil straightened his cuffs.

“Clifford's gift remains Clifford's. This is not an open weapons counter.”

“Got it.”

Brian, who should have left with the others, chose that instant to help.

“It's like Busta. You don't have to explain why it's impressive.”

Dr. Evil closed his eyes.

Kaze started laughing again.

Later, when the arrivals had moved down the quay, Dr. Evil returned to the gangway with Brian at his heels.

“You will keep your enthusiasm,” he said.

Brian looked up, suspicious.

“But occasionally direct it toward the people actually providing your accommodation.”

“I can admire more than one person.”

The doctor stopped.

Brian stopped too.

For half a second neither of them improved the sentence.

Then Brian added, “Professionally.”

Dr. Evil looked down at him.

“Your next professional contribution will be a concise account of the meeting.”

Brian took out his notebook.

“Fine.”

“No lyrics.”

“I wasn't going to—”

“No guest appearances.”

Brian glared.

The doctor went aboard.

Brian stayed on the quay and wrote the heading.

Busta Rhymes Situation.

He looked at it.

Crossed it out.

Underneath he wrote: Initial Contact.

Then, smaller, where the rest of the page wouldn't have to know, he added: excellent presence.
