EPISODE 81: HIGH FRUCTOSE HOMICIDE

(Main Story — Narrator: Wade / Deadpool)

Now, you would think that after you jump a corporate superhero, beat him with
his own cape, and kick him out the garbage chute in his unmentionables, the
universe would grant you at least twenty minutes of peace.

Maybe a nice cup of tea. Maybe a quick nap on the sofa.

Not in this story. In this story, the moment you close one door, some absurd,
red-tinted nightmare kicks the entire wall off the hinges.

Chris was back in the galley, wiping down the counter with a greasy rag,
muttering under his breath with that low, scratchy backwoods cadence.

“I’m telling you, Dana, that man was unnatural. A grown man in red boots
flying through space with no helmet on. That’s that demonic stuff, dude.
That’s like when a fella down in Terrebonne Parish claims he saw an angel
behind an Arby’s dumpster and then his teeth turn black. It ain’t right.
It ain’t wholesome.”

Dana leaned against the doorframe, chewing on a dry cracker. “At least he’s
gone, Chris. Focus on the positive. The hull is sealed, and we’re still
moving.”

“He ruined my milk, Dana,” Chris said, his jaw tightening until the tendons in
his neck showed. “The dust from the ceiling fell right into the bowl. Three
perfectly good Eyeholes, ruined by ceiling drywall. I am one minor
inconvenience away from chewing through the primary wire harness.”

Down the hall, Kaze was sitting on the floor by the weapons locker, dumping a
handful of greasy Ooblits into Homelander’s gold belt buckle to see if it would
hold them like a bowl.

“Dana, look at this. It’s like an ash tray for rich people. We need to find an
orbital gas station. I’m trying to get some of those hot dogs that roll on the
little metal rollers for three days.”

Before Dana could reply, the Falcon’s internal pressure sensors began to wail.

It wasn't a normal malfunction alarm. The ship’s main computer didn't even
have a registry for what was happening. The bulkheads in the lower cargo hold
were groaning, metal shrieking as if something massive, blunt, and impossible
was pushing against the outer hull from the void.

From the lounge booth, Master Oogway lifted his head from his wooden bowl,
sniffing the dry, recirculated air.

“A sweet scent,” the old tortoise murmured, his tongue darting out to taste
the air. “Like cherries… left too long in the sun… mixed with pure, distilled
diabetic rage.”

Daniel stepped into the corridor, dropping into a low, defensive stance, his
senses extending through the metal deck. “Something is breaching the lower
bay. It has no heartbeat. It is vast… and liquid.”

A sudden, violent tearing of metal echoed through the entire lower deck.

The cargo bay doors didn't slide open. The reinforced blast walls of the hold
were violently shattered outward, thick chunks of durasteel folding like cheap
foil.

Standing in the smoking breach, twelve feet tall, completely ignoring the
vacuum of space, was a colossal pitcher of solid, transparent glass.

Inside the glass sloshed hundreds of gallons of thick, dark-red cherry syrup,
foaming with white sugar head at the top. Two round, hollow eyes were etched
into the front of the pitcher, along with a wide, horrifying, static grin.

Two massive arms made of condensation-slicked glass hung at its sides.

The entity stepped into the corridor, its sheer weight denting the floor
plates, red liquid splashing violently over its rim and sizzling against the
electrical conduits.

Its voice didn't come from a throat. It boomed through the metal itself, deep,
resonant, and horrifyingly cheerful.

“Oh yeah.”

Chris stopped wiping the counter. He slowly turned his head toward the
corridor, his eyes locking onto the red giant.

A wave of sticky, sweet cherry fluid began creeping down the hallway, pooling
around the crew’s ankles, shorting out the floor lighting in a shower of
sparks.

“What is that,” Chris whispered, his voice cracking into a thin, breathless
line. “What the fuck is that.”

“It’s juice, bro,” Kaze said, stepping out with Boba Fett’s dented blaster
held like a club. “Why the hell is a giant glass of Kool-Aid in our hallway?
This ship has zero security.”

“The liquid is high in acidity,” Daniel warned, his voice steady as he backed
up a step. “The sugar content is corroding the life-support wiring. If it
floods the core, the thrusters will shut down.”

The Kool-Aid Man took another heavy, lumbering step forward, raising one
massive glass fist to crush the galley bulkhead.

“Oh yeah,” it boomed again, completely indifferent to their existence.

“Not on my ship,” Chris whispered.

There was no negotiation. There was no hesitation.

The Sins gang swarmed the beverage titan simultaneously.

Chris moved first, driven by pure, manic sugar-rage. He ripped a heavy foam
fire extinguisher off the wall bracket, lunged forward, and swung the steel
cylinder with everything he had, slamming it straight into the Kool-Aid Man’s
glass kneecap.

A spiderweb of white fractures shot through the thick glass, sticky red syrup
jetting out from the crack like arterial blood.

Dana followed immediately, carrying a four-foot steel pry-bar. He jammed the
flat edge directly into the fracture Chris had made, leaning his entire body
weight into the lever to pry the crack open.

Daniel launched himself off the bulkhead, soaring through the air to land
directly on the entity’s glass rim. Balancing precariously over the swirling
red vortex inside, Daniel drove his palm downward, using a focused kinetic
pulse to disrupt the internal surface tension of the liquid, sending waves of
syrup splashing out of the pitcher and onto the ceiling.

The entity roared, its massive glass arms swinging around to swat Daniel off
its head.

Kaze stepped right into the giant’s path.

He didn't try to dodge. He grabbed the emergency hull-breach foam cannon from
the wall locker, aimed the wide nozzle directly into the Kool-Aid Man’s open
smiling mouth, and held down the trigger.

Expanding industrial sealant pumped directly into the red liquid at four
hundred gallons a minute.

The cherry juice instantly reacted with the chemical foam, turning from a
fluid into a solid, rubbery, purple block of coagulated gel inside the
pitcher.

The Kool-Aid Man froze. The liquid inside couldn't slosh anymore. The entity
tried to speak, but only a muffled, choked sound came out through the
hardening rubber foam.

“Oh… no…”

“Yeah, bitch, oh no is right,” Kaze said.

Chris didn't wait for it to recover. He raised the fire extinguisher high
above his head and brought the base down across the center of the entity’s
hollow glass face.

The heavy glass pitcher shattered into ten thousand blunt fragments.

A tidal wave of congealed, rubbery red sludge collapsed across the corridor
deck, burying the crew up to their knees in sticky, cherry-scented gelatin.

The Falcon went quiet again, save for the dull hum of the air filters
struggling to process the overwhelming smell of artificial fruit punch.

Master Oogway slowly paddled his way down the sticky hallway, dipping two
ancient claws into the red gel on the floor. He brought them to his mouth,
tasting it with a thoughtful, slow smack of his lips.

“Too much sugar,” the tortoise muttered, shaking his head. “Needs more lime.
And a splash of gin.”

Kaze stood in the puddle of syrup, looking down at his slides, which were now
completely coated in sticky red goo.

“Look at my shoes, Chris. Look at my fucking shoes. They’re stuck to the
floor.”

Chris stood frozen in the middle of the mess, holding his dented fire
extinguisher, covered from head to toe in cherry flavoring, his face blank
and lifeless.

High above the wreckage, the holo-comm flickered on once more, displaying
another priority alert across the stars:

CRIME REPORT — SECTOR NINE:
INCIDENT: Assault on Sovereign Corporate Asset (Homelander), Unlawful
Liquidation of Ancient Beverage Deity.
SUSPECT VEHICLE: Corellian YT-1300.
CURRENT BOUNTY ON HAN SOLO: 4.8 Billion Brapples.

Kaze glanced at the screen, sighed, and reached down to pull his slide free
from the congealed syrup with a wet, sticky peel.

“Four point eight billion,” Kaze muttered, shaking his head. “Han Solo is
never seeing the light of day again, bro. That man is doing life in the
shadow realm.”
