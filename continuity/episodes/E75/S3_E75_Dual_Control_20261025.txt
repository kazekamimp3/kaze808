Episode 75: Dual Control

Chris had made it to the top of the freight road when Billy asked him to turn around.

“Why?”

“I wish to see the view.”

Chris looked back toward the harbor. Beyond the damaged roofs, sunlight lay in a broad white stripe across the water.

Inside, Billy watched the exterior feed with his hat in his lap.

His swollen eye narrowed against the brightness spilling across the screen.

“Very pleasant. Proceed.”

Chris stood still.

“You just used my whole fucking head like a mouse.”

“The accommodation lacks a window.”

“You have a window.”

“It keeps looking at your feet.”

Chris raised his eyes to the empty stone terrace beside the road. Freight had worn pale tracks across it; grass grew in the joints beyond the wheel marks. There was room for the Falcon and room around it. Nobody would have to move a building first.

He walked onto the terrace.

“Here.”

Billy came outside through the shared body's inversion, bringing his banjo with him. He planted his feet, opened his jaws and returned the Millennium Falcon to daylight. Its landing gear met stone with three heavy knocks.

Kaze, Arthur and Artoo came out separately afterward. The droid rolled beneath the maintenance hatch. Kaze walked straight up the ramp.

Arthur watched him go.

“You fixing it?”

“Protecting my seat.”

“From who?”

“Chris.”

Through the banjo, Chris said, “I'm fixing the ship.”

“Exactly. You gonna come in here feeling entitled.”

This was a delicate dispute between the man who had stolen the vehicle and the man expected to make it work. Neither had invited the owner to mediate. His friend had rather a lot on his plate, spinally speaking.

Artoo extended a tool into the exposed cooling connection. He loosened the scorched fitting, withdrew it and turned his dome toward Billy.

Billy looked down.

The droid held the part up again.

“Christopher. Your little gentleman has produced a tooth.”

“Let me out.”

Billy yielded. Chris crouched beside Artoo and took the fitting.

The damaged sealing face was visible once the soot came off. Behind it, the valve still moved. Artoo's probe lit the adjoining line while Chris checked the connection from both ends.

“All right. That's a joint. I can do a joint.”

Artoo chirped.

“Don't agree with me yet. I said it like that on purpose.”

He needed his bench. Another brief switch put Billy outside while Chris worked inside with the removed part. Existing salvage supplied a sleeve; the original valve stayed intact. He cut the matching seat, fitted it, checked it against the damaged piece and made the support the bowcaster shot had destroyed.

When Chris returned outside, the sun had moved off the terrace's eastern edge.

He installed the assembly. Artoo ran the circulation while Chris watched the joint. Cold first, then warm. No seep. The support stayed firm under vibration. Chris checked it again after the line heated.

“There you go.”

He said it to the ship.

Kaze leaned from the ramp.

“Can we go now?”

“You been in the chair this whole time?”

“No.”

“Where'd you go?”

“Bathroom.”

Chris's expression changed.

“How is it?”

“Good. Door locks.”

For a moment, their theft had delivered everything either man could reasonably ask of it.

The rest of the afternoon went into learning the Falcon while it was still on the ground. Chris followed the controls, watched what responded, and stopped when he could not account for an indication. Artoo worked at the connected socket, showing him the affected systems on the display. They understood each other best when something physically happened.

At one point Chris pointed at an unfamiliar control.

Artoo gave a short whistle.

Chris looked at him.

“Yes, no, or your fucking birthday?”

The droid extended an arm and pushed his hand away from it.

“No. All right. We got no.”

Kaze moved out of the pilot's seat when Chris was ready to test the lift. He did it without an argument, which made Chris inspect the cushion before sitting down.

The Falcon rose a hand's breadth above the stone.

Dust blew outward. Arthur, standing well beyond the engines, lowered his hat against the wash.

Chris held it there, brought it down, and did it again. He tested turning and stopping over the empty terrace before he tried anything faster. The cooling connection held. The cabin kept its pressure during the sealed-system check.

By the time he shut down for boarding, the stolen ship could lift, turn and land under his hands. That was useful. It was not a degree from Space Harvard, where the tuition is three hundred thousand dollars and a son who won't call you.

There was still Billy.

“I ought to experience my own purchase,” he said through the mask.

“You didn't purchase it.”

“I selected it.”

“Then you should've selected a door.”

“The exterior suggested generosity.”

Chris stood at the foot of the ramp, looking into the narrow passage.

“You stay inside me. Daynah and Mahnah stay with you. Everybody gets there.”

“And if I wish to stretch my legs?”

“We land.”

Billy considered that.

“I will require a view.”

“We have been through the fucking window.”

“And yet I have seen remarkably little of it.”

Inside BELLY, Daynah had come over to the exterior monitor. Mahnah followed, sniffed Billy's banjo, then lowered himself near the couch. Daynah's back still caught when he straightened too quickly. He took his time sitting.

Billy looked at him.

“Would you care to lodge a complaint concerning the transport?”

“No. Got legroom in here.”

That wounded Billy more efficiently than the Wookiee had.

Arthur came aboard last and secured himself in a rear cockpit seat. Artoo settled at the socket behind the forward seats, wheels braced. Chris checked that the ramp was closed, then glanced at Kaze in the copilot's chair.

“We're going up, trying it in open space, and coming back here. No jumping anywhere.”

“I heard you.”

“I'm saying it before you find a button that makes this somebody else's afternoon.”

“Nigga, I'm sitting here.”

“Keep doing that.”

The ship lifted.

The terrace dropped beneath them. The freight road narrowed into a crooked pale line, then the roofs slipped below the cockpit. Chris turned out over the water before he climbed.

Arthur leaned forward despite himself.

The horizon widened.

For a while nobody talked.

Cloud swept across the glass, turning the view white. Then they broke through into hard blue light. The coast curved beneath a ragged edge of cloud, all the broken streets and furious people reduced to a place that looked peaceful from far enough away.

That is one of distance's filthier tricks.

Kaze watched the land fall behind them. He had stopped pretending the controls were the interesting part.

“Fuck.”

Arthur rested one hand against the window frame.

“Yeah.”

Inside, Billy had gone quiet too. Daynah watched the same view through Chris's live feed. Mahnah lifted his head at the change in the engine sound, found nothing he wanted, and put it down again.

Above the last thin cloud, the blue darkened. Sunlight remained sharp on the Falcon's metal while the sky ahead turned black.

Chris checked the cabin readings and the cooling line's response. He eased the thrust down once they were clear, leaving the world large beneath them.

Kaze pointed at the controls.

“So that one's up and down?”

“Depends which way you're moving it. Watch.”

Chris demonstrated a small change. The curved horizon shifted through the glass, then settled.

“And that?”

“Thrust.”

“That's the gas.”

“Close enough.”

“See? I know shit.”

“You know gas.”

Kaze watched another correction, following Chris's hand.

“Let me drive.”

“No.”

“Just a little.”

“No.”

“I've flown shit in GTA.”

Chris turned his head.

“You've flown shit in GTA.”

“Yeah.”

“How did it end?”

“Different ways.”

Arthur looked away from the window.

“Any of 'em good?”

“For me.”

Chris started to turn back.

Kaze caught the motion with a hand held up between them.

“Wait. No, seriously. How am i gonna learn to drive, if i never fucken start?”

“You start with something smaller.”

“Like your car?”

“No.”

“See? That's what I'm talking about. It's always some other thing. I get us a thing, you don't want me driving the thing. You want me to learn in a theoretical fucking car nobody has.”

Arthur nodded slowly.

“Could steal another.”

“Don't help,” Chris said.

Kaze tapped the armrest, considering him.

“You think I'm gonna do it on purpose?”

Chris looked at him properly then.

“No. I think you'll get annoyed with the part where you don't know how yet.”

Kaze opened his mouth.

Closed it.

“All right. That's fair. But you can tell me.”

The Falcon drifted steadily. Nothing close showed ahead. Chris studied the instruments again, reduced their motion further and pointed to the few controls he had just used.

“These. Only these. When I say let go, you let go.”

“Okay.”

“Not after you finish your thought.”

“Okay.”

They changed seats.

Arthur tightened his restraint.

Kaze noticed.

“Damn. You too?”

“I'm giving you my full attention.”

Chris put Kaze's hand where it belonged, then took his own hand away.

“Little movement. Don't chase what you just did with a bigger one.”

Kaze moved the control.

The ship's nose dipped slightly. He brought it back and held it.

“There,” Chris said. “Good.”

Kaze smiled without looking at him.

“I know.”

He tried a turn. Too much at first, then less when Chris told him. The world slid sideways and stopped where he wanted it.

“Now bring it back.”

Kaze moved his hand. The nose passed the point he'd meant to stop on.

“There. Stop it there.”

“I stopped moving it.”

“The ship didn't.”

“I can fucking see that.”

He corrected too far again. Chris watched the movement without reaching in.

“Less. Less than that.”

Kaze's jaw tightened.

“You're saying less after I do it.”

“I don't know what you're gonna do until you fucking do it.”

“You're supposed to be teaching me.”

“I am. I'm not a fucking driving psychic. I can't climb inside your head and find the little raccoon before he touches something.”

Arthur looked at Kaze's hands.

“He's reaching again.”

“Both of y'all shut the fuck up for one second.”

Chris went quiet.

Kaze made the correction. This time he began easing it back before the nose reached the point. The turn slowed, then stopped almost exactly where he wanted.

He waited.

Chris nodded.

“That was it.”

“I know.”

But this time he said it softly. He tried again, watching the horizon instead of his hand, and did it better.

For several minutes, the lesson worked.

Nobody crashed. Nobody died. The chair did not reject him for failing algebra.

“What'd they teach you in driver's ed?” Chris asked.

“I was asleep.”

“All of it?”

“I dropped out. I don't know what they did after.”

“You don't need a fucking diploma to stop overcorrecting.”

“Then quit talking like there's gonna be math.”

Billy's voice came through Chris's mask.

“How is the young man progressing?”

“He's doing fine.”

Kaze glanced over.

Chris caught it.

“Look where you're going.”

“You just said—”

“You were doing fine when I said it.”

Inside, Billy leaned nearer the monitor. Daynah had stretched his legs out, one hand resting in Mahnah's fur.

“I might like a turn,” Billy said.

Daynah looked at him.

“You?”

“Very much.”

“You'd have to let go of Chris.”

Billy considered a cockpit without Chris in it.

“I should like a bell, then. So I may summon the driver.”

“Got a whole banjo.”

Billy looked down at it.

Outside, Chris heard the first deliberate pluck.

“Do not fucking start.”

Chris pointed ahead.

“Pick something. Keep the nose steady on it.”

Kaze chose a bright point well clear of the planet and held their heading.

It was going well enough that he began trying to make it interesting.

He added thrust.

“Ease that back,” Chris said.

“I barely touched it.”

“I watched you touch it.”

Kaze reduced it, then tried to combine the turn with a roll.

The horizon tilted.

“One at a time.”

“I got it.”

“You're doing both.”

“Because I got both.”

The Falcon kept rotating after the movement Kaze expected to end it. He corrected the other way, harder.

Artoo shrilled.

A small contact flashed on the display. Ahead and below them, something white caught the sun.

Chris saw it move across the glass.

“Hand off.”

Kaze looked toward the light.

“What the fuck is—”

Chris reached across and pulled the control back. Kaze tightened his grip at the unexpected movement.

“Let go.”

“You're pulling it the other—”

“Let. Go.”

The Falcon's nose swung across the white object.

There was just time to see two small wheels beneath a seat.

Kaze released the control.

Too late.

The forward prong caught the chair's projecting framework. Metal struck metal with a deep knock through the hull. The chair spun, its little thrusters firing sideways, and slammed against the Falcon again farther back.

The second impact tore a strip from the ship's outer plating. In the cockpit, a warning sounded over Artoo's scream.

Chris's shoulder hit the side frame. Arthur caught the seat ahead with his reconstructed hand before its loose back swung into Kaze.

“Hands clear!” Chris shouted.

This time Kaze did it.

Chris killed the added thrust and brought the rolling view under control. The planet swept past the glass, slowed, then steadied below. A piece of pale debris drifted across the cockpit's side window.

Beyond it, the wheelchair was still moving.

Its occupant was strapped in beneath a clear helmet. One side of the chair's metal surround had folded inward. A thin white mark crossed the helmet near the man's temple.

The comm speaker clicked.

A flat electronic voice said, “You absolute cunt.”

Kaze leaned forward.

“Is that Stephen Hawking?”

Chris stared.

The chair corrected its spin with short, uneven bursts. The man inside worked his cheek against a control. His eyes were open, furious and fixed on them.

“You have hit Stephen Hawking.”

“I can see that,” Chris said. “Your helmet's damaged. Get alongside the hatch.”

Billy's voice came through the mask, suddenly stripped of its performance.

“Stephen?”

The man looked toward the cockpit.

“Who is that?”

“Billy. Aloysius.”

“I don't know a Billy.”

“We attended school together.”

“I attended school in St Albans.”

Billy said nothing.

That was not his friend returned. Different life. Different memories. The old Stephen stayed dead, and the universe had supplied another one for these two to hit with a fucking freighter.

Chris was already easing the Falcon alongside the chair. He kept its remaining thrusters clear of their hull.

“Arthur, side airlock. Keep the inner door shut till he's in. I'll bring him level.”

Arthur unfastened and moved down the passage. At the airlock panel, he checked the inner door's latch, vented the empty chamber and opened the outer hatch.

Kaze kept staring through the window.

“How the fuck do you hit a wheelchair in space?”

“You tell me!”

A pale feather of escaping air appeared at the damaged helmet seam.

Hawking's gaze flicked toward it. His chair turned toward the ship, one damaged thruster coughing short bright spurts.

“Which one of you was driving?”

“Talk inside,” Chris said. “Come on. That opening. Left of you.”

“Your left or my left?”

“The open fucking door!”

The chair lurched. One of its bent guards scraped along the Falcon, catching briefly on the torn plating.

Arthur, behind the closed inner airlock window, saw Hawking's eyes widen.

The little white mark forked across the helmet.

Chris brought the ship's sideways motion to a stop. Hawking fired his remaining maneuvering jet and dragged the guard free. The helmet struck the bent frame at his shoulder.

The crack opened.

Air streamed out. His mouth opened; the comm carried a harsh breath instead of words. He worked the control again, trying to keep the chair pointed into the lock.

It struck the sill with one wheel.

Arthur's hand stayed over the outer-door control, waiting for the rest of the frame to clear.

“Come on,” he said, too quietly for Hawking to hear.

The chair slid inward. Its last thrust carried the rear guard past the doorway. Arthur shut the outer hatch immediately and started the pressure cycle.

Inside the helmet, Hawking's eyes had stopped following him.

The man's fingers lay still against his lap. A loose cable tapped his wheel as the chair settled, making a small, ordinary sound in the air he had needed sooner.

When the inner door released, Arthur went through.

Chris secured the flight controls, leaving the Falcon steady, and hurried down the passage with Kaze behind him.

Arthur had opened the broken helmet. He checked the man's neck, then looked with the crimson eye.

He stayed there long enough to be sure.

“He's gone.”

Chris stopped in the doorway.

Kaze looked at the crushed guard, then at the dead man.

“There was fucking nothing out there.”

Chris turned on him.

“He was out there.”

“I know. I'm looking at him. I'm saying, out of all the room—”

“I said let go.”

“And I did.”

“After you finished talking.”

Kaze looked back at the chair.

For once, he did not immediately try another version of the answer.

Arthur reached down to stop a small wheel still turning against the floor.

The chair spoke.

“I have been involved in a collision.”

Chris lurched forward.

“Stephen?”

Arthur held up a hand.

“Look at the little screen.”

Across the chair's display, beneath the dead man's motionless hand, a message read: AUTOMATED INCIDENT ASSISTANCE.

“This is a stressful time,” said Stephen Hawking's voice. “Please remain calm.”

Nobody moved.

“Your wellbeing is important to us.”

Chris slowly put his hand over his mouth.

Oh, that is fucking evil. A company took a man's voice, put it in a box, and made him reassure his own corpse. I hope the design team choked on their complimentary fucking wraps.

Kaze crouched to look at the display.

“Does it know?”

“Occupant response unavailable,” the chair said. “Other driver, please identify yourself.”

Kaze pointed at Chris.

Chris slapped his hand down.

“You were in the seat.”

“You had your hand on it.”

“Because you wouldn't take yours off!”

“Then we both had a hand on it.”

The display changed.

“Multiple operators indicated.”

Chris looked at the little microphone.

“Don't fucking help him.”

Arthur folded his arms.

“Seems to me it's being thorough.”

“You saw what happened.”

“I did. You sure you want me to explain it?”

Chris looked away first.

The chair chimed. A short-range connection symbol blinked beside the Falcon's identifier. Its collision unit had found the ship it had just touched; the ship was answering with the identity it still carried.

“Registered owner,” said the dead man's voice. “Han Solo.”

Kaze looked up.

Chris looked down.

Arthur's mouth moved at one corner.

“Well,” he said. “We finally found something he left us that works.”

The chair copied an incident notice to the Falcon's communications record, addressed to its registered owner. It showed the damaged chair, the ship identification and an insurance-contact field awaiting a reply.

A box marked “Owner response” sat empty beneath the notice.

Han Solo's ship. Han Solo's name. Stephen Hawking's insurance claim. All it lacked was Han Solo having the sort of evening where he checked his messages.

“Can you take my name off?” Kaze asked.

“You didn't give it your name,” Chris said.

Kaze considered that.

“Good. You learning too.”

Chris stepped toward him.

Arthur caught the edge of the chair before their argument could bump it against the door.

“Both of you. There's a dead man in the way.”

Chris stopped. He looked at Hawking, then at the bent wheel wedged across the threshold.

Together they moved the chair clear. Arthur secured it in the nearby hold with an existing cargo strap. The body stayed buckled in. Chris shut off the damaged thruster assembly using its marked power control.

The speaker asked whether they would recommend the incident service to a friend.

Arthur switched that off too.

Billy had heard everything through Chris's feed. He sat inside with the banjo against his knee, looking at the dark screen of the silenced chair until Chris turned away.

Daynah rested a hand on his shoulder.

“Not your Stephen.”

“No,” Billy said.

After a moment he put his hat back on.

“Mine had considerably better road sense.”

Chris returned to the cockpit.

Artoo had remained at the socket. He displayed the affected section of the hull while Chris checked the ship's response. Torn outer plating; the pressure boundary held. The engines answered. Their repaired cooling joint had survived its first customer.

Kaze stood behind the copilot's seat instead of sitting in it.

“I didn't mean to hit him.”

Chris kept his eyes on the readings.

“I know.”

“You keep acting like—”

“I know you didn't mean to. I was there.”

Kaze rubbed his jaw and looked out at the planet.

Chris eased them into the return.

The horizon rose. Pale blue spread around the dark curve, then cloud washed across the cockpit again. Beneath it, the same coast returned, with the same broken harbor and all the people whose problems had continued while the Sins were briefly above them.

They had not escaped the world. They had gone for a drive and brought back a body.

Artoo sounded a warning as they descended. Chris corrected the angle, watched the terrace emerge beside the freight road, and brought the Falcon around over the water before lining up.

Kaze leaned forward.

“Can I at least park—”

“FUCK NO.”

He leaned back again.

“All right. Damn.”

The landing gear met stone. Chris held the ship steady until its weight settled, then shut the engines down.

For several seconds he sat with his hands resting on the controls.

Arthur unfastened his restraint.

“Nice landing.”

Chris looked back.

Arthur meant it.

“Thanks.”

Kaze stood to let him through.

“See, that's what I need. Positive reinforcement.”

Arthur squeezed past him.

“You let him land. Proud of you.”

Outside, evening wind moved dust along the terrace. Chris walked around the damaged forward section and looked up at the strip of missing plating.

Kaze followed him.

From this angle, the gouge looked small.

“Could've been worse,” Kaze said.

Chris looked at him for a long time.

“For the ship,” Kaze added.

Inside the Falcon, the incident notice remained on its little screen.

Registered owner: Han Solo.

New correspondence: one.
