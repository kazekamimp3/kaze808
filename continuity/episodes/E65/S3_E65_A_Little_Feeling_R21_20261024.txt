Episode 65 — A Little Feeling

Doctor Evil had put the word SENSATIONAL above a man who made pain go away.

He was very pleased with himself.

The sign stood at the back of an open freight shelter beside the submarine's berth. Black letters covered a length of pale cloth stretched across a wooden board. Beneath it, Shang Tsung examined a dockworker's swollen wrist while the doctor watched the collection box.

Shang rubbed a little dark salve along the injury.

“Slowly,” he said.

The worker flexed his hand. His shoulders lowered.

“Oh.”

“The pain will ease. The swelling still needs time. Use the other hand for lifting.”

The man paid, thanked him and left carrying his coat in the uninjured hand.

Doctor Evil waited until he was out of earshot.

“You rather spoiled the ending.”

“How?”

“By telling him it wasn't fixed. He had the happy face. We had the money. That was the ending.”

Shang closed the jar.

“He will return when he needs more.”

The doctor considered this.

“Yes. Well. Obviously, I was testing your grasp of the business.”

Behind them, two crewmen finished securing the low demonstration platform. A heavy stone treatment table occupied its front edge. Shang's cases stood against the rear wall beside a bronze basin; a brazier burned near the open entrance. The road ran past that entrance, with a narrow loading yard beyond the shelter's side opening.

Doctor Evil adjusted his collar and pointed at the sign.

“You must admit, the name is rather good.”

Shang looked up.

“People arrive suffering. They leave feeling wonderful. Sensational.”

The doctor waited.

“Yes,” Shang said, and reopened his case.

The doctor's little finger slowly descended from his mouth.

“You see, it's also a—never mind.”

Wade Wilson here. Welcome to the only clinic where the man openly calling himself Evil may be underselling the other guy. Still, the ointment worked. Already ahead of half the things I've had injected into me by men wearing lanyards.

Outside, a lean, tattooed stranger stopped the departing dockworker.

“That help?”

The man showed him the hand.

“Aye.”

Baldur watched the fingers close. Then he looked past the man at the sign.

He entered barefoot, bare-chested, bringing none of the hesitation Shang's other customers had shown around the brazier.

“What can you make me feel?”

Doctor Evil opened his mouth.

Shang stepped around him.

“What have you lost?”

“Everything.”

Baldur put his palm against the brazier's iron rim. A faint hiss rose from the contact. He held it there while Shang approached.

“Heat. Cold. Pain. Food. Drink.”

He lifted his hand. The skin was intact.

“I could be eating shit. You could tell me it was a delicacy. I'd have to judge you on your fucking enthusiasm.”

Shang took his wrist and turned it toward the light.

“How did this happen?”

“My mother.”

“An enchantment?”

“Protection.” Baldur spat the word. “That's what she calls it.”

Shang's fingers paused against the undamaged skin.

“And you want it removed.”

“I want to feel the water when I wash my hands.”

Shang looked down at the wrist he was holding.

“I want to know if a drink is cold without asking some bastard watching me drink it. If you have to hurt me, hurt me.”

Baldur stepped closer. The brazier hissed behind him.

“But don't tell me how fortunate I am.”

“I haven't.”

“Keep it that way.”

Shang released his wrist, making room between them.

Baldur stayed where he was.

“Can you?”

Shang met his eyes.

“I would need to examine it.”

Baldur searched his face. Then he sat on the stone table and offered his arm.

“Examine.”

The doctor pulled up a chair, decided it was too close, and carried it behind the thick post beside the loading-yard opening.

Shang laid out a bottle, cloth and slender metal instrument. His movements were precise. Baldur followed each one, leaning toward the case when its lid briefly hid what Shang was selecting.

“You can put that through my eye,” he said.

Shang looked up.

“If that's what it takes.”

“Your arm will suffice for the first test.”

Baldur extended it farther.

Shang applied a narrow stripe of liquid. When it produced no response, he dipped the instrument into another preparation. Smoke wound from its tip.

He pressed it against Baldur's wrist.

The point blackened. The skin did not open.

Baldur watched the sorcerer's eyes.

“Well?”

Shang drew the instrument away and set it down.

“The protection resists the treatment itself.”

“Yes. That's the part I fucking need help with.”

Shang closed the ordinary case. From his empty palm, a small flame unfurled.

Baldur stopped talking.

It swelled, curling around two dark hollows. A jaw opened beneath them. Fire crawled between the teeth of a skull hanging above Shang's hand.

Its light reached Baldur's face.

For the first time since entering, he smiled.

Shang let him look.

“This is more dangerous.”

“Good.”

“And more costly.”

Ah. There's the second price. I've trusted men who promised to fix my body. Funny how the difficult part always comes after you've started imagining it working.

Baldur pulled a purse from his belt and dropped it onto the table without looking away from the flame.

Doctor Evil came out from behind the post.

Shang's free hand reached the purse first.

The doctor stopped with his fingers spread above an empty patch of stone.

“My venue,” he said softly.

“Our arrangement stands.”

Shang tucked the purse inside his robe.

Doctor Evil drew back his hand, then held it out palm-up as though he had been introducing the table all along.

“And what a venue it is.”

Baldur slid off the slab.

“Hit me.”

Shang indicated the open yard.

“Outside would be preferable.”

“Here.”

“The structure—”

“You've got the money. Stop finding things to worry about.”

The skull left Shang's hand.

It struck Baldur in the chest with a crack that drove his bare feet across the grit. Fire spread over his ribs and shouldered up around his head. The bronze basin rang as he stumbled against the table.

He stood inside the blaze.

Waiting.

Behind him, a lick of flame caught the sign's lower edge.

Doctor Evil snatched up a cloth and beat at it.

“Not the lettering. The lettering was the expensive bit.”

Baldur pressed a hand to his chest. His smile had gone. As the fire guttered out, he looked down at the soot, then at Shang.

“Harder.”

The sorcerer glanced toward the burning cloth.

Doctor Evil dragged it clear of the board, stamped on it and pointed the crewmen toward the side opening.

“Out. And take the box. The collection box, not his—yes, that one.”

They carried it into the yard. The doctor remained beside the post.

Baldur stepped closer to Shang.

“Stop showing me what you sell to dockworkers.”

Shang spread both hands. The next skull grew between them, throwing hard shadows across the rafters.

He drove it forward.

The impact lifted Baldur off his feet.

His back struck the table. One stone leg snapped; the slab tipped, hit the platform and split down its length. The basin bounced across the floor. Flame rolled up the rear boards, and the shelter filled with the grinding crash of stone crushing timber.

Doctor Evil's chair tipped over behind him.

Nobody laughed.

Baldur rose through the smoke.

He stared at his hands. Flexed them. Turned them toward the firelight as though the answer might be on the other side.

Shang lowered his arms.

“Anything?”

Baldur's fist came down on one half of the tilted slab.

Stone burst apart.

A chunk punched through the display board and struck the post beside the doctor's head. He dropped behind it. Smaller pieces skittered out onto the road.

“Nothing!”

Baldur seized the bronze basin and hurled it through the rear wall. Boards exploded into the yard beyond. The basin struck outside with a long, flattening clang.

Shang pointed at him.

“There. A response.”

Baldur went still.

“I could get fucking angry before I paid you.”

Shang lowered the pointing hand.

“Your anger is understandable.”

“Don't tell me about my anger.”

“We have established that this is no ordinary affliction. To proceed, I would need time. Access to you. Cooperation.”

He moved sideways, putting the other half of the slab between himself and Baldur. The opening into the loading yard lay behind his left shoulder.

“What you possess could transform our understanding of protection.”

Baldur's jaw tightened.

“Possess.”

“A defense other men would give anything to—”

Baldur kicked that remaining chunk.

It hit the post like a battering ram.

The timber split. Doctor Evil threw himself toward the stacked crates outside, crawling as a roof beam crashed onto his abandoned chair.

Shang was already moving.

He slipped outside Baldur's reaching arm and drove his claws across the wrist. The points scraped skin without opening it. Shang twisted away before the answering hand closed, his young features shrinking into the older, sunken face as he dropped beside the ruined platform.

Fire burst low across Baldur's path.

Baldur went through it.

His punch struck the place Shang's head had occupied and demolished the platform's corner. Splinters lashed the wall. Shang rolled clear, came up facing the open side, and fired a skull from close range beneath Baldur's chin.

Baldur's head snapped back.

His body smashed into the next support. The post broke across his shoulders, and the roof dropped on that side with a roar of sliding tiles.

Shang ran for the opening he had kept behind him.

He had moved Baldur. He had not stopped him.

One hand caught the back of his robe.

The cloth pulled tight across Shang's throat. His feet left the floor, and Baldur swung him sideways into the stone foundation beneath the broken wall.

The impact emptied his lungs.

Baldur dragged him upright by the chest of his robe. Shang struck at the gripping wrist, claws grinding uselessly against it, while flame gathered in his other palm.

Baldur caught that hand and drove it against the foundation.

The fingers broke.

Shang screamed. Fire spilled between his bent knuckles and died against the stone.

Baldur stopped moving.

He watched the sorcerer's face.

“You can feel that.”

Shang tried to pull away. The movement brought another sound out of him, high and involuntary.

Baldur tightened his grip around the ruined hand.

“You can.”

His voice had dropped. He was close enough to see the blood collecting between Shang's teeth, close enough to watch the older face falter toward the younger one.

Beyond the crates, one crewman started forward.

Doctor Evil caught his sleeve and dragged him down.

Shang's good hand rose toward Baldur's throat.

Baldur caught him under the jaw and drove his head backward into the foundation.

The stone cracked.

He pulled him forward and struck him against it again.

The skull gave.

Shang's body dropped when Baldur opened his hand. Blood ran down the cracked foundation into the dust. His claws settled against the ground.

He did not move again.

Baldur stood over him, breathing through his nose.

A burning board fell from the roof. Somewhere inside the wreckage, a bottle broke.

He touched the blood on his chest and rubbed it between finger and thumb.

Then let his hand fall.

Doctor Evil rose just high enough to see over the crates.

The purse remained inside Shang's robe.

He looked at Baldur. At the purse. Back at Baldur.

He came around the crates in a crouch, eased the money out, and began drawing it toward his collar.

Baldur turned his head.

The doctor extended his arm instead.

“Refund.”

Baldur looked at the purse.

“You were going to keep that.”

“I was bringing it to you.”

“In your shirt.”

The doctor's smile tightened.

“Personal delivery.”

Baldur came closer.

Doctor Evil held the purse farther out. It shook against his fingers.

“I should clarify that my doctorate is not in this particular field.”

Baldur took the money.

For a few seconds they stood facing each other, with the broken man between them and smoke passing beneath what remained of the roof.

Then Baldur walked through the ruined wall and onto the nearby road.

The doctor did not follow.

Neither did anyone else.

When the footsteps had faded, he turned to the crewmen.

“Keep people out. Pull the cases away from the fire. From the outside. Nobody goes under that beam.”

One man went for water. The other retrieved the collection box, still intact behind the crates.

Doctor Evil accepted it and looked into the shelter.

“Should we fetch someone?” the crewman asked.

The doctor glanced at Shang.

“Yes. Someone with a shovel. Not another consultant.”

The scorched cloth lay folded beneath a fallen plank. Enough of the lettering remained to read SENSATION. The board behind it had a hole through its middle.

The doctor had it dragged clear, turned over and propped across the entrance. On its unburned back he wrote CLOSED.

Underneath, he added WE FEEL TERRIBLE.

He stepped back, waiting for the crewman to read it.

The man looked past the sign at Shang's body.

Doctor Evil capped the paint.

“Yes. Well. It's been a difficult afternoon for everyone.”

Across the water, three mornings had passed since Brian burned the poster.

The first, he slept. The second, he could cross the yard without the horizon tipping. By the third, his fingers were still tender and his shoulder complained when he lifted the bag, but he could keep up with Jar Jar's shortened stride.

Jar Jar had finished the promised loading work. His palms were healing, his water skin was full, and the northbound travelers would leave at noon.

Late in the morning, Brian was carrying both their breakfasts toward the awning when he found the Jawa on the crate.

Knees opening. Closing.

Hips thrusting.

A second Jawa slapped a rhythm against the cart. One Tusken scout stood behind the performer with his arms folded. Another leaned against the wall, shoulders shaking.

The Jawa arched backward and made Brian's little sleeping effort noise.

Brian stopped so abruptly that breakfast climbed the side of the bowl.

“Oh, shit.”

The performer saw him. Pointed. Thrust again.

Brian's face lit up.

“Oh, SHIT.”

Jar Jar came out beneath the awning.

“What happen?”

“They know me.”

The Jawa on the crate shook his hips and flicked the hem of his robe.

“They're doing my shit. Look at that. That's my shit!”

He handed Jar Jar the bowls and approached with both palms against his chest.

“I love you too. Seriously. I didn't know it had reached people like this.”

I have been booed in several jurisdictions. Sometimes while legally dead. That is laughter, Brian. But look at him. The little bastard has already booked the reunion tour.

Over the past days, the discovery had traveled with the people who'd made it. One demonstration beside a repair became another at a water stop, then an argument over who could reproduce the knee movement most accurately. The people who hadn't been on the dune had received a remarkably thorough account of Brian's pelvis.

Now the scout with folded arms stepped forward.

He planted his feet, lowered his hips and delivered three powerful thrusts with the solemn concentration of a man driving fence posts.

The Jawa answered by lifting the back of his robe.

Two bare ass cheeks flashed in the sunlight.

Brian slapped a hand over his mouth.

“Bro!”

He laughed. Then pointed delightedly.

“That's from the sleep stuff too. Deep cuts. These motherfuckers did their research.”

The Tusken turned, adjusted the back of his clothing and produced a competing flash. His mask remained firmly on, apparently the one remaining location where mystery still mattered.

The other scout barked out a laugh. A Jawa at the far wall kept repairing a wheel and ignored all of them.

Jar Jar held both bowls very still.

“Meesa was gone one minute.”

The Jawa on the crate began loosening an outer strip of cloth. He pulled it through his hands, spun it over his head and snapped it toward Brian.

Brian caught it against his chest.

His mouth fell open.

“A gift.”

The Jawa tugged.

Brian held tighter.

“No, I'm keeping this. First fan gift. You don't understand what this means.”

The Jawa pulled him a step closer to the crate, reached down and took the cloth back.

Brian nodded rapidly.

“Part of the routine. Got it. Respect the choreography.”

He climbed onto an empty crate beside the performer.

The drumming stopped.

“No, keep that going. I can work with that.”

He tapped his fingers against his leg, listening as the Jawa resumed. The rhythm stumbled whenever a loose iron handle rattled against the cart's side.

Brian held the handle still with his sore hand, shifted the other Jawa's striking point toward the wooden corner, and tapped an answering beat on the crate beneath him.

The sound sharpened.

He left a space. The Jawa struck into it.

Brian grinned.

“There you go.”

He changed the pattern. The Jawa followed the change, then added two quick taps of his own. Brian caught them on the next pass and left room for them again.

The Tusken adjusted his step.

Jar Jar's foot moved before he noticed. One bowl tilted. He caught it, hurried to put both down, and returned just as Brian brought the beat around.

For a little while, it worked.

Hands against wood. Iron answering at the end of a measure. The scout's heel landing in time. Brian hearing the next turn and making room for somebody else inside it.

He laughed out loud.

“That's fucking nice.”

He turned toward Jar Jar and let the next words ride over the tapping.

“T. P. P.? Yeah, I failed it, I bailed it.
Two weeks’ notice? Never mailed it.
I’m still lit, like a star being killed.
You get it because—”

The Jawa struck into the gap. Brian caught the beat again, laughing.

Jar Jar leaned closer to hear.

“Yousa leave them?”

“My old group. I named it. Founded the whole fucking thing.”

“Then why yousa say failed?”

Brian's fingers missed a tap. The Jawa filled the space.

“Because I bailed. I failed to remain in a situation that wasn't serving me. It's wordplay. Starkiller got it.”

Jar Jar looked toward the drum.

“Meesa like when yousa doing it with him.”

Brian looked too. The Jawa was still holding the rhythm for him.

He joined in, smiling again.

Then he lifted one hand.

“B. Moo.”

The Jawa copied the sound, badly.

“Moo.”

Brian stopped playing.

“B. Moo. There's a B.”

The Jawa struck the cart twice.

“Moo.”

“No, you can't drop the B. That's half the fucking name.”

He put a hand over the striking palm. The Jawa pulled away and took his rhythm with him.

Brian turned toward Jar Jar.

“Tell him.”

“Meesa no speak Jawa.”

“You can hear it, though. The B is silent to him. That's not a thing.”

“Then keep playing. Him was getting it.”

Brian looked back at the waiting hands.

Instead, he reached for his journal.

“I should do the proper introduction.”

He opened to the signed page, keeping the crossed-out bottom tucked against his palm.

“Starkiller said he loved the wordplay. This is the original artist. B. Moo. Questions after.”

The Tusken behind him began copying the raised hand.

Brian pointed to himself.

The Tusken pointed to himself.

Brian tapped the journal.

The scout solemnly tapped his empty palm, then raised one finger and waited with an exaggerated tilt of the head.

The Jawa bent over laughing.

Brian beamed.

“See? Even the presentation. They know the whole package.”

He tried to start the rhythm again, but the Jawa was now watching the scout impersonate Brian waiting for his own applause.

“Okay, let me show you where that comes in.”

He reached toward the drummer. The Jawa swatted his hand aside.

A cart creaked at the gate behind the gathering. Its handlers stopped, faced with bodies, crates and a strip of cloth now being whirled through the only clear passage.

From beneath the awning, Jabba barked an order in Huttese.

The dancers looked toward him. The cart handlers pointed into the yard.

Brian spread his arms.

“I know. The turnout's crazy.”

Jabba indicated the blocked entrance.

“Move your audience.”

Brian's smile widened.

“My audience. Exactly. That's what I've been saying.”

“Move them.”

Brian climbed down and approached the awning.

“Listen, if you're interested in having this regularly, we could talk about—”

Jabba leaned toward him.

“You can move them. Or you can carry everything they are stopping.”

Brian looked back at the loaded cart.

Then at his hands.

“I'm not carrying cups professionally. I'm definitely not carrying a fucking shop.”

Jar Jar stepped beside him with their bags.

“Then come. We leaving.”

“I've got to close the show.”

“North travelers not waiting for encore.”

Jar Jar handed Brian his bag and walked toward the gate. He paused to retrieve the bowls from the shade, set them out of the cart's path, then continued.

Brian turned back to his followers.

The Tusken had copied his entire confrontation with Jabba, including the glance at the cart and the outraged inspection of his own hands.

Brian clutched the journal against his chest.

“You guys are fucking unbelievable.”

He meant it fondly.

“All right. Walk with me.”

He beckoned grandly and headed for the gate. The Jawa followed for several paces, thrusting after every third step. The scout joined him, imitating Brian's grateful wave.

The cart rolled through the gap they left.

Jabba watched it pass and settled back onto his cushions.

Brian reached the road with his head high.

Behind him, the Jawa returned to the crate. The scout stayed beside the gate long enough to lift the back of his clothing one final time.

Brian put a hand over his heart.

“Love you too, bro.”

He joined Jar Jar and the northbound travelers.

The water skin knocked gently against Jar Jar's bag. Brian checked his own: wrapped saber, journal, three coins. His hand passed over the empty space where the poster had been.

It stayed there for a moment.

Then he closed the flap.

Behind them, the drumming began again without him.

Brian listened until distance thinned it into small, uneven knocks.

“They'll get the B,” he said.

Jar Jar glanced down.

“What?”

“Next time.”

He took out the journal while walking and found a blank space.

First international following.

Beneath it: Movement spreading organically.

The torn note slipped from between the pages.

Brian caught it before it reached the road.

Good words. We should make something.

Jar Jar slowed beside him.

“Friend?”

Brian stared at Shake's handwriting.

“Someone I worked with.”

“Yousa miss him?”

Brian folded the note and put it back.

“We had creative differences.”

Jar Jar waited for more.

Brian shut the book.

Then, very softly, he tried the opening cadence Shake had shown him.

The first two words landed.

This time, the pause was right.

Brian tried it again.

Jar Jar smiled.

“Yousa make that?”

Brian looked up at him.

“Yeah.”

He tucked the journal away.

“All me.”
