Season 3, Episode 31: Dead Money

Somebody had killed their ransom.

Lord Odran lay behind his desk with his throat open, one expensive slipper still twitching against the rug. Kaze stood in the doorway looking down at him. Beyond the open window, Daniel gripped the balcony rail with his feet and leaned into the room.

“That's going to affect the asking price.”

A red-haired woman stepped out from beside the curtains. She held a bloody dagger low against her thigh. A second blade was already pointed at Kaze.

“Close the door.”

Kaze looked at her, then at the dead man.

“Did you fucking do that?”

“No. I found him shaving.”

Daniel laughed. Kaze shut the door with his heel.

They had reached this inland market town after sunset, a few hours beyond the ravine. Odran's house overlooked its busiest square. Music spilled from the lower windows; hired soldiers drank in the courtyard while their employer entertained merchants upstairs. Arthur had heard enough of the lord's loud discussion of tomorrow's payments to propose taking the man himself. A living financier, carried off before his business was settled, might be worth considerably more than the contents of his pockets.

Chris and Arthur were still downstairs, expecting a hostage.

Kaze crouched beside the body.

“You couldn't have waited ten fucking minutes?”

The woman's eyes followed his hand, the black living tissue across his right side, the bloodstained ruin at his feet.

“Were you planning to ask politely?”

“I was planning to keep him alive.”

“Then you should have arrived earlier.”

Daniel climbed over the rail. His black hair was wind-tossed, his unbreakable bottle frosted around the amber liquor. He tucked it against his belt and put his spear beside the window, within reach.

“I'm Daniel. He's having trouble with a bereavement. Mostly financial.”

“Kaze,” Kaze said, standing. “And you are?”

“Katarina.”

Katarina Du Couteau. A Noxian assassin with a scar across one eye and a family name that opened certain doors and made others worth locking. She had entered this house to kill Odran, not to meet the people who wanted to steal him.

Footsteps approached outside.

“My lord?” a servant called. “Captain Veylan asks whether you've finished.”

Katarina's attention snapped to the door.

Kaze noticed.

“Another one?” he whispered.

“The man he's paying.”

“Then we're not fucking finished.”

He pulled Odran's chair upright, lifted the corpse into it and turned the back toward the door. Daniel caught the loose head before it struck the desk.

“Sir,” Daniel murmured, adjusting the collar, “we talked about posture.”

Kaze opened the door a crack.

“He's occupied. Where's the captain?”

The servant looked past his tattooed chest toward the familiar chair. Odran's right hand rested on the desk. Daniel, hidden behind its far corner, held the wrist in place.

“In the courtyard. Who are you?”

Kaze smiled, showing gold above silver.

“The reason he's occupied. Tell the cowboy downstairs to come up.”

The servant withdrew rather than push through a man blocking the doorway. Kaze closed it again.

Katarina lowered her raised dagger.

“Odran's money is downstairs. Three iron-bound chests. Veylan came to collect it. The price for killing the provincial governor tomorrow.”

Daniel released the dead hand. It dropped onto a bowl of sugared almonds.

“Very ambitious breakfast.”

Kaze looked at Katarina. “You want the captain dead too?”

“I want his company broken before it reaches the governor's residence.”

“Broken how?”

“Unable to carry out the attack.”

Kaze considered that, then looked toward the music below.

“Where are these chests?”

Katarina went to the window. Below, household porters were dragging three heavy boxes from a strongroom beneath the courtyard stairs. An empty wagon waited under the carriage arch opposite. A broad man in a dark breastplate watched the boxes. A red sash marked him out from the others.

“Odran inspects the payment before it leaves. Then Veylan gives the men their first share.”

“So they haven't been paid.”

“Not for tomorrow.”

Kaze's irritation gave way to interest.

“You killed the buyer before the seller got his money.”

“I killed the man whose money was buying a murder.”

“Yeah. Your version sounds better at dinner.”

She glanced at him.

“We aren't having dinner.”

The door opened behind them. Arthur entered with the servant, took in Odran's motionless shoulders, the bloody rug, and the woman with two blades.

“Well. That's unfortunate.”

The servant drew breath to scream. Arthur caught him by the collar and shut the door.

“Don't make me regret bringing you.”

The man stared at the blood. “You killed him.”

“She did,” Kaze said.

Arthur looked at Katarina. “You got any money?”

“No.”

“Then this is getting worse.”

Daniel pointed toward the courtyard. “Three chests. Soldiers waiting to get paid. Their captain's trying to buy a new governor.”

Arthur listened as Katarina gave him the rest. He asked one question about the payment, another about the courtyard exits, and then turned to the servant.

“Does your master generally inspect purchases up here?”

“Yes.”

“Captain ever been up here before?”

“Yes.”

“Good. Tell him Lord Odran would like his money brought upstairs.”

The servant's eyes moved to the corpse.

“He isn't going to believe that.”

“He doesn't have to believe you forever.”

Katarina stepped between Arthur and the door.

“Once they discover he's dead, Veylan may take the money and leave. I need him here.”

Arthur met her stare.

“And I need the money away from his horses. Seems we can be useful to one another.”

“For how long?”

“Till we ain't.”

Kaze laughed. Katarina considered the room, the window and the three men who had made a mess of her departure without yet drawing a weapon on her.

“Don't promise what I will do,” she said.

Arthur tipped his head. “Wouldn't know where to start.”

Downstairs in the courtyard, Chris stood beside a stone fountain with THE CRITIC slung across his back. Billy remained inside BELLY with Charles and Meatwad.

A soldier approached Chris carrying two cups.

“You with the captain?”

“No.”

The soldier offered him one. Chris sniffed it.

“What's this?”

“Wine.”

“That answered very little.”

He took a cautious sip and looked into the cup again.

“Tastes like somebody washed a saddle in fruit.”

The soldier laughed. “Better than what we've had on the road.”

“That's a terrible way to sell a drink. Better than hardship. Put that on the bottle.”

The man took a long swallow. Across the courtyard, Veylan struck the nearest chest with his knuckles while arguing with Odran's steward. A plate of roast meat rested on its lid, beside his wine.

“Been waiting long?” Chris asked.

“Since noon.”

“For the wine?”

“For our money.”

Chris looked at the soldier's worn boot soles and the patched seam beneath his mail.

“The wine's to stop you thinking about that?”

“The wine's what he gave us when we started asking.”

He pointed his cup at the chest nearest the wagon wheel.

“Our advances are in that one. Enough to send something home. If he ever opens it.”

Chris watched the captain wave away a man who had approached the boxes.

“Doesn't look like he's in a hurry.”

Above them, Daniel appeared at the head of the courtyard stairs. He didn't shout. He beckoned Chris over, waited until he reached the bottom, and leaned across the rail.

“Lord's dead. New plan. Take the money.”

Chris blinked.

“Whose plan?”

“Arthur's.”

“All right. Why's the lord dead?”

“Woman upstairs.”

“Kaze?”

“Different kind of meeting.”

Daniel pointed out Katarina's position, the captain, and the chests. Chris followed the gesture, then glanced toward the man who had given him wine.

“They haven't paid these boys.”

“That's becoming the theme.”

“No, listen. They were told noon. They've been standing around hungry all day while their captain's eating roast fucking meat.”

Daniel's expression sharpened.

“How many feel like that?”

“I talked to one. I'm not conducting a fucking survey.”

“Fair. Tell Arthur that when he comes down.”

Daniel headed back up the stairs. Chris set the wine aside and unshouldered his rifle.

“Don't confuse his company with his escort,” said a dry voice from the weapon.

Teemo, the scarred scout whose consciousness was bound to THE CRITIC, had heard the conversation. Chris glanced at the rifle.

“They're dressed the fucking same.”

“Some are guarding the boxes. Some are waiting for them to open.”

Chris looked again. The men Veylan had placed around the chests stood facing their own comrades.

“Yeah. All right.”

“Decide which you're shooting before they all decide about you.”

Chris carried the rifle to the fountain, changing his angle so the waiting men were no longer behind the escort.

Chris checked THE CRITIC's action, seated one of his heavy cartridges, and rested the rifle across the fountain's broad stone rim. The muzzle faced the empty strip between the carriage arch and the stairs. His shoulder settled behind the stock; his feet found firm stone.

The soldier with the cups stopped smiling.

“What're you doing?”

Chris kept his attention on the arch.

“Waiting to get paid.”

Veylan brought one chest and five men. Arthur had asked for all three boxes; this was what he got. Four soldiers hauled it up the stairs, swearing at its weight, while the captain followed with a bodyguard.

Inside the study, Odran sat with his back to them. Kaze stood beside his chair. Daniel had moved behind a tall screen near the window, taking his spear with him. Katarina waited out of sight behind the open door.

Veylan stopped on the threshold.

“I don't know you.”

Arthur gave him an agreeable smile. “We've had much the same complaint.”

“What happened to his household guard?”

“Downstairs. Eating. You'd be surprised what a man can miss over supper.”

The four carriers set the chest on the carpet. One straightened slowly, rubbing his lower back. Veylan watched Odran's unmoving hand.

I'd been wondering how long they could make a dead man look wealthy instead of dead. About one trip up the stairs, as it turned out.

“My lord.”

No answer.

Kaze put a hand on the chair.

“Before we get into all that, how much is in the other two?”

Veylan's eyes went to the rug beneath the desk. Blood had soaked through the fringe.

His hand went to his sword.

One of Arthur's heavy UNSHAKEN revolvers was already level with his chest.

“Leave that where it is.”

For an instant the captain held the blade half drawn. His bodyguard's hand stopped on a pistol. The carriers looked from one weapon to the other.

Veylan breathed out through his nose.

“You've killed him.”

“That's been established,” Arthur said.

“And you think you can ransom a corpse?”

Kaze turned Odran's chair around.

The lord's head sagged sideways. A ribbon of fresh blood ran down his expensive waistcoat. One almond remained stuck to his palm.

“No,” Kaze said. “I think we can rob you.”

Veylan looked at the carriers.

“Put the chest back on the landing.”

Nobody moved.

Arthur watched the men watching their captain.

“Before you hurt yourselves carrying it again, when was he planning to pay you?”

“After delivery,” Veylan said.

Arthur glanced down at the chest.

“Looks delivered.”

“It's not their money yet.”

The carrier rubbing his back stopped.

Kaze saw the change and smiled at Veylan.

“Say that louder. Might help.”

The captain let go of his sword. His empty hand rose slowly.

“I can give you a share. Enough to make this worthwhile. You let me take the company out, and none of us needs to die here.”

“Out to where?” Katarina asked.

She stepped along the side wall, clear of Arthur's line to the doorway. Veylan knew her name when he saw her; recognition tightened his mouth before he could hide it.

“Du Couteau.”

“Where are you taking them?”

He said nothing.

She drew a folded order from Odran's desk and opened it with one hand.

“Your column goes through the north gate before dawn. The governor's guards have been ordered to admit a supply escort. Does your company know what happens once they're inside?”

Veylan looked past her at the open door.

Arthur saw him calculate the distance.

“Don't.”

The bodyguard drew anyway.

Arthur shot him through the face. Blood and bone struck the doorpost behind him, and the bodyguard folded before his pistol cleared its holster.

At the same moment Veylan grabbed the nearest carrier and drove him toward Arthur. The man stumbled into the revolver's line. Arthur shoved him aside with his rebuilt left hand. The terrified servant scrambled into the corridor and fled the other way. Veylan was already through the doorway, shouting to the courtyard.

“Assassin! Take the money out!”

Kaze followed him onto the landing. At the upper stair rail, Veylan saw Chris below, dragged out a compact pistol and aimed into the courtyard.

Kaze caught the barrel in his right hand and wrenched it upward.

It fired through his palm into the night above the roof.

Black blood struck the rail. Kaze's fingers broke around the weapon, then tightened as living tissue began knitting them together. He drove Veylan backward into the study door with enough force to split its panels.

“That's my fucking friend.”

Veylan stabbed up beneath Kaze's ribs with the sword he'd finally drawn. The blade entered. Kaze flinched, a sharp breath escaping him, and caught the captain's wrist before he could pull it out.

For half a heartbeat they were close enough to hear each other breathe.

Then Kaze dragged him forward by the sword arm and drove his forehead into Veylan's face.

The captain's nose and upper jaw collapsed. His knees buckled. Kaze ripped the lodged blade free of his own side, turned it in his bloody hand, and shoved it through Veylan's throat into the broken door behind him.

The shouting stopped. The four carriers backed against the study wall and raised their hands.

Below, the captain's order had already started moving the money.

Two soldiers pushed the remaining chests toward the wagon. Another seized Chris's wine companion by the shoulder and ordered him to take the reins. The man hesitated. A crossbowman under the carriage arch lifted his weapon toward the top of the stairs.

Chris had a clear line to him across the emptied strip of paving.

“Move away from the horse,” Chris told the man with the cups.

The man jumped back.

THE CRITIC fired.

The heavy rifle drove against Chris's shoulder and the stone rest. Water leaped from the fountain; the report struck the enclosed courtyard hard enough to drown the music inside. The projectile tore through the crossbowman and buried itself in the thick inner pier behind him. His weapon spun onto the cobbles. Broken stone sprayed the mouth of the arch. One escort soldier staggered back, blood running between the fingers pressed to his ear.

Chris recovered his stance and worked the action. One spent casing hit the paving beside his boot.

The second chest had stopped moving.

“Now watch the others,” Teemo said from the rifle.

Chris did. Two men had dropped flat. Neither was reaching for a weapon. He shifted his aim past them toward the escort still trying to reach the wagon.

Upstairs, Daniel sprang from behind the screen and went through the window onto the balcony. He caught the rail with his free hand and passed the spear to his gripping right foot. Then he kicked off the balcony, cracking a baluster as he launched across the courtyard toward the wagon.

A swordsman turned into the point.

Daniel drove it through the man's chest, landed on his other foot and wrenched the spear free as the body fell. He caught the haft in his left hand. A second man rushed him from the side. Daniel's weight seemed to collapse away from the blow. His ice hand caught the attacker's wrist; his heel swept the man's ankle, and both went down with Daniel above him.

The stone received the back of the man's skull. Daniel struck him once more with a short, brutal palm and got up, breathing liquor into the cold vapor around his face.

“Terrible onboarding. Nobody showed me the break room.”

Across the courtyard, Chris drew CHOW MAIN from his right hip rather than try to swing the heavy rifle into the approaching men. Two stopped. A third ducked behind the wagon.

Katarina came down the stairs on the opposite side. A loyal escort soldier lunged at her from beneath them. She caught his sword against one dagger, stepped inside his reach and drove the other under his jaw. She let him fall and turned toward the arch.

“North gate,” she called to Arthur. “A rider's leaving.”

Arthur had reached the landing. Beyond the arch, one of Veylan's men was mounting a horse, a dispatch case banging against his hip. Arthur raised his revolver. The rider twisted in the saddle, reaching for his own weapon.

Arthur fired once, hitting him beneath the jaw.

The rider fell backward off the horse and lay still, his neck torn open. The animal shied, dragging an empty stirrup against the paving. Katarina reached the fallen man, took the dispatch case and checked what was inside while the surviving escort backed away from her.

Two escort men started toward the stairs, saw Veylan's body in the doorway, and stopped. One put his sword down before anyone asked.

Arthur walked down into the courtyard.

“Those chests stay here.”

The soldier beside Chris stared at him.

“That's our pay.”

Arthur looked at the two boxes, then up at the study. Kaze stood on the balcony. He dropped the captain's pistol beside his feet as his palm knitted closed. Blood darkened his bare side. He was watching the argument rather than looking for another man to kill.

“Is it?” Arthur asked.

“We walked three weeks for him.”

“Doesn't answer the question.”

The soldier's mouth tightened. Behind him, surviving men began gathering around the wagon. Some had swords in their hands. Some had set them down. Their captain was dead upstairs, and the strangers were now making their own claim on the reason they'd come.

Daniel came to stand beside Arthur.

“They carried one upstairs for us,” he said quietly. “I'd call that billable.”

Arthur glanced at him.

“You proposing we pay everybody we rob?”

“No. I'm proposing they take one box and stop being our problem.”

Chris kept his revolver down beside his leg, not holstered.

“The one by the wheel,” he said. “He showed me that one when he was complaining. Told me it covered the first shares.”

The soldier nodded once, quickly.

Arthur looked up at Kaze.

Kaze rested his wounded hand on the rail. “You're buying an army now?”

“I'm buying the end of an argument.”

“Expensive fucking silence.”

“Still leaves us two.”

Kaze considered the men and the coins they hadn't yet seen. Then he laughed.

“Tell them to take it before I change my mind.”

Arthur turned back.

“One chest. You put your weapons on the ground, take your own wounded, and leave the other two.”

“Where do we go?” the soldier asked.

“Somewhere you weren't paid to murder a governor.”

Katarina approached with the dispatches in her hand.

“I'll take the gate orders. Your company banners stay here.”

One of the men spat onto the paving.

“And we get blamed for all of it?”

She met his eyes.

“You can leave before dawn with your share, or arrive at the governor's gate carrying a dead traitor's orders. Choose.”

The man looked toward the house. In the upper doorway, Veylan slumped beneath the sword jutting through his throat.

He put down his banner.

They lowered their weapons because they wanted the money, their lives, and a way out that didn't require either of these strangers to like them. Four men lifted the selected chest. Others helped the wounded toward the gate.

Arthur watched until the last armed holdout set his sword on the wagon.

“Pleasure doing business.”

“Fuck you,” the man said.

Arthur nodded. “There it is.”

Chris holstered CHOW MAIN and slung THE CRITIC. He looked at the stairs, then at the remaining chest below.

“I'm not carrying that heavy bastard down.”

Kaze leaped from the balcony and landed beside the fountain, cracking two paving stones beneath his shoes. He looked at Chris's shoulder.

“You all right?”

“Yeah. You?”

Kaze flexed his hand. The last split along the palm was closing. The hole beneath his ribs had drawn tight, leaving black blood smeared down his side.

“Fucking rude, wasn't he?”

“Shot at me before introducing himself.”

Kaze's laugh came out sharp and genuine.

Chris stepped into the open center of the courtyard and let Billy take over. His human jaw opened wide, their shared body folding through its familiar grotesque inversion. Billy emerged at that exact spot, enormous beneath his hat. Chris was now inside BELLY, with his own equipment; the great catfish alone stood outside in his place.

Billy surveyed the broken doorway, the blood, and the two very different groups of guests fleeing the house.

“I see the evening has become informal.”

“The money,” Arthur said, pointing.

“I had noticed.”

Kaze went back up the stairs and dragged the first chest onto the landing. He lifted it over the rail and lowered it into Billy's waiting hands. Billy set it beside the second.

Back on the paving, Kaze formed Black Marrow into his right hand. The living sword emerged from his own flesh; two short cuts broke the chests' iron hasps. He lifted the lids. Stamped gold filled their upper trays, with silver packed beneath.

“Now that's a fucking evening,” he said.

He closed them again and let the sword reintegrate. Behind him, the four carriers came down the stairs with their hands empty. Arthur waved them toward their departing comrades.

Katarina watched Billy's head and neck swell as he bent over the first box. His mouth spread wider than the iron-bound lid. He took it through his jaws whole, then the second, routing both into protected storage inside BELLY. Nothing remained on the paving but scrape marks.

She looked at Arthur.

“How much did you get?”

“Haven't counted.”

“And you paid them without counting theirs.”

Arthur glanced toward the open gate.

“They did the same. Everybody took a chance.”

Katarina folded Veylan's orders into the dispatch case. The governor's gate seals, the time of entry, the payment instructions: enough to warn the intended victims and expose the men who had arranged access. She checked the seals against the order from Odran's desk, folded the papers together and closed the case.

Kaze stepped into her path, leaving room to pass.

“You leaving already?”

“I have somewhere to be.”

“After that, have a drink with me.”

Her eyes moved over the black blood on his hand and the still-wet smear along his ribs.

“Is that how you usually ask?”

“Usually there's less blood.”

Daniel, passing behind him, made a doubtful sound.

Kaze didn't turn. “You're not helping.”

“I thought we were building trust.”

Katarina's mouth twitched despite herself. She looked back at Kaze.

“You've had enough of my evening.”

“For now.”

She walked past him toward the gate, neither hurrying away nor inviting him to follow. At the arch she stopped beside the soldier's abandoned cup, moved it out of her path with one boot, and disappeared into the street with the captured orders.

Arthur joined Kaze beneath the stairs. Billy had taken the two chests; Daniel was pulling the company's banner off its pole, laughing at the motto stitched into it.

“Loyalty beyond gold.”

He held it up for Arthur.

“Apparently not very far beyond.”

Arthur took the banner, wiped a speck of blood from his revolver with its hem, and handed it back.

Kaze looked up at Odran's lit window.

“Still think we should've got paid for him.”

“We did,” Arthur said. “Just changed who paid.”

Billy turned toward the open gate, rolling his shoulders comfortably beneath his hat.

“A fine host. Everything settled before dessert.”
