Season 3, Episode 71 — Joint Custody

Daynah had almost got the last splinter out when somebody set a stone wheel down beside the ruined pavilion.

Not dropped. Set.

The wheel sank through the lane.

A green hand rested on its upper edge. Behind it stood another enormous green man, barefoot, black-haired, his torn trousers powdered with pale quarry dust. Light burned beneath his skin. The air around his shoulders shivered.

Daynah looked at the wheel, then at the man.

At last.

An actual fucking problem.

Hulk looked past him at Richard Simmons's body.

“He wanted heavier weights.”

The wheel had a hole bored through its center. A second lay across Hulk's other shoulder. Richard had apparently found someone capable of supplying exercise equipment that required a geological survey.

Hulk lowered that one too.

His gaze moved to Bob on the footing, then to the broken wooden limbs scattered around Daynah's feet.

“You did this.”

Daynah pulled the splinter free. Blood followed it down the inside of his gauntlet.

“Yes.”

No bad-contractor defense. No claim that Richard had tragically slipped onto a fist. He stood up from his stone, thirteen feet of battered green muscle, and threw the splinter away.

He set his shortgun in the shelter behind that stone. Then he flexed his fingers inside Kahuhu.

Hulk stepped between the wheels.

The harbor lay downhill behind him: roofs, lifting cranes, the close-packed streets leading to the water. Behind Daynah, the pavilion backed onto a bare limestone shoulder. Beyond that shoulder were quarry cuts and the open sea.

“Behind Daynah,” Daynah said. “Plenty room.”

Hulk glanced uphill, then back at the bodies.

“You're worried about where I hit you?”

Daynah's mouth pulled into a bloody grin.

“Worried you miss.”

He drove forward.

Kahuhu's broad right fist came at Hulk's jaw. Hulk stepped inside the swing, caught the forearm with both hands and turned beneath it. Daynah's forward foot left the ground. His other leg struck a wheel, tipped it, and then Hulk drove him headfirst through the pavilion's stone footing.

The platform disappeared around his shoulders.

Earth struck his mouth. Rock burst against his cheek. The whole upper terrace dropped, carrying Hulk down with him into the opening.

Behind them, the roof slid off its timber supports and crashed across the wrecked exercise floor. One of the stone wheels rolled after it and split the remaining steps.

Hulk still had Daynah's arm.

He hauled it upward and stamped between his shoulder blades.

Daynah's chest went deeper into the stone. Something inside him gave with a short, wet knock.

This was not a firm motivational adjustment. We had left the personal-training portion of the afternoon and entered the part where your spine sends a resignation letter through your asshole.

Daynah spat grit and blood. He brought his free gauntlet against the rock beside his face, drove through its broken seam and tore away the ledge Hulk was standing on.

Hulk fell half a step.

That was enough.

Daynah rolled beneath the held arm, drove a knee into Hulk's abdomen and ripped his wrist out of the grip. His shoulder screamed. He kept moving, came up inside the crater and struck twice beneath Hulk's ribs.

The first punch folded flesh around the gauntlet.

The second lifted Hulk off his heels.

Hulk smashed his forehead into Daynah's face.

Daynah hit the crater wall backward, nose running red, and Hulk was already on him. A fist crushed the stone beside his ear when he slipped. The next caught his raised guard and drove both gauntlets into his chest.

The hillside shook.

Down at the harbor, windows burst along a warehouse frontage. A roof ridge slumped. People spilled into the lane beneath it, shielding their heads as tiles struck the road.

Daynah saw the falling tiles over Hulk's shoulder.

He stopped trying to hold that patch of ground.

As Hulk came in again, Daynah seized an arm, turned with the charge and drove them both uphill through the back of the crater. Stone peeled away around their bodies. They burst out across the limestone shoulder and tore a trench toward the quarry.

Hulk twisted during the slide and put Daynah underneath him.

The trench deepened.

Daynah planted both feet against Hulk's middle and kicked him clear. Hulk went backward through an abandoned cutting shed, its walls exploding around his shoulders. He came out carrying the roof beam.

Daynah rose into a pale, gathering glare.

His body grew heavier, taller, the green of his skin deepening toward dark jade. His black coils remained short and dense. Kahuhu expanded around his hands with a grinding adjustment of plates. Gold and green moved through the pale light close to his body.

Kahuna Sovereign.

Hulk hurled the beam before Daynah had finished straightening.

Daynah knocked it sideways. It burst apart against the quarry face.

Hulk came through the splinters and hit him.

This time Daynah's guard held its shape.

His feet still cut two long furrows through the limestone.

He lowered one fist.

“Better.”

Hulk's mouth tightened.

“For who?”

He drove a shoulder into Daynah's waist and carried him off the quarry lip.

They fell into the lower cut together.

Daynah hooked an arm around Hulk's neck, turned in the air and forced Hulk beneath him before they struck. The impact punched a bowl into the quarry floor. Blocks stacked along the nearest wall toppled inward. White dust rushed up around them and covered the sky.

Daynah stayed on top.

He brought Kahuhu down against Hulk's face, once, twice, then a third time as Hulk caught the descending wrist. Blood splashed the pale stone. Hulk's split cheek pulled back across his teeth.

His free hand closed around Daynah's throat.

He stood up under him.

Daynah's feet left the ground. He drove an elbow into Hulk's temple, but Hulk turned with it, swung him by the throat and slammed him sideways through the nearest stack of cut blocks.

The blocks became flying wreckage.

Daynah struck the far wall shoulder first. His left arm went slack for a beat. Hulk tore a slab out of the shattered stack and thrust it after him like a blade.

Daynah moved off the line.

The slab drove into the wall beside him, splitting it from floor to rim.

He caught Hulk's wrist against the embedded stone. His other gauntlet hammered the elbow. Hulk bent the arm before it could be straightened, drove his knee into Daynah's thigh and tore the slab free with both of them still attached.

Daynah released before the stone carried him over.

Hulk swung it flat.

The edge caught Daynah across the abdomen. Skin opened above his waistcloth. He flew across the cut and landed on one knee, a hand pressed against the wound.

The flesh was already drawing together beneath his fingers.

It was doing so around pain that had no intention of leaving.

Hulk's cheek had closed too. He wiped his mouth with the back of his wrist and came forward through the settling dust.

He threw the slab ahead of him. Daynah stepped out of its path, and it struck the opposite wall edge first, leaving a broad broken tongue projecting into the cut.

Daynah moved left.

Hulk shifted to meet him.

Daynah dipped toward the same low entry that had landed beneath the ribs. Hulk's knee rose into the space his head should have occupied.

Daynah had stopped short.

His right hand caught the raised leg. He stepped through, turned his hips and threw Hulk across the quarry into the slab embedded at the far wall.

Stone snapped under Hulk's back.

Daynah followed before he could rise, stamping the broken slab across his chest and driving a fist through it.

The rock shattered.

Hulk caught the gauntlet beyond it.

His fingers squeezed until the articulated plates ground against one another.

“Again,” Hulk said.

He yanked Daynah down into an uppercut.

Daynah's jaw snapped shut on blood. His head struck the quarry wall, and a shelf of stone broke loose above them.

Hulk shoved him into its path.

Daynah raised both gauntlets. The shelf struck them and drove him to one knee. Through the rock he could feel the load shifting, the outer end sagging toward the open cut while the inner edge remained wedged against the wall.

Hulk stepped in to drive him farther underneath it.

Daynah broke the inner edge.

The shelf dropped sideways.

He moved with it, shoving the falling mass across Hulk's advance instead of holding it overhead. Hulk punched through the stone. Daynah met him through the opening with his forehead, then hooked behind his leg and drove him backward beneath the rest of the collapse.

The quarry wall came down.

For a moment there was only white dust, falling grit, and Daynah's breathing.

Then green light showed through the rubble.

The mound lifted.

Hulk came out of it with both arms spread, splitting the buried weight above him. Blood striped his chest. A torn piece of scalp hung against one temple and drew back into place as Daynah watched.

The light beneath Hulk's skin strengthened.

Loose stones began jumping on the floor.

The first pulse struck Daynah in the chest and shoved him backward. The second tore the dust away in a widening ring. Cracks ran up the remaining quarry face, joined at the rim, and continued toward the harbor road.

Daynah looked toward the daylight above them.

Beyond the broken lip, a crane at the waterfront swayed hard enough for its suspended load to strike the mast. Bells rang below, irregular and urgent.

Hulk was coming through the rubble.

Daynah did not wait for the next pulse.

He drove upward on a burst of Ki, caught the rim and pulled himself onto the seaward side. Hulk leapt after him. Daynah struck him in the air from the flank, carrying the collision away from the harbor and out over the bare headland.

They landed on a slope of shattered limestone above the surf.

The slope failed under them.

A whole white section of coast broke loose and slid toward the water, taking both men with it.

Hulk struck first on the moving ground. Daynah turned enough that the blow caught his shoulder instead of his head. The arm behind that shoulder went numb. He drove the other gauntlet into Hulk's mouth and felt teeth against the metal.

Hulk spat blood into his face.

They kept hitting each other as the land fell.

There was no solid footing left to win. Every planted foot broke another stone free. Daynah seized Hulk around the waist and tried to turn him; Hulk widened his stance across two sliding slabs and dragged him down. Daynah's back struck a ridge, and the ridge ripped open his skin from shoulder to hip.

He roared, threw his weight into the roll and carried Hulk over it with him.

They struck the surf in a rising wall of white water.

The incoming wave vanished beneath the impact. Water rushed out of the broken inlet, exposing weed-dark stone. Then the sea returned across their knees, their waists, and the cliff fragments that had fallen around them.

Daynah rose with Hulk held across his shoulders.

His wounded back stretched open again.

He drove up, turned and slammed Hulk onto a broad slab above the waterline. The slab broke through its middle. Hulk's body folded into the gap, and Daynah followed with both fists.

The next blow drove him deeper.

The one after that split the stone beneath him.

Hulk caught Daynah's arms before the third could fall.

His skin shone green through the blood.

He pulled Daynah forward, planted both feet into his chest and launched him back through the spray.

Daynah hit the cliff, punched into it, and came out before the collapsing rock could bury him.

His left arm was working again. Badly. Enough.

Hulk wrenched himself out of the split slab.

Daynah charged low, drove his gauntlet under Hulk's raised arm and turned through the hit. Hulk swung across his back. Daynah's fist struck the rock behind him as the two bodies twisted past each other.

Hulk's shoulder caught between the gauntlet and the jagged stone.

A thick strip of flesh tore away.

It hit the wet ledge beside them with a slap.

Hulk drove Daynah's head into the rock.

Daynah struck back into the open shoulder, shoved him off and raised his fist again.

Something sneezed.

Both men stopped moving forward.

They did not lower their hands.

The torn flesh on the ledge contracted.

A small fist pushed out of it.

Then another.

The wet lump stretched, rolled onto its front and acquired a head with the grim concentration of a man assembling himself without the fucking instructions.

A green baby sat up between two stones.

It blinked seawater off its eyelashes.

Daynah looked at Hulk.

Hulk looked at the baby.

The baby looked directly at Daynah, raised both arms and said,

“Dada.”

All right.

I would like to withdraw every joke I have ever made about the rhythm method. Apparently the danger is percussion.

Daynah slowly pointed at Hulk's bleeding shoulder.

“Came off him.”

The wound was already closing. Hulk looked down at the little green face, then back at Daynah.

“You pulled him out.”

“Punched.”

“I was there.”

The baby made an impatient sound and opened and closed its hands.

Daynah took one step away.

The baby stood.

Its knees wobbled. It sat down hard enough to crack the stone beneath its backside, frowned at the result, then tried again.

Hulk put out a hand to steady it.

It slapped his fingers away and reached past him.

“Dada.”

Daynah's jaw worked.

“Daynah.”

“Dada.”

“Day. Nah.”

The baby slapped its knee, delighted.

Hulk glanced at Daynah.

“He knows your name.”

“No. Knows wrong name.”

Water struck the ledge and washed around the baby's feet.

Daynah's hand moved before the next wave reached it.

He lifted the child by the middle, tucked it against his less battered side and climbed onto the higher rock.

Hulk watched him do it.

Daynah noticed the look.

“Water coming.”

“I saw.”

“Then stop looking like that.”

Hulk climbed after them. His fists were open now, though his face had lost none of its anger.

“This doesn't settle what you did up there.”

“Daynah knows.”

“And you're not leaving him on a rock.”

“Daynah holding him.”

The baby seized the rim of Kahuhu and tried to bite it.

Metal squealed.

Daynah immediately turned the little hand away from a moving plate.

“Fingers out.”

Hulk held out his arms.

Daynah passed the baby across without hesitation.

“Yours.”

Hulk took the child carefully. It looked at him, looked at Daynah, and began to complain.

Not cry. Complain.

There was a low, furious little rumble in its chest, accompanied by a pointed finger and an expression that suggested it would be reviewing this hospital.

Hulk shifted it higher against his shoulder.

“He wants you.”

“Lots people want Daynah. Most want repairs.”

The baby reached out and pulled a loose strip from Daynah's torn waistcloth.

Daynah caught the other end.

The child pulled harder.

“Leave that. Last fucking piece.”

Hulk freed the tiny fingers one at a time. Daynah retrieved the cloth and tucked it back into place.

“Strong grip,” Hulk said.

“Seen yours.”

“He's got your temper.”

Daynah stared at him across the destroyed coastline.

Hulk looked back without a flicker of amusement.

That was the problem. He meant it.

They climbed out by separate routes, Hulk carrying the baby against his chest, Daynah testing each hold with the hand that had recovered first. Behind them, seawater filled the fresh cleft in the headland. Stone continued to fall into it long after they reached the top.

The quarry was a broken basin. Beyond it, dust hung over the harbor. A warehouse roof had caved inward, and people were carrying someone out beneath a sailcloth. Daynah watched long enough to see the cloth move with the person's breathing.

Then he turned back toward the pavilion.

Hulk followed.

Neither apologized for the ground between them.

At the wrecked terrace, the child pointed at the surviving coffin bottom.

It lay beyond the fallen roof, one end propped on rubble. The underside bracing Daynah had helped improve was still holding the broad boards together. Everything built above it had been torn away.

Hulk set it level with one hand.

The baby leaned toward it.

Daynah said, “No.”

Hulk looked up.

“What?”

“That one.”

“It's flat.”

“Daynah knows.”

“It held you?”

“Briefly.”

Hulk lowered the child onto it.

The base did not sag.

There it was. The proudest moment of Bob's professional career, and he had inconsiderately died before the customer testimonial.

The baby crawled across the boards, stopped at an upright splinter along the edge and grabbed it.

Daynah caught its wrist.

“Sharp.”

He broke off the splinter, rubbed the edge with the flat side of his gauntlet and tested it with his thumb.

Hulk watched.

“You know what you're doing.”

“Wood.”

“I didn't say otherwise.”

“Thinking otherwise.”

Hulk crouched beside the base. “Can he roll off?”

Daynah looked at the open edge.

Then at a straight plank lying beside the roof.

Then back at Hulk.

“Don't.”

“I asked a question.”

Daynah picked up the plank.

Oh, buddy.

Hulk held it while Daynah seated it against the surviving end bracket. Daynah used two recovered fasteners, pressed them through with the edge of his gauntlet, then bent their projecting ends flat underneath. He checked that no sharp metal faced the baby.

He stopped.

Both hands were on the cradle.

The baby was looking up at him.

Hulk was still holding the board exactly where he'd been told.

Daynah released it.

“Finished.”

The baby seized the new rail and pulled.

The wood snapped down the middle.

It looked at the broken piece in its fist.

Then it offered the piece to Daynah.

“Dada.”

Daynah did not take it.

The baby pushed it toward him again, more insistently.

Hulk said, “He brought it to you.”

“Daynah fucking sees.”

The child waved the broken rail. When Daynah kept his arms at his sides, it slapped the piece against the base.

The bottom held.

It hit it again.

Still held.

The baby looked impressed.

Daynah looked betrayed by his own craftsmanship.

He reached down, took the broken piece and placed it beyond the child's reach.

“No more.”

“He's only just started,” Hulk said.

Daynah's shoulders lowered.

Slowly, the pale light around him receded. His body settled back toward its ordinary, still enormous proportions. Kahuhu contracted with it. The wounds remained: raw across his back, closed unevenly along his abdomen, swollen at the mouth. Breathing still hurt.

The baby stared as he became smaller.

Daynah pointed at Hulk.

“There. Bigger one. More father.”

Hulk looked down at the child.

“That's not how that works.”

“Neither was any fucking part of this.”

He returned to the stone where he'd left his shortgun, cleared away the fallen grit and retrieved it. Then he secured it against his remaining waistcloth and stepped away from the cradle.

Hulk stood.

Daynah met his eyes.

“You want to keep fighting, put him somewhere safe first.”

The baby pulled itself up against Hulk's shin.

Hulk looked down, then bent and picked it up.

“Go.”

It was not forgiveness.

Daynah nodded once and went down the surviving side of the lane.

Behind him, the baby called again.

He kept walking.

At the ropewalk, Billy stood beside Arthur with his banjo hanging against his belly. Dust had settled over the damaged Subaru. One warehouse beyond the next lane was shedding its remaining roof tiles while people shouted for space underneath.

Daynah came around the corner with blood drying down his side.

Arthur looked up the hill, then at him.

“You finish helping?”

“Don't call it that.”

Billy leaned sideways to look behind him.

“Who all's comin'?”

“Nobody. Let me in.”

From Billy's banjo came Chris's voice.

“What happened to your face?”

“Hulk.”

A pause.

“Well, that'd do it.”

Billy opened his mouth. Daynah stepped into the offered intake and vanished through the impossible space beyond it. Billy closed his jaw and remained outside beside Arthur.

Inside, Daynah arrived on the dry, living floor, found Chris by the gaming station and sat down heavily beside him.

Chris took in the blood, the split clothing, the gauntlets.

Then he slid a clean cloth across.

“You get him?”

Daynah pressed it against his side.

“Got a kid.”

Chris stopped reaching for the controller.

“You went up there to help a man with a roof.”

“I know where I went.”

“How long was I readin' that letter?”

Daynah held out his free hand.

Chris looked at it, then passed him the controller.

For a little while, the only voices came from the game.

Daynah guided Arthur's horse along a wooded road. When a man called from the grass beside it, clutching a snakebitten leg, he slowed and turned toward him.

Chris watched him dismount.

“Careful.”

“I see him.”

“That's how the last one got you.”

Daynah turned his head.

Chris nodded at the wounded stranger.

“Just one little favor. Next thing you're buyin' shoes for a fist.”

Daynah looked back at the screen. He chose to give the man medicine.

Chris leaned forward.

“That's ours.”

“He's poisoned.”

“I can see that. I ain't disputin' his snake.”

The man drank. Daynah waited until he could speak again, then turned Arthur toward the horse.

Chris pointed at the screen.

“Look at him. Legs work. Hands work. Fella could make a little contribution.”

“You want to rob him?”

“I want him involved in his recovery.”

Daynah snorted. It hurt his mouth. He did it again anyway.

Chris grinned, then tried to recover his grievance.

“Medicine ain't rainwater. We had to get that somewhere.”

“Steal it?”

Chris settled back.

“That's not the part we're discussin'.”

Chris leaned over to examine his swollen jaw, then settled back without touching it.

“You name it?”

“No.”

“Good. Don't name nothin' in here. We got enough residents.”

Meatwad came past with his open cereal box.

He paused at Daynah's knee, looked up at the cloth pressed to his side, and held the box toward him.

Daynah took a handful.

Chris watched the exchange. His hand briefly touched the silent device at his belt, then moved away.

On the screen, the traveler thanked Arthur.

Chris sighed.

“Ask him for money.”

“No.”

“You ain't even got to mean it. Just see what he says.”

Daynah rode away.

Chris looked personally robbed.

“You know what? Fine. Charity. Whole afternoon of it. We can sell the fuckin' couch next.”

Daynah shifted his weight against the cushion, holding the controller comfortably between his hands.

“Couch is staying.”

Chris's elbow rested beside his.

Outside, through the second monitor, Arthur asked Billy whether he thought the hillside was finished moving.

Billy turned toward the dust above the roofs.

“Wouldn't put money on it.”

Daynah reached over and lowered that monitor's volume.

Chris let him.
