EPISODE 80: THE FOUR-BILLION-BRAPPLE VISITOR

(Main Story — Narrator: Wade / Deadpool)

There is a fundamental rule about having a four-billion-Brapple bounty pinned to
your bumper: peace is officially off the menu.

You don’t get to ease the seat back. You don’t get to crack a window and let the
cool solar wind hit your arm. The minute your price tag looks like the gross
domestic product of a medium-sized star system, every apex predator with an ego
and a cape is going to try to take your head off.

Which brings us to the cockpit of the Millennium Falcon, cruising along the
outer edge of Sector Nine.

Chris was sitting at the console with a clean towel laid over his lap. In his
hands was his ceramic bowl, three fresh, glistening Eyeholes floating in blue
milk. He had his eyes closed, his breathing slow and measured, treating the
breakfast like a religious ceremony.

Behind him in the lounge, Kaze was lying on the bench with Boba Fett’s beskar
chestplate still strapped over his hoodie. He was using a butter knife to try
to pry the Mandalorian clan crest off the metal to see if the gold underneath
was real.

“Chris, this metal is stubborn as hell,” Kaze said, flicking the knife. “Go get
that heavy wrench from the back. I bet this badge alone is worth thirty clean
Brapples at a pawn shop.”

“Do not talk to me,” Chris whispered, his voice trembling with restrained venom.
“I am having a moment with my breakfast. If you make me drop one of these
spheres on this dirty carpet, I am going to cut the coolant line and let the
engine fumes take us all in our sleep.”

At the small round table across the room, Daniel sat in deep meditation. Sitting
next to him, slumped against the upholstered wall, was Master Oogway.

The ancient tortoise was holding a dented tin flask he’d stolen from the docking
bay, his head nodding in slow, rhythmic circles. He let out a soft, wine-soaked
burp that smelled like fermented apricots.

“Daniel-san,” Oogway slurred, his ancient lids fluttering. “When the storm
comes… the reed bends with the wind. But when the debt collector comes… the reed
hides in the trunk of the car and turns off his comm-link.”

Daniel nodded slowly, eyes closed. “The path of evasion is deep, Master.”

“Very deep,” Oogway muttered, taking another slow pull from the flask.
“Especially when you owe three months of child support in the spirit world.”

Suddenly, the Falcon groaned.

The sub-light engines sputtered, the deck plates vibrating with a violent
shudder that sent Chris’s spoon clattering against the rim of his bowl.

Through the main viewport, the black void wasn't empty anymore.

Hovering twenty feet outside the cockpit glass, completely naked to the vacuum
of space without a helmet or suit, was Homelander.

His stars-and-stripes cape drifted in the solar current. His blond hair was
combed back, gleaming in the distant starlight, his arms folded neatly across
his chest. He was smiling that smug, dead-eyed, corporate smile—the face of a
man who thought he was showing up to collect an easy four billion Brapples from
a bunch of frightened peasants.

Homelander raised one finger, pointed it downward, and unleashed two searing
beams of red heat from his eyes.

The lasers sliced clean through the Falcon’s upper hull plate above the lounge,
carving a glowing, molten circle in the ceiling.

A split-second later, the cut section of metal collapsed inward. Homelander
dropped through the breach, landing with a heavy, deliberate thud in the center
of the lounge deck, his cape settling behind him as the ship’s emergency shields
hissed to seal the vacuum leak.

He dusted off his shoulders, looking around the grimy Corellian freighter with
undisguised disgust.

“Honestly, it’s pathetic,” Homelander said, his voice dripping with
condescension. “Four point two billion Brapples for a flying tin can filled
with dirt and garbage. Do you people have any idea who I am? You’re looking at
a god. You’re looking at the only man in this quadrant who can do whatever the
hell he wants.”

He took one step forward, puffing his chest out.

Under normal circumstances, villains get to finish their speech. People cower.
People beg for their lives.

The Sins crew didn't give him another second of breath.

Dana was the first to move. He didn't square up or reach for a weapon. He
launched himself off the floor from Homelander’s blind spot, grabbed the hero’s
heavy cape with both hands, and violently yanked it straight over Homelander’s
head, twisting the heavy fabric into a blindfold.

“Get this shiny bitch,” Dana said, dragging down with his full body weight.

Homelander’s eyes flared red beneath the cloth, the heat vision blinding him
and scorching the inside of his own cape, but before he could pull his arms up
to rip it away, Chris arrived.

Chris had completely forgotten about his breakfast. His eyes were wide,
twitching with the unhinged hostility of a man pushed past his breaking point.
He swung a twenty-pound steel hydro-spanner with both hands, driving the blunt
head straight into the back of Homelander’s left knee.

The joint buckled under the kinetic shock.

Daniel closed the distance next, moving with effortless, fluid balance. He
didn't strike for blunt trauma; he drove three rigid fingers directly into the
nerve cluster beneath Homelander’s collarbone, interrupting the flow of energy
to the hero’s shoulder and paralyzing his left arm at the socket.

Homelander let out a muffled, furious scream beneath his tangled cape,
thrashing wildly.

Kaze didn't even drop his blunt.

He stood up, picked up Boba Fett’s heavy iron blaster rifle by the hot barrel,
and began methodically, repeatedly caving in Homelander’s ribs with the solid
beskar stock.

He swung the rifle like an aluminum bat in a parking lot brawl, landing heavy,
dull blows against the supe’s kidney, ribs, and collarbone, completely
unconcerned with proper form.

“Talking all that loud shit in my living room,” Kaze muttered between swings.
“Got your dirty-ass boots on the carpet. Acting like you pay rent.”

From the corner booth, Master Oogway slowly reached his wooden staff across the
floor. He precisely poked Homelander dead-center between the thighs.

Homelander’s body went completely rigid, his eyes bulging beneath the fabric
as all the breath left his lungs in a sharp, wheezing gasp.

Together, the four of them dragged him down into the dirt. Dana held his head,
Daniel pinned his paralyzed shoulder, Chris stomped down on his ankle, and Kaze
stripped the solid gold eagle belt buckle straight off his suit with the
butter knife.

Within twenty seconds, Homelander was on his stomach, battered, wheezing, and
stripped down to his star-spangled boxers, his aura completely dismantled by
four guys who didn't even know his name.

Kaze grabbed him by the hair, dragging his bruised face toward the emergency
airlock hatch.

“Wait,” Homelander wheezed through swollen lips, actual fear creeping into his
eyes for the first time in his existence. “Wait, listen to me, I have
resources, I have Vought—”

Kaze hit the exterior cycle button.

“Tell your boss to send somebody with some manners next time,” Kaze said, and
pushed him through the hatch with the bottom of his slide.

The airlock door slammed shut.

Outside the viewport, Homelander drifted into the silent black void in his
underwear, clutching his bruised ribs, spinning helplessly past an asteroid
field.

Kaze wiped his hands on his shorts, looked down at the gold eagle buckle in his
palm, and nodded.

“Now this,” Kaze said, “is worth at least fifty Brapples.”
