Episode 64 — A Little Feeling

Brian followed the wheel ruts until the wind took them away.

At first they were two shallow grooves beside the dune. Then one groove. Then a smooth slope on which practically anywhere could be the road, an exciting development for a man whose entire water supply had recently exploded against a rock.

He shook the broken container over his mouth.

A grain of sand landed on his tongue.

“Fuck you too.”

Behind him lay the salvage yard. Shade. Water. A gate shaped, in Brian's recent experience, like an exit wound.

He could go back.

He took out the journal instead.

To B. Moo. I loved the wordplay.

“He wouldn't have written that if he didn't mean it.”

Sure. Wade Wilson here, watching a man use a customer testimonial as sunblock. The review is genuine, sweetheart. So is the fucking dehydration.

Brian tucked the book away and climbed toward what looked like the next stretch of road. His shoulder hurt when he swung his arm. The wrist Starkiller had twisted throbbed against the bag strap. He changed hands, slipped, and finished the climb on his knees.

More sand waited on the other side.

He stood there long enough for the horizon to begin moving without him.

“Hello?”

The word scraped out. He tried again, louder, and got less.

Brian turned toward his tracks. Wind was combing their edges flat. He managed three steps downhill before one knee folded. His bag slid beneath him, the journal pressing against his ribs.

He pulled it closer.

Then his elbow gave, and his face settled into the sand.

The Tusken scout found the dragging mark before he found Brian.

He raised a gloved hand. Behind him, two adult Jawas stopped pulling their handcart. Another scout came around its piled scrap, following the first man's gaze toward the small body below the ridge.

One knee opened.

Closed.

Brian's hips lifted.

The scout crouched beside him, checked his breathing, then drew back as Brian attempted another determined little thrust into an environment that had already rejected his application for continued life.

A Jawa chirped.

The second Jawa looked from Brian to the first, pushed his own hips forward once, and received a sharp swat across the shoulder.

The scout pointed downhill toward the water stop.

They got on with it.

The wrapped contents of Brian's bag were checked before they lifted him. The saber's switch brought a pause, a pointed gesture, and another fold of cloth around the hilt. Nobody pressed it to find out. Two people took the blanket beneath Brian, a third kept his head from knocking against the cart, and they rolled him into the narrow shade cast by the load.

His heels began tapping the boards.

The Jawa at the rear tucked the blanket over him.

Brian kicked it away and gyrated.

The Jawa tucked it back.

Again.

By the third attempt, the poor bastard was securing shade cloth to the cart rails while Brian auditioned for a nightclub beneath it. They had rescued a man and accidentally accepted delivery of a haunted massage chair.

Time broke into pieces.

Coolness at his neck. Cloth moving above him. A voice telling him to swallow slowly. Then darkness again, shallower this time, with sound pressing through it.

Brian opened his eyes halfway through a thrust.

Jabba the Hutt was looking directly at him.

Jar Jar Binks stood beside Jabba, holding a cup.

Brian stopped at the top.

His back trembled.

Jabba's eyes moved down to the small bridge of body suspended above the mat, then back to his face.

Brian lowered himself with the care of a bomb technician.

“I was stretching.”

Jar Jar leaned forward. “Yousa awake! Oh, good. Good.”

“The lower back. It's a thing. People neglect it.”

Jabba's laugh rolled under the awning.

Brian sat up, saw the stone wall swing sideways, and nearly emptied the cup into his own ear. Jar Jar caught his shoulder.

“Easy. Yousa been cooking out there.”

Brian drank. His hands shook against the rim.

Past Jar Jar's long ears, patched canvas stretched between a low building and wooden posts. The open side faced a yard of carts, stacked jars and pale stone. Beyond its wall, dunes shimmered beneath the afternoon sun. Jabba occupied a padded platform in the deepest shade, a small chest within reach of one broad hand.

No palace. No orchestra. Somehow he'd still found somewhere to look disappointed in the help.

Brian stared over the cup.

“Holy shit. You're actually Jar Jar.”

“Meesa Jar Jar Binks.”

“I know. Representative Binks. Public service. The whole—I'm a huge—”

Jabba shifted.

“Longtime admirer,” Brian finished, looking firmly at Jar Jar.

The Gungan's ears lifted. “Yousa know how we get home?”

Brian's mouth remained open a moment.

“I know who you are. That's different from knowing where we are in relation to where you were.”

“Oh.”

Jar Jar lowered the cup slightly.

“Meesa keep asking.”

Jabba spoke. Low Huttese, a heavy finger pointing toward the wrapped saber on the slab beside him.

Brian nodded with an expression he evidently hoped meant international business.

Jabba continued.

Brian nodded again.

Jar Jar watched him. “Yousa understand?”

“The broad thrust.”

Jabba laughed harder.

“Of the conversation. Fucking hell.”

He looked to Jar Jar for help. Jar Jar spread his hands.

“Meesa no speak Huttese.”

Jabba's laughter subsided. When he spoke again, the Basic was slow and quite clear.

“Show me the weapon.”

“That's mine.”

“Then show me.”

Brian pushed himself upright. His sore wrist buckled immediately.

Jar Jar set the cup aside and stepped between him and the slab.

“Later.”

Jabba turned his enormous head.

“He can rest afterward.”

“Him already fell down once. Yousa want him falling down with that thing on?”

The Tusken at the awning entrance looked at the bundle. Then he took a deliberate step away from it.

Jar Jar kept his place. His fingers flexed against his own palms, and his voice rose a little when Jabba didn't answer.

“Yousa got all afternoon to be impatient.”

There. A public servant making himself useful by telling a rich asshole to wait. Somebody preserve this footage. It'll never survive the campaign edit.

Jabba exhaled, waved them aside, and addressed the Jawa waiting near his chest.

Jar Jar helped Brian settle against the rolled blanket.

“You just told Jabba the Hutt off.”

“Yousa need sleep.”

“I know who he is.”

Jar Jar glanced back at the platform.

“Meesa starting to.”

When Brian woke again, the strip of sunlight across the mat had moved beyond his feet. His mouth no longer felt lined with old carpet, and he could sit without the roof trying to escape.

Across the awning, Jar Jar was arguing over two tied bundles and a water skin.

“We agree for the bundles. Yousa say water come with.”

Jabba made a small gesture toward the cup beside Brian.

Jar Jar's ears swung forward.

“That water? Him almost died! Yousa counting that?”

The Hutt settled more comfortably into his cushions.

Brian got to his feet.

It took a moment. Neither of them mistook the moment for a dramatic pause, which irritated him enough to finish standing.

“What are we negotiating?”

“Meesa negotiating,” Jar Jar said. “Yousa recovering.”

“I can do both. I've produced entire tracks with a migraine.”

Jar Jar explained anyway. Supplies for other people stranded here. Water for the journey north. A landing where travelers might know more than the people at this stop. Jabba had acquired things Jar Jar needed, and every time they approached an agreement, something formerly included became available separately.

Brian looked at the bundles, then at his saber.

“I can show you the blade.”

Jabba's attention returned at once.

“One demonstration,” Brian continued. “Food. Somewhere to recover. Water. And payment.”

Three silver coins appeared on the slab.

Jabba put a finger on the wrapped hilt.

Brian put his hand on the other end.

“That's not the price of the weapon.”

The finger stayed where it was.

Brian's hand was small enough to disappear beneath it. He kept his grip.

“I said demonstration.”

Jabba studied him, then withdrew the finger and drew the coins back toward his chest.

“Where did you get it?”

Brian swallowed.

“Doctor Evil developed it. He has unusual equipment. Submarine. Workshops.”

“Where?”

“Noxian coast. Old export depot beside the outer berth. Ropewalk along the approach. That's where I left them. I'm not telling you they're standing still waiting for a fucking brochure.”

Jabba tapped one coin.

“Show me.”

Brian described the approach again, using a crack in the slab for the shoreline. He could name the berth and show where the submarine had been moored. He couldn't promise its present location or sell equipment he didn't own.

Jabba asked about the weapon twice. Brian answered the maker question twice and did not answer the question the Hutt hadn't quite asked about how it had become his.

“You return to this doctor,” Jabba said.

“No.”

The answer came out before Brian could dress it up.

Jabba watched him.

“You left without permission.”

“I left without a fucking farewell cake. There's a difference.”

“And he will welcome you back?”

Brian looked at the three coins.

“You want directions, I've got directions. You want someone to knock on his door smiling, that's a different service, and I'm not offering it.”

Jar Jar stopped fussing with the water skin.

Brian kept his eyes on Jabba.

The Hutt slowly smiled.

“Then bring me something else worth knowing.”

He tapped his chest of valuables. The lid closed with a soft, solid click.

Brian's gaze followed it.

“We can discuss future opportunities.”

“After this one.”

I know that smile. I've worn that smile. Usually while somebody explains that the exposure will more than cover the missing money. Jabba, to his credit, had actual coins. To his considerable discredit, he was Jabba.

Finally, the three coins slid across the stone.

Food, water, a place beneath the awning while he recovered. The demonstration and the useful coastal lead in return. No sale of the hilt.

Brian took the money.

“Now we're communicating.”

Jabba pointed toward the yard.

Outside, a Jawa was hammering a bent iron strap against a low stone block. Nearby, the loaded handcart stood on a slight slope, one wheel held by a square wooden chock. Jar Jar examined the strap, then brought it to Brian.

“Yousa cut this, him can finish repair. Then we move supplies.”

The Jawa indicated two chalk marks and placed the iron flat. A short damaged section lay between them.

Brian crouched to examine it.

“That's doable.”

The scout cleared people from the block. Jar Jar moved to the cart, where the rope securing its load had worked loose. Jabba watched from beneath the awning, close enough to see the weapon, comfortably beyond anything resembling an obligation to help.

Brian unwrapped the hilt.

The Jawa leaned forward.

“Give me space, brother. You're about to see why somebody signed my book.”

He placed his feet, lifted the hilt in both hands, and ignited it.

Red light snapped across the stone.

The Jawa recoiled. At the entrance, Jabba's eyes narrowed against the glare.

Brian lowered the blade through the first mark. Iron hissed. The cut glowed orange around a white center, and a thin ribbon of smoke twisted past his fingers. He withdrew, shifted his stance, and made the second cut without rushing it.

The damaged piece fell clear and rang against the block.

Brian switched off before turning.

The Jawa bent over the result, caught himself reaching for the hot edge, and changed hands to a pair of tongs.

“Yeah,” Brian said softly. “That'll fuckin' do it.”

No flourish. He stood there smiling at the two clean cuts.

Jar Jar looked over from the cart. “Good! Now him can—”

“One second.”

Brian's gaze had found Jabba.

From beneath the awning, the Hutt could see the blade demonstration perfectly well. What he could not see, Brian evidently decided, was enough Brian.

Brian spotted the square block under the cart wheel.

“I just need a little elevation.”

He pointed to the rope in Jar Jar's hands. “You got it?”

“Meesa got rope, but—”

“Perfect.”

Jar Jar turned fully.

“No, that keeping—”

Brian pulled.

The wheel rolled over his fingertips.

“FUCK!”

He jerked his hand free. The cart moved another inch, then another, gathering weight down the slope toward the open gate and the stone drainage drop beyond it.

Jar Jar grabbed the side rail.

His feet slid.

The Jawa lunged for the handle, missed, and scrambled out of the wheel's path. A tied jar knocked against the next one with a deep, hollow clunk.

Brian ran after his new stage.

“Stop it! Stop—I'm helping!”

“Put the block back!”

He shoved it toward the approaching wheel. It struck at an angle and spun away across the stones.

Jar Jar had one hand on the rail and the loose load rope in the other. The gatepost passed on his left. He flung the rope around it, caught the swinging end against his chest, and tried to pull it tight.

The cart dragged him forward.

He hit the post shoulder-first. One long leg hooked around it; the other scraped for purchase as the rope burned through his hands.

The nearest Tusken seized the free end.

Together they hauled.

The cart swung sideways. Its outer wheel struck the gate's stone base hard enough to chip the corner. A water jar toppled against the rail, rocking outward above the drop.

Brian reached for it and remembered, far too late, that his arms did not come with an extension cable.

Jar Jar stretched over the rail, caught the jar's binding, and dragged it back while the scout held the rope.

The jar slammed into the bed.

Everything stopped.

For a moment all I could hear was rope creaking and Brian breathing through his teeth.

Jar Jar remained wrapped around the gatepost.

“Yousa,” he said, “leave that little block where it belongs.”

“You said you had it.”

Jar Jar looked at the wheel. Then at Brian. Then, with tremendous restraint, at the sky.

“Meesa had ROPE. Rope tied around jars. Yousa want jars pull cart back uphill?”

The Jawa retrieved the chock, shoved it firmly into place, and slapped Brian's reaching hand away.

Brian looked down at his fingers. Red, sore, still moving. His saber remained dark, gripped against his chest throughout the chase.

“I could've cut something.”

“Meesa glad yousa didn't.”

Jabba laughed from the shade.

He held out one hand toward the hilt.

Brian tucked it behind his hip.

“Demonstration's over.”

The hand waited.

Brian waited too.

Jabba let it fall onto his cushion, then pointed at the cart and addressed Jar Jar in Basic.

“My supplies arrive intact.”

Jar Jar unwound his leg from the post.

“They yours when them rolling away. They mine when meesa pay for them.”

“When you pay.”

Jar Jar rubbed his palms, looked at the repaired iron in the Jawa's tongs, and pointed back at the bundles.

“Meesa finish moving these. Meesa help load north travelers tomorrow. Water skin stays full when we leave. No little cup surprise.”

The Hutt considered him. Then he nodded once.

Jar Jar stayed until the water skin was placed beside his bundles.

Brian helped reset the load. Nobody offered him a raised position.

Jar Jar tried to knot the rope with his scraped palms and hissed through his teeth.

Brian put the saber away.

“Let me.”

The Gungan hesitated, then lowered the loose ends. Brian could reach them now. He threaded one around the rail, doubled it back through the loop already there, and pulled until the load stopped shifting.

“Pull from there,” he said, pointing. “Not across the raw part.”

Jar Jar tightened the knot with his fingers.

It held.

“Thank you.”

Brian rubbed his own sore hand against his trouser leg.

“I've tied down equipment.”

The Jawa pointed at the wheel.

“Yes, I understand the fucking block now.”

He stayed while they finished. When the cart needed moving the last few feet, he waited until both scouts had the handles before touching the chock.

By sunset, the cart stood level against the wall, its repaired fitting secured. Brian sat beside it with the signed journal on his knees. Jar Jar finished checking the lashings, shook grit from an ear, and caught him watching.

“What?”

“You're quick.”

“Cart was quicker.”

Brian snorted despite himself.

Then he opened the journal.

“You want to hear what I actually do?”

Jar Jar sat on the stone edge. “Meesa listening.”

Brian tapped four beats against the cover. Jabba, still within earshot, lifted his head at the sound.

“This is the opening. It's personal history, but there's layers.”

He began.

Brian kept his rhythm.

“Somebody cut off yousa head?”

“There's a lot of autobiography in the work.”

“Last I checked. Nearly wasn't earlier. Thank you for that, by the way.”

Jar Jar's expression softened.

Brian tapped the page. “Hip. Head. Waist. See how it's all connected?”

“It wasn't connected when him cut—”

“The WORDS, Jar Jar. I'm talking about the words.”

Jabba made a low noise and spoke in Basic without leaving his cushions.

“You were paid to show the weapon.”

“This is complimentary.”

“It sounds expensive.”

Brian shut his mouth, opened it again, then turned his entire body toward Jar Jar.

“Some people only value destruction. I build things too.”

From the gate, the Jawa held up the wooden chock.

Brian pointed at him without looking away from Jar Jar.

“That was one fucking incident.”

Jar Jar reached toward the book. “Yousa write all this?”

“Yeah. And look who—”

Brian turned to the autograph.

His eyes hit the lines directly above it.

And not retards like Jar Jar.

More like ree-tar-tar.

His palm came down over them.

Jar Jar paused.

“Who?”

“Starkiller. He loved the wordplay.”

Brian lifted one finger to expose the compliment. The bottom of Jar Jar's name appeared with it. He slapped the finger down again.

“It's smudged.”

He tried folding the page. Hid the signature. Unfolded it, covered the insult with his forearm, and twisted the journal so far sideways Jar Jar had to lean off the stone to look.

Jar Jar steadied himself against the cart.

“Meesa see him loved the wordplay. Yousa can put it down now.”

Brian held the pose another second, then closed the book with both hands.

Jar Jar got up to check his supplies. Brian waited until his back was turned and scratched a line through the insult.

Above it he wrote misunderstood representative.

He mouthed the replacement.

Tried fitting it to the beat.

Stopped.

“Motherfucker.”

Another line went through that too. He considered tearing out the page, but Starkiller's signature occupied the bottom. Brian pressed the paper flat and shut the book.

Across the yard, one of the Jawas was showing the other how they'd found him. Small invisible body. Knees apart. One solemn pelvic thrust.

The listener folded over laughing.

Brian looked up. Both adults abruptly inspected the repaired cart.

“Glad somebody's happy with the workmanship.”

Jar Jar returned carrying his bag and found Brian's journal tucked beside it.

“Meesa leaving in few days.”

“I heard. North landing.”

“Yousa got another place to go?”

“I know the way from the salvage yard. There are actual signs after you get past it. I can show you.”

Brian picked up the journal and put it back beside Jar Jar's bag, closer this time.

“Yousa leave the blocks.”

Brian laughed. A real little burst, surprised out of him.

“Okay. That's fair.”

Night cooled the stones. Beyond the awning, a small fire burned inside a ring of rocks, its light lifting and falling against the wall.

Brian took the folded poster from his bag.

He carried it to the fire and opened it across his knees. Busta Rhymes looked past him from paper worn soft along the creases. Brian smoothed one corner, then the other.

“We need to talk.”

He waited.

From the awning, Jar Jar called good night.

Brian looked back. The Gungan was settling beside his packed supplies.

“See? He's here.”

The poster shifted in the breeze. Brian caught its edge.

“You can't keep doing this thing where I'm talking and you're looking over my shoulder.”

He turned the paper toward himself.

“I'm trying to be honest with you.”

For a while, only the fire answered.

Brian's thumb moved along the crease across Busta's shirt.

“He asked me to come with him.”

He waited again, eyes fixed on the printed face.

“Busta… I don't know what to say… It's not you, it's me. Just know it's not personal and you'll always be my home and rhyme whenever I busta nusta in my sleep. This is just life, you know? People grow… apart and… I'm sorry, babe.”

Nothing.

Brian swallowed.

“I know you're hurt.”

He brought the poster to his mouth and kissed it. His eyes closed against the cold paper.

Then he held it over the fire and let go.

The lower edge caught. Flame ran up a fold, curled the shirt inward and opened a black seam through the printed face.

Brian reached forward.

The paper collapsed into the coals.

His knees struck the ground.

He threw both hands toward the sky.

“NOOOOOOOOO!”

Little tears ran down his puny face.

The fire bent in the wind, and the last corner burned.
