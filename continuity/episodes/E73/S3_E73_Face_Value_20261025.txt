Season 3, Episode 73 — Face Value

Chris turned the box around.

Kaze turned it back.

Chris looked at him.

“Front faces out.”

“I was reading it.”

“You weren't reading shit.”

Kaze put it on its side.

Chris picked it up.

There was his face, printed on the front of an unopened box of Eyeholes. Brown hair. Scruffy beard. Little amber headlamp. A smile so broad it looked as though somebody had promised him a tax-free graveyard.

He had found it beneath the top layer of cartons in the enormous delivery. Same cereal. New representative.

No photographer had asked him to smile.

That part bothered him until he looked at the picture again.

“They got the beard pretty good.”

Kaze laughed.

Billy had admitted him and Mahnah a few minutes earlier. Kaze had come to see Daynah. He'd found Daynah playing Red Dead and Chris arranging breakfast propaganda.

“You like it.”

“It's accurate.”

“They've got you smiling like you're about to eat the fucking customer.”

Chris inspected the mouth.

“That's enthusiasm.”

Daynah kept his eyes on Arthur's horse.

“Put it somewhere I can see around it.”

Chris moved the box away from the game screen and stood it beside the exterior monitor.

Mahnah's nose came over his shoulder.

Chris held the carton closer to his chest.

“No.”

The dog sniffed the top seam. His warm breath moved Chris's hair.

“You can smell the cereal. You cannot have my face.”

Mahnah took the loose cardboard flap between his front teeth.

Chris clamped both hands around the box.

“Let go.”

Mahnah let go.

The corner came with him.

Chris stared at the small crescent missing from his printed forehead.

Kaze bent over laughing.

“He got your good side.”

Mahnah dropped the cardboard, found a cereal crumb near the couch and ate that instead.

Chris checked the inner bag. Still sealed.

“Fine. You get this one.”

He set the damaged carton down, opened it and poured a little into a bowl for the dog. Then he went back to the giant delivery and selected a second box.

Daynah glanced over.

“Nobody inside gets beaten.”

“I know.”

“Just checking. You looked hurt.”

“He bit a hole in my head.”

“The good one's still on your shoulders.”

Chris stood the replacement front-facing.

Kaze reached for it.

Chris caught his wrist.

They looked at each other.

Kaze's grin widened.

“Oh, you fucking love it.”

A little later, Billy let Kaze back outside carrying an untouched carton. Mahnah stayed stretched near Daynah's feet. Chris's replacement remained beside the monitor.

On the ropewalk, Arthur accepted the box from Kaze and studied it beneath a harbor lamp.

“Well,” he said. “They found you.”

Chris answered through Billy's banjo.

“That's what I'm saying. How'd they get the picture?”

Arthur turned the carton over.

“How much they paying?”

“Lifetime supply.”

Arthur looked up.

“Of this?”

“Yeah.”

He handed it back.

“Best hope you don't get tired of it.”

Billy leaned down to admire the portrait.

“A handsome likeness. They have captured the precise moment before you begin explaining what is wrong with dinner.”

Chris paused.

“Thank you.”

“I intended about half of that warmly.”

Brian was coming up from the landing office when Kaze held the carton out to him.

“Free one.”

Brian stopped beneath it.

“Why is Chris on it?”

“He's famous now.”

Chris's voice came from the banjo.

“Do not bend the front.”

Brian took the box with both hands. It covered most of his torso.

He looked from the portrait to Kaze.

“I've been building an identity for years.”

“He killed a guy,” Kaze said.

Brian's eyes narrowed.

“That's not a transferable career strategy.”

I would like that objection entered into evidence beside the corpse of Master Shake.

Brian couldn't hear me. Still an excellent arrangement for Brian.

He carried the cereal down the lane toward Jar Jar, who was waiting with the key to their room above the landing office.

Inside Billy, Chris watched him go.

“You gave him my cereal.”

Kaze looked into Billy's hat aperture.

“Sealed.”

Chris glanced at the silent device on his belt.

Kaze's smile told him exactly how much thought had gone into the gift.

“You're a fucking asshole.”

“Go play your game.”

Behind the office, a little animal was trying to eat a flower through a broken slat.

It had the rounded, striped body of a bumblebee, six busy legs, and a tiny cow's head. Two horns caught the lamplight. Its broad pink nose pushed between the timbers while its wings made a furious electrical hum.

The flower was outside the crate.

The animal was working on that.

Brian stopped beside it.

Jar Jar had paid the clerk with one of Brian's three silver coins. The room came with water and bedding. Brian had objected to the price until Jar Jar began handing the key back.

They still had the room.

Now Jar Jar wanted to ask the departing boat crews about a route home. Brian wanted him to look at a bee with a cow on it.

“That's not an accident,” Brian said.

Jar Jar looked at the creature.

“Mesa think somebody made a mistake.”

Brian crouched. He set the cereal on the dry step and laid his journal on top, safely clear of the animal.

A metal placard hung from the crate's end. Jar Jar pulled it around.

“Livestock pollination trial,” he read slowly.

Below that, in larger letters, was a word Brian recognized without assistance.

Radioactive.

“Don't touch it,” Jar Jar said.

Brian extended one finger toward the nose.

“I'm not touching it. I'm establishing—”

The cow licked him.

Brian smiled.

“See?”

The broken slat slipped. The animal squeezed through, wings blurring, and landed on the flower.

It began eating the whole blossom.

Brian put a hand beneath it.

“Bee. Moo. You understand the significance?”

Jar Jar folded his long arms.

“Yousa name doesn't make it yours.”

“I didn't say it did.”

Brian lifted the animal away from the flower.

It bit him.

Not a sting. Its little cow mouth opened around the fleshy edge of his hand and clamped down with the determination of a stapler discovering religion.

Brian shrieked and shook his arm.

The wings buzzed against his wrist.

Jar Jar seized the loose flower stem and pushed it toward the animal's nose. The creature released Brian, took the flower and dropped onto the step.

Blood ran between Brian's fingers.

He reached for the wrapped saber at his side.

Jar Jar caught his forearm.

“No. People here.”

The animal chewed.

Brian glared at it.

Jar Jar set an empty basket over it, slid a broad board beneath and carried it back to the crate. He braced the broken slat with the board and wedged the basket against the gap.

The humming continued.

So did the chewing.

“It bit me deliberately,” Brian said.

Jar Jar looked at the blood, then at the flower.

“Yousa picked it up deliberately.”

“Those aren't equivalent actions.”

“Come wash that.”

The room upstairs overlooked the lower quay. One bed, one narrow cot, a washstand and a broad window above a shallow stone balcony. A table stood between the window and the door.

Jar Jar took the cot without an argument. Brian placed his journal beside the bed, the wrapped saber within reach and the cereal upright on the table. Chris's printed smile faced the room.

Brian turned it toward the wall.

Over the next two hours, the bite swelled, the swelling receded, and the rest of him began making up for it.

He grew hot, then cold. He pulled the blanket over himself, kicked it away and accused the room of changing temperature.

Jar Jar brought water and informed the clerk about the animal before returning to find Brian standing on the bed.

“What are you doing?”

“Testing something.”

Brian jumped.

He landed on the mattress.

Nothing else happened.

He looked disappointed.

Jar Jar put the water down.

“Test sitting.”

Brian sat.

A moment later he leaned forward, both hands gripping his knees.

Something moved beneath the cloth across his back.

His breath stopped.

Two ridges rose between his shoulder blades. The fabric pulled tight. Brian clawed at the collar, trying to see over his own shoulder.

“Get it off.”

Jar Jar helped him loosen the clothing. The seams split before they finished.

Four folded wings pushed through, wet and trembling, then slowly opened into the lamplight.

Brian sat very still.

The translucent membranes quivered.

Jar Jar stepped back far enough to let them spread.

“Oh.”

Brian turned toward the mirror above the washstand.

“Help me up.”

Jar Jar moved a chair into place. Brian climbed it himself, holding the back until his knees stopped shaking.

White patches had appeared across his forearms, broken by irregular black markings. Fine yellow fuzz thickened along his shoulders and upper torso. Small horns curved above his temples.

Same face.

Same little hands.

Same two feet two inches of man, now with considerably more to explain at a tailor's.

Brian touched one horn.

He touched the other.

His expression changed.

Jar Jar recognized that expression and immediately regretted giving him access to a mirror.

“It's happening,” Brian whispered.

“Yes. Mesa can see.”

“No. You don't understand.”

The wings jerked open.

Jar Jar ducked as one swept past his face.

“Mesa understanding from farther away.”

Brian looked down at his hand. The bite marks remained, but he could close his fingers. He flexed them again, then lifted the wrapped hilt from beside the bed.

His grip still fit.

He did not ignite it.

For several seconds he simply stood there holding it, staring at the impossible reflection.

Then he raised his chin.

“Bee Moo.”

Jar Jar waited.

“With bee.”

“Mesa heard.”

“A Sith. A transformation. A whole new—”

His wings started beating.

The sound swallowed the rest of the announcement.

Brian's feet left the chair.

Both of them froze.

He rose another few inches.

His face lit with genuine amazement.

“I'm doing it.”

Jar Jar smiled despite himself.

“Yousa doing it!”

Brian crossed the gap from the chair to the bed. He wobbled, corrected and landed on both feet without dropping the saber.

It had worked.

The buzzing stopped. He was breathing hard.

He looked back at the distance he'd crossed.

Then he looked at Jar Jar.

“Did you see that?”

“Mesa was here.”

Brian laughed. Not a speech. Just a startled, delighted laugh.

He tried again after resting, made it halfway back and had to catch the chair before his knees hit the floor.

That result received less publicity.

He returned the saber to the bedside, sat down and drank the water.

Downstairs, someone called for help shifting a bundle away from the loading doors. Jar Jar checked that Brian was steady, then picked up their empty water jug.

“Mesa going down. Those boat people leaving soon.”

Brian was watching one wing move in the mirror.

“Tell them to wait.”

Jar Jar stopped at the door.

“Yousa can wait.”

He left.

Brian waited until the footsteps had reached the bottom of the stairs before standing in front of the mirror again.

He spread his wings slowly this time. Turned sideways. Practiced holding the hilt where it didn't knock against his knee.

The first pose looked uncomfortable because it was. He adjusted it.

Better.

His stomach growled.

Brian looked at Chris's face on the cereal box, then rotated it away and pulled the top open.

The inner bag resisted his fingers. He tried his teeth, stopped before his new horns struck the table and finally tore the seam with both hands.

He could fly.

Breakfast still required the old technique.

Brian ate a handful standing beside the bed, the intact saber within reach. The wings settled against his torn clothing.

Inside Billy, Daynah made Arthur put a stolen purse back into its owner's hand.

Chris sat forward.

“We caught the thief.”

“Yes.”

“That's the difficult part.”

“Then this should be easy for you to watch.”

The woman thanked Arthur. Daynah walked away before Chris could suggest a fee.

Chris looked at his cereal portrait.

“Funny how everybody expects free work now.”

Daynah smiled.

The device on Chris's belt began to beep.

He looked down.

The arrow had shifted toward the landing office.

On the exterior monitor, its upper window glowed above the quay.

Chris stood.

Daynah glanced at the device, then at him.

“You don't have to.”

“Says I do.”

“You want to.”

Chris buckled on his revolvers, settled the Critic into its sling and fitted his mask headgear.

“I'll be back. Keep playing.”

“I was going to.”

Chris opened the banjo route.

“Billy. Let me out.”

Billy's voice reached the monitor.

“Our indoor arrangement remains intact, I trust.”

“Yes.”

“Then do try to return without a new roommate.”

Billy rose in the cleared space beside the ropewalk. The jaw opened, flesh rolled inward, and Chris pulled through into the night. Billy passed inside, full-sized, with his banjo.

Daynah didn't lose the picture on his game.

Outside, Kaze looked from Chris's device to his face.

“Already?”

Chris pointed at him.

“You knew.”

Kaze laughed and stepped clear of the Subaru.

R2 was beside its passenger door. He gave a sharp whistle as Chris approached, then rolled forward to join him. Chris opened the door and helped guide him into the supported passenger cradle.

“Short trip.”

R2 answered with a descending electronic complaint.

“Same to you.”

Chris drove into the open loading apron. The damaged mirror mount stayed bare. Starred glazing caught the lamps as the blue body unfolded, road controls rearranging beneath his hands.

Gold-edged wings extended. R2's cradle carried him continuously aft and locked into the socket behind the cockpit.

The X-wing rose above the broken lane.

It did not need to go far.

Brian heard the engines while reaching for a second handful of cereal.

His wings lifted.

Outside the window, a blue-and-gold starfighter passed across the lamps and settled onto the empty stone apron below.

Brian looked at the box.

Chris smiled back.

“Oh, come on.”

He reached for the saber.

Chris left R2 with the powered-down craft, crossed the apron and took the outside stairs two at a time. The device's beeping quickened at the upper door.

He kicked beside the latch.

The door struck the table and stopped.

Brian had shoved it into the opening.

A red blade burned through the tabletop from the other side.

Chris pulled back as it swept toward his legs.

The severed edge crashed onto the landing.

Inside, Brian backed away with the saber raised and the cereal hugged under his other arm.

“You can't just—”

Chris drove the broken door forward. Its lower corner struck Brian's shin. Brian stumbled sideways, cut through a hinge and sent the whole panel falling between them.

He had cleared his weapon's path.

Chris saw that and stopped advancing.

The mask locked over his face.

“Give me my fucking Eyeholes.”

Brian's wings shivered.

“Kaze gave me these.”

“He gave you the box.”

“What did you think was in it?”

Brian lunged as he finished the question.

Chris stepped outside the blade's line. Heat brushed his trouser leg.

Brian pulled the weapon back behind the broken table edge. The blade vanished.

Chris moved for his wrist.

The dead hilt passed beneath the obstruction. Red light returned on the other side, angling upward toward Chris's reaching arm.

Chris jerked away. The edge scored his outer forearm before he cleared it, leaving a hot line through skin. His hand closed on the cereal box instead of the weapon.

Brian's grin appeared for one dangerous instant.

He had remembered the timing.

Chris yanked the carton sideways before he could admire it further.

Brian held on.

Cardboard tore. Cereal struck the floor between their feet.

“Look what you did!” Chris snapped.

Brian let go of the ruined carton and shoved his free hand out.

The Force hit Chris squarely.

His feet left the floor. He struck the washstand with his hip, broke its rear legs and went down beneath the falling basin.

Water burst across the boards.

The mask saved his face from the basin's rim. His ribs met the wooden frame without that courtesy.

Brian had room to run.

He took it.

His wings roared, lifting him over the fallen door toward the balcony window.

Chris rolled away from the next cut. The red blade split a bedpost, dropping the mattress corner into Brian's flight path.

Brian banked over it, landed on the sill and turned.

Beyond the rail was a drop to stone, then black harbor water. His first attempts at flight had barely crossed a room. He tried to lift again, felt the shaking pull between his shoulders and caught the frame instead.

His journal was still behind Chris.

Brian planted both feet on the sill. He wasn't leaving it there.

Chris was already up.

Blood gathered along the burned cut on his forearm. He kept the broken table between himself and the saber.

“You got a whole cow on you,” he said, breathing hard, “and you're still stealing breakfast.”

Brian raised the blade.

“I'm not stealing anything.”

Chris shoved the table.

Brian cut through the approaching corner, but the severed piece kept moving. It struck his chest and knocked him backward onto the balcony.

He hit the stone hard enough to lose his breath.

The saber stayed in his hand.

Chris couldn't reach him without crossing its sweep.

Brian slashed at the window frame. Burning splinters sprayed into the room. Chris ducked behind the wall as the blade tore through the space his throat had occupied.

He could draw and shoot.

Both revolvers were where they belonged.

He wanted Brian beaten, and he wanted his cereal back. Blowing holes through the new cow was not the particular job he had come to enjoy.

He seized the fallen blanket and threw it across the window.

Brian cut it in half.

Chris came through behind the dropping cloth, low and close. One hand clamped Brian's saber wrist and drove it outward against the stone rail. The blade pointed over the quay.

Brian struck at Chris's face with his free hand.

Chris took the small fist on the mask, planted his boot outside Brian's ankle and slammed him against the wall.

The impact emptied Brian's lungs.

His wings beat wildly against the stone.

Chris kept the weapon arm extended, turned the wrist until the thumb lifted and wrenched the hilt free. He shut the blade down and threw the intact weapon through the window onto the bed.

Brian drove both feet into his stomach.

Chris folded enough for Brian to slip one shoulder free.

The wings caught air.

For half a second Brian rose between them.

Chris caught him around the waist and hauled him back through the window.

They hit the room together.

Brian struck the floor first. The remaining table leg collapsed beneath Chris's knee. Cereal jumped across the boards.

Chris punched him in the mouth.

Brian's head snapped sideways. Blood dotted the floor and the white patch on his cheek.

He tried to turn toward the saber.

Chris dragged him away from the bed and hit him again.

Jar Jar reached the doorway with the water jug still in his hand.

For an instant he took in the broken furniture, the burning frame and Brian underneath Chris.

Then he threw the water across the smoldering wood and drove the empty jug into Chris's shoulder.

Chris lurched sideways.

Brian crawled clear of his knee.

Jar Jar pulled him toward the bed, putting his own long body between them.

“Enough!”

Chris caught Jar Jar's forearm when he reached again.

“Move.”

“No!”

Brian hooked the loose table fragment with his foot and kicked it past Jar Jar toward Chris's legs.

Chris stumbled, caught the bedframe and drove forward again. Jar Jar shoved Brian aside as the remaining upright took the impact. Wood cracked. Brian rolled into the gap beneath the fallen mattress instead of taking the blow against his skull.

Chris stopped with a fistful of torn bedding.

Brian was down, blood running over his lips. One wing had folded beneath his shoulder at a bad angle. He pulled it free with a sharp cry and kept it close to his back.

Jar Jar seized the cereal box from the floor.

“This?”

Chris looked at it.

Jar Jar thrust it against his chest.

“Take it. Get out.”

Chris gripped the torn carton.

The room was quiet enough to hear individual pieces of cereal falling through its split seam.

He folded the bottom closed.

Jar Jar didn't move aside.

Chris looked past him at Brian, then down at the spilled Eyeholes.

“I said give them back.”

Brian spat blood onto the boards.

“You fucking gave them to me.”

Chris bent, collected the inner bag's torn remainder and pushed it into the carton. He left the wet cereal where the basin had spilled.

Chef standards. Finally arriving at the crime scene, just a little behind everyone else.

Jar Jar pointed at the door.

Chris went.

At the threshold, he looked back once.

“Don't open another one.”

Jar Jar hurled the empty jug.

Chris ducked. It shattered against the landing wall.

That concluded the customer feedback portion.

Jar Jar dragged the fallen door panel across the opening and knelt beside Brian.

The saber lay intact on the mattress. The journal had slipped behind the bed, its cover wet along one edge. Jar Jar retrieved it and placed it beside the weapon.

Brian reached for the book first.

His fingers shook too badly to open it.

Jar Jar took a clean strip from the torn sheet and held it out.

“Mouth.”

Brian pressed it against his split lip.

His face had begun swelling. When he tried to spread his wings, the right pair trembled and stayed down.

He stopped trying.

Jar Jar sat beside him, breathing hard, listening to the engines outside.

“Mesa liked the flying,” he said.

Brian looked at him.

“Before.”

Brian swallowed blood and nodded once.

Below, Chris climbed into the cockpit with the damaged carton. R2 turned his dome, looked at the blood on Chris's arm and gave a low, disapproving warble.

“Don't start.”

R2 made it longer.

Chris secured the box and took the craft back over the broken lane. No weapons fired. No city disappeared. A man had used a starfighter to retrieve approximately half a breakfast and leave the rest stuck to somebody's floor.

Back on the loading apron, the wings folded. R2's cradle returned him to the passenger position. Chris helped him out before leaving the damaged Subaru where the ground was clear.

Billy took the exterior again. Chris passed inside carrying the box and his new bruises.

Daynah was still playing.

He glanced at the blood, then at the carton.

“Get paid?”

Chris set it down.

A few damp pieces fell out.

Daynah looked back at the game.

Chris went for a cloth.

When he returned, he turned his undamaged portrait toward the room.

Daynah moved Arthur out of the path of an approaching wagon.

“So that one's the before picture.”

Chris sat beside him with the cloth pressed to his arm.

Chris inspected the smiling face on the box.

“They haven't paid for an after picture.”

He straightened the carton, front facing out, and reached for the clean end of the cloth.
