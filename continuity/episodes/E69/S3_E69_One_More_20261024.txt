Season 3, Episode 69 — One More

Back at the ruined exercise pavilion, Daynah was trying to leave without fixing anything else.

He had made it three steps.

Behind him, a loose board tipped under Bob's good hand. The builder made a small, wet noise through his broken nose.

Daynah stopped.

“Don't put weight there.”

Bob moved his hand.

“Not there either.”

A man in striped shorts came up the harbor lane carrying a rolled exercise mat beneath each arm and a canvas bag slung across his back. He had a bright sleeveless shirt, a crown of brown curls and the expression of somebody arriving early to make everybody else's morning worse.

It wasn't morning. Richard Simmons apparently kept a reserve.

He stopped at the ruined doorway.

One mat rolled from beneath his arm.

“Oh, my God.”

Daynah pointed at Bob.

“Ask him.”

Richard looked at the injured builder, the broken bench, the roof resting on a stack of timber. Then he looked at the enormous coffin with its side punched out.

“You put him in that?”

“He put me in it.”

Richard took in Daynah's height.

“And you got in?”

“The bottom wasn't right.”

For a few seconds Richard said nothing.

Daynah could hear how it sounded. That did not make the bottom any less fucking wrong.

Richard set down the other mat and his bag, then crouched beside Bob. He asked where it hurt. Bob raised his damaged hammer hand, then seemed to remember the rest of himself.

Richard's face tightened.

“This was going to be a place where people could feel good about their bodies.”

“Then you picked a bad man to put a roof over 'em.”

“You destroyed it.”

“Saved the part heading for the road.”

Daynah indicated the heavy roof frame. Richard followed his gesture to the stacked timber beneath it.

“You want credit?”

“No. I want you looking at the right fucking thing.”

Richard reached into his bag and pulled out a clean towel. He folded it around Bob's injured hand without trying to straighten it.

“Hold that against you.”

Bob obeyed. Richard eased him farther from the broken steps, leaving him seated against the broad stone support.

Daynah moved a splintered plank out of their way with his foot.

Richard saw him do it.

“You know how to be careful.”

“Yeah. That's why I'm still here.”

“You could have stopped.”

Daynah pointed at the coffin again.

Bob stared very hard at his towel.

Richard looked inside the box. His fingertips passed over the shattered lid, the heavy braces beneath the bottom, the marks where Daynah had punched out.

He looked back at Bob.

“You built that during a fight?”

Bob nodded once.

“And the handrails weren't finished?”

Daynah spread one massive hand toward Richard, delighted despite himself.

“There. That. You're nearly there.”

Richard stood, stepped around a broken upright and approached him. He didn't fetch a weapon. He came with both hands open, surveying Daynah's shoulders and planted feet as though the problem was obvious.

“You're carrying a lot of tension.”

“Carried a roof. Put it down. Keep up.”

“Show me how you move.”

Daynah stared at him.

Richard pointed toward the unbroken patch of platform.

“Just a step. Across and back. Let me see where you're putting your weight.”

“You think I don't know where my feet are?”

“I think you keep turning your whole body to tell me you're leaving.”

Daynah glanced down. A broken plank lay across his route, the jagged end pointing toward his ankle.

He moved around it with one short, clean step. His shoulders barely shifted.

Richard smiled.

“There. Much easier.”

Daynah's face changed.

“That wasn't you.”

“I didn't say it was.”

“You're standing there like you did it.”

Richard demonstrated the same step, light on the balls of his feet. He moved around the plank, reversed and finished facing Daynah without crossing his legs.

“Now try it with me.”

“Just did it without you.”

“And you can do it again.”

The fucking confidence. This man would stand beside a sunrise and tell the sun he was proud of it for getting up.

Daynah pointed past him.

“Out. That's how.”

He turned toward the lane.

Richard stepped across the opening.

“Not through Bob.”

Bob wasn't in the opening. Bob was six feet to the left, concentrating on having a face.

Daynah pointed at the clear ground between them.

“There's a whole person missing from your argument.”

Richard caught Daynah's forearm and tried to turn him toward the platform.

Daynah shoved him.

It was not a demonstration. Richard flew backward, struck the lower exercise step with both heels and bent into the impact. The step skidded a foot. His hands never touched the floor.

He straightened.

“Okay,” he said, breathing harder. “Now we're moving.”

Richard sprang off the step.

His shoulder struck Daynah beneath the breastbone. The force lifted the big man's heels and drove him backward through the doorway. Daynah caught Richard around the upper arms, but Richard planted a foot on the remaining doorpost and turned in his grip.

A knee cracked into Daynah's ribs.

Daynah answered with a short gauntleted hook. Richard ducked beneath it, landed on the platform and struck twice more while Daynah was still turning. One blow hit the shoulder. The other drove into the sore place beneath his ribs.

“Two, three—”

Daynah swept a fist across at head height.

Richard hopped onto the low rail. The fist smashed through the spindle below his feet.

“—and four!”

He kicked Daynah in the jaw.

Daynah's head snapped sideways. Blood struck the rail. He caught it with one hand, hauled himself around it and drove forward.

Richard was already gone.

He crossed the exercise steps in three light contacts, using the last one to spring past Daynah's left shoulder. Daynah turned on him, guard high. Richard struck low, changed direction and came back high.

The gauntlets met the next blow with a sound like somebody shutting a vault on a sack of potatoes.

Daynah shoved him away.

Richard slid across the boards, recovered and clapped twice.

“That's better. Stay with me.”

“Try staying still.”

“That's what we're getting you away from.”

I have seen people charged for classes with less personal attention. Admittedly, those instructors didn't usually make your teeth taste like pennies before explaining the membership options.

Daynah stopped following him.

Richard moved left. Daynah shifted only far enough to keep him in front. A feint toward the steps drew no chase. Richard came in, and Daynah slipped the first strike, rolled beneath the second and drove a shovel hook into his middle.

The air left Richard in one sharp grunt.

Daynah caught the back of his shirt and brought him across the rail.

Wood split. A broken end cut Richard's side as he went over. He hit the platform on one shoulder, rolled and came up with a hand pressed against the spreading red patch.

Daynah stepped through the gap he'd made.

“Stay there.”

Richard looked at his bloody hand.

Then at Daynah's feet.

“You're still crossing when you turn.”

Daynah's jaw worked.

Richard lunged before the answer came.

Daynah brought both gauntlets together to catch the strike. Richard hit them, let the resistance turn his body, and planted his other foot against Daynah's hip. He drove down and forward at the same time.

The platform broke beneath Daynah.

He landed on his back beneath the broken deck. A joist caught across his chest. Broken planks scraped his chest as Richard landed above the hole and shoved him farther into it.

Daynah's breath punched out.

His right foot found earth beneath the platform. His left was trapped between two joists.

Richard stepped around the hole.

“One. Two.”

Daynah drove an elbow up. Richard skipped clear, planted on the next joist and struck the gauntlet reaching for him.

“Three. Four.”

Daynah caught his ankle.

Richard twisted, dragging Daynah's arm across the hole, and stamped his free foot into the exposed shoulder.

Pain flashed down Daynah's back. The trapped joist bowed against his shin.

Richard pulled again.

“Come on. You've got more than that.”

Daynah looked up at him.

That one landed in exactly the wrong place.

He stopped trying to pull his leg through the gap.

Instead he drove his heel outward, broke the timber trapping it, and planted both feet in the dirt. His shoulders rose. The boards around them began to separate.

Green spread across his skin.

His chest widened beneath the broken joist. Muscle climbed his neck and packed across his shoulders; his dark curls lifted as his head rose past Richard's face. Kahuhu expanded around his forearms, thickening with the hands inside them.

The platform could not hold thirteen feet of Big Kahuna coming up through its middle.

Daynah stood, tearing the joist free across his chest. It broke against his shoulders. The remaining planks fell away from him.

Richard backed onto the solid footing.

His eyes shone.

“There you are!”

Daynah's enlarged jaw tightened.

“Daynah was here.”

“I knew you had it in you!”

“Not yours.”

Richard lifted both hands.

“That's right. All you. I'm just helping you find it.”

Imagine becoming a fucking landslide to end an argument and the man taking it as a positive testimonial.

Richard moved again.

“One—”

Daynah kept his guard compact.

“Two—”

Richard changed his step, preparing the same turn that had taken him around the gauntlets before.

Daynah went before three.

A short lateral step put him outside Richard's approach. One enormous hand closed around the upper arm. Daynah turned his hips and drove the other fist into Richard's body, folding the forward charge around it.

He carried that momentum through the turn and slammed Richard across the remaining floor.

Boards blew apart beneath him. Richard slid through the spilled exercise steps, struck the stone footing and stopped.

Daynah stayed facing him.

Richard tried to get one knee underneath himself. It slipped. He settled back, breathing in short, careful pulls, one hand still held against his bleeding side.

The fight was over.

Daynah lowered his fists.

Richard raised a trembling thumb.

Daynah stared at it.

“Put that down.”

Richard managed a small, proud smile.

“You didn't wait for permission.”

Daynah picked up a loose board.

Richard's thumb remained up.

The board cracked in Daynah's hand.

He threw the pieces aside and walked away from him.

Richard reached for one of the scattered exercise steps and dragged it within reach. He put his good heel against it, tested the support and used it to raise himself into a seated position.

Daynah watched him do it without offering a hand.

Richard noticed.

“I've got it.”

Daynah nodded once.

That ought to have been the end of the conversation.

Richard looked up at the new height above him.

“I'd have started you on the smaller step.”

Daynah looked down at the splintered platform around his legs.

“Step too small.”

“We know that now.”

“Daynah knew.”

Richard pressed his hand harder against his side. His breath caught, and for a moment the bright expression went away. He waited through it instead of trying to talk.

Daynah's fists loosened.

Then Richard inhaled carefully and said, “First session's always an adjustment.”

“Last session.”

“Let's see how you feel after a drink.”

The cuts along his forearm had begun drawing together, but the skin at his hip pulled sharply when he stepped. Blood dried on his lower lip. His body was repairing itself. His clothes were still nailed to the afternoon.

Bob remained against the stone support. He looked up at the bigger Daynah, then at the broken coffin.

Daynah saw the calculation.

“No bigger box.”

Bob lowered his good hand.

For a little while nobody tried anything.

Daynah cleared the wreckage from the doorway. He wasn't rebuilding it. He dragged a broken beam aside, kicked the pointed ends away from the walking line and shoved a fallen panel flat so Richard could get out without climbing over nails.

Richard watched him.

“Good recovery.”

Daynah pointed through the opening.

“Door. Use door.”

Richard reached toward the bag beside his exercise mats. He pulled out a towel, pressed it to his side and looked toward the one wall still standing.

“I arranged drinks for the first class,” Richard said. “He should be here.”

Daynah looked at the empty doorway.

“Tell him class finished.”

Richard peered past him toward the lane.

“Could really use that drink now.”

Daynah turned.

The wall exploded inward.

A round glass body filled the breach, red liquid sloshing behind a huge smiling face. Splintered timbers hit the newly cleared route. A broken lintel spun toward Richard.

“OH, YEAH!”

Daynah caught the beam in one gauntlet. His arm dipped under its weight. He turned it down into the dirt instead of letting it carry through to the injured man.

Dust swallowed the doorway.

The Kool-Aid Man lifted one broad hand.

Richard coughed into his towel.

“Thank you.”

Daynah looked from him to the pitcher.

Then to the opening he'd just cleared.

The Kool-Aid Man's smile did not change.

Daynah pointed at the rubble.

“Door was there.”

“Oh, yeah!”

“Then why wall?”

The pitcher stepped forward. Glass scraped along broken timber. His great round belly pushed a beam sideways toward Bob.

Daynah put a hand against it and stopped it.

The Kool-Aid Man pushed harder.

Red drink surged up behind the smile.

Daynah's feet dug into the ground.

“Move back.”

The pitcher body drove into him.

It hit like a tank full of fruit punch deciding it had right of way. Daynah went through the cleared doorway backward, crushed the fallen panel beneath his heels and struck the embankment above the harbor lane.

Earth burst around his shoulders.

He came forward out of it with his guard raised.

The Kool-Aid Man charged again.

Daynah let the belly meet his left gauntlet at an angle. He turned with it, sending the charge along the slope instead of straight down toward the road, and drove his right fist into the side of the pitcher.

A crack flashed across the glass.

The liquid struck the opposite wall inside it, came back, and slammed the cracked section against his knuckles.

Daynah's arm jolted. His feet skidded through loose earth.

The crack stopped just short of the thick glass foot.

Daynah saw it. He shifted for another strike at the same place, but the pitcher dropped his weight and swung his round body across the slope.

The red liquid rolled with him.

It carried the turn harder than Daynah expected. The glass foot caught his planted ankle. He went sideways, smashed a shoulder into the earth and slid toward the drop above the lane.

Below him, a handcart rattled past the foot of the steps.

Daynah dug his left gauntlet into the bank.

Soil tore away. His fingers found the buried edge of the pavilion's stone footing, and he stopped himself against it.

The Kool-Aid Man was already coming down the slope.

Daynah hauled himself up along the stone, moving across the charge instead of meeting it from below. His free hand caught the thick rim as it passed. The pull wrenched his shoulder, but his anchored arm held long enough to swing the pitcher into the empty work area.

He released before the force could tear the footing loose.

The pitcher crashed across the broken exercise steps. One lodged beneath its round foot. It rocked, trying to free itself.

Daynah came off the bank after it.

He kicked the trapped step farther underneath. The pitcher tipped. Red liquid climbed the inside of its glass, its weight dragging the damaged side downward.

Daynah struck that side again.

The crack lengthened with a high, thin squeal.

The pitcher swung a glass arm at his head.

Daynah ducked beneath it. The arm sheared a chunk from the embankment. He stepped in close, caught the thick handle at the pitcher's back and hauled it around.

The smile turned away from him.

He drove a knee into the fractured side.

Glass snapped.

A red jet hit his chest.

The Kool-Aid Man lurched, braced and dragged him back toward the pavilion. Daynah held the handle with one hand and hammered the spreading fracture with the other.

The pitcher burst.

Glass flew across the work area. Red liquid slapped the surviving posts and poured over the floor. Daynah turned his face behind a gauntlet. A shard drove into his calf; another scored his shoulder before striking the ground.

The huge handle remained in his hand.

For a moment he stood in a settling rain of sweet red drink.

He looked through the handle at Richard.

Richard had lifted his towel over his face.

“Everybody okay?” he asked from behind it.

Daynah dropped the handle.

“Ask wall.”

The liquid moved.

It ran against the slope.

Daynah watched the red stream climb over a broken plank and slide into the open lower half of Bob's coffin. More followed it, gathering inside the surviving walls. The torn upper edge dripped. The reinforced bottom disappeared beneath a rising red surface.

Daynah looked at Bob.

Bob was watching too.

“Good base,” the builder said faintly.

The coffin hit Daynah in the shins.

Both feet went out from under him.

He landed on his back, and the wooden body drove over him, its broken upper edge tearing a line across his thigh. He caught the front corner before it reached his throat and shoved it sideways.

The coffin rolled off him. Red liquid climbed its inside walls without spilling out.

It righted itself.

Daynah got one knee under him.

The reinforced underside slammed into his chest.

He hit the slope again. Air left him in a roar. One broad corner dragged across his ribs, opening skin as the coffin drove him through the loose earth.

Behind it, Bob was trying to sit straighter.

Don't look proud, you little grave contractor. This is not the customer using your product correctly.

Daynah planted his heels, caught both corners and stopped the coffin short of the lane.

The wood bucked between his hands.

Red fluid surged against the far end. The coffin twisted, dragging his right foot through a fan of glass. Daynah shifted his weight off the cut foot without releasing it.

He drove a fist into the bottom.

The braces held.

He hit it again, harder.

The reinforced face bowed, sprang back and smashed the same gauntlet into his chest.

Daynah bared his teeth.

He knew why it was holding.

He had fucking told Bob where to put the nails.

The coffin tore free and skidded sideways toward the stone support where Bob sat. Daynah moved across its path, seized the near corner and swung its front away from him.

It struck the support instead.

Stone chips flew. Bob curled around his injured hand.

Daynah kept hold of the corner. His other hand went to the end board, above the reinforced base.

He felt it shift.

The bottom was sound. The wall above it wasn't.

The coffin pulled backward, trying to wrench him off balance.

Daynah stepped with it.

He let it build speed, then drove his fingers into the loose end joint and pulled outward while turning his body clear of the front.

The end board tore away.

Red liquid surged into the opening. The coffin's own charge peeled the side from its fasteners. Nails ripped through wet wood. The remaining wall split and collapsed outward.

The reinforced base shot on alone, skidded across the dirt and stopped against the broken steps.

The liquid spread across the low ground.

Daynah stood holding the torn end board.

The coffin walls lay around the intact base, nails pointing out through torn fibers. Red drink ran between them.

The red puddle began gathering itself again.

Daynah watched thick granules bunch beneath its surface. Clearer water ran around the edges while the red center pulled inward, getting smaller and darker.

Richard lowered his towel.

“Don't give him room.”

Daynah glanced toward the rolled mats by the doorway.

He dropped the board and snatched up the nearest mat. Heavy canvas unrolled between his hands. He swept it beneath the gathering mass, folded the edges over and clamped them together before the liquid could run free.

The bundle kicked.

Daynah brought it down on the stone footing.

Red water spurted through the weave.

He folded the canvas tighter, put the second gauntlet over the first and leaned his weight into it. The footing took the load. Water streamed down its sides, carrying loose pink grit into the dirt.

Inside the cloth, something pushed back.

Daynah adjusted his hands, keeping the edges trapped.

The pressure drove another stream from the weave. The bundle shrank. Its violent kicking became a hard, uneven tremor beneath his palms.

He held it there until the movement stopped.

When he opened the canvas, a dense red block sat in the middle of a ring of powdered drink mix.

Daynah prodded it with one thick finger.

It didn't move.

He looked at the wrecked pitcher. Then the block.

“Stay dry.”

Richard stared at the ruined exercise mat.

“That was new.”

Daynah looked at him.

Richard looked at the glass in Daynah's leg.

“Never mind.”

Daynah pulled the shard free and set it on the stone beside the mat. Blood followed it. He stood still until his foot could take his weight without slipping in it.

The huge body remained green. His breathing had slowed, but his shoulders stayed high and his hands kept opening and closing. Nothing in the wreckage offered another attack.

Richard tried to stand. His knee folded, and he caught himself on the stone with a hiss.

Daynah watched him for a moment. Then he dragged the intact coffin base away from the steps and set it beside him, low and flat.

Richard sat on it carefully.

The braces held his weight.

He looked down, then up at Daynah.

“You made something useful.”

Daynah lifted one gauntlet.

Richard held up a hand before he could speak.

“I know. It wasn't for me.”

Daynah let the hand fall.

Richard adjusted the towel against his side and reached for the bag. He found a small water bottle, unscrewed the cap and offered it upward.

Daynah looked at the bottle.

Then at the red block lying on the open canvas beside Richard's foot.

He took the bottle, put the cap back on and moved it to the far end of the footing.

Richard followed it with his eyes.

“You have to hydrate.”

Daynah picked up the red block.

“Not everybody.”
