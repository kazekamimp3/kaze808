Season 3, Episode 41: “Cluck Around and Find Out”

Chris screwed the cap onto Daniel's bottle, missed the thread, and tried again.

They buried Daniel above the stream that evening. Daynuh broke the ground. Kaze pulled out the stones. Arthur took the unopened purse off Daniel's belt before they lowered the blanket, and kept it separate.

Chris set the marker.

When he returned to the water, Kaze was sitting with Daniel's spear across his knees.

“Don't lose that,” Chris said.

“I won't.”

By morning, the grave was behind them. It wasn't farther away.

Daynuh fell asleep beside Mahnuh while Chris worked over the cooking fire. They were keeping the stream camp for another few days: shelter, water, the planted pear tree, and a place to return to while Kaze checked the crusher yard again. Nobody had identified Daniel's killer. Nobody had volunteered to march in a random direction and pretend that counted.

Chris had just lifted the pan when Daynuh woke.

His hand moved over empty ground.

“Mahnuh?”

He sat up.

Looked behind himself.

Then beneath the blanket, which was ambitious. His dog stood five feet seven inches at the shoulder. Unless the blanket had developed a fucking basement, the search was over.

Chris pointed his spoon toward the trees.

“He was here.”

“When?”

“When he was here.”

Daynuh stood.

“Useful. If I find him yesterday, I'll tell you.”

He checked the bank, then the road beyond the trees. Chris put the pan aside and followed. At his belt, the banjo carried a low, interested chuckle.

Inside BELLY, full-sized Catfish Billy was watching the second monitor. Chris's amber headlamp supplied the actual view. The first monitor wasn't showing their search. Nobody was playing the game.

“Christopher. Lift your head. If the animal is hiding beneath your boots, we've already lost him.”

“Looking for tracks.”

“Then I withdraw my concern. We shall recover whichever shoe you wore here.”

They reached the road as a local watchman came up from the market settlement downstream. He saw Daynuh, slowed, and stopped outside arm's reach.

“You missing a dog?”

Daynuh's face changed.

“Where?”

“Poultry shop.”

“Outside?”

The man shook his head.

“Inside.”

Daynuh looked at Chris.

Chris looked toward town.

Billy made one pleased note through the banjo.

“Oh, I should like to see the premises.”

Cluck Around and Find Out occupied a stone shop beside a broad loading yard. Its painted chicken had one raised foot and the unpleasant confidence of a bird unaware of the rotisserie beneath it.

Mahnuh couldn't read the sign.

He had followed hot chicken through the open loading doors.

Which is how most religions would recruit me, if they stopped getting distracted by my moral condition.

Four customers stood outside. One still held his money out in front of him, as though the transaction might resume at a safer address.

The poultry seller stood behind the counter.

Mahnuh stood in front of it.

His great blue-gray head hovered above an empty tray. His tail moved slowly enough to suggest that, from his perspective, everybody was having a lovely morning.

Daynuh stepped through the door.

“Mahnuh.”

The dog's ears turned.

“Come.”

Mahnuh looked at the seller.

The seller looked at Daynuh.

“He already had it.”

“How much?”

“One roast.”

Chris inspected the tray.

“Clean work.”

“He ate my chicken.”

“I'm talking about the tray. You could see your fucking childhood in that.”

Billy's voice rolled out of the banjo.

“Sir, have you considered serving something less persuasive?”

The seller stared at Chris's belt.

Chris shook his head.

“That's a whole other problem. Finish the dog first.”

Daynuh put a hand against Mahnuh's neck.

“Out.”

The dog followed him. At the threshold he stopped, turned, and took one scrap from beside the counter.

The seller made a strangled sound.

Daynuh looked down.

“Mahnuh.”

The dog swallowed.

Chris put a hand over his mouth.

Daynuh turned toward him.

“You laughing?”

“No.”

Billy said, “He is obstructing the evidence with his fingers.”

Daynuh led Mahnuh outside. He didn't pretend a scolding would restore a chicken. He told the seller he'd return to settle the loss, then walked his dog home.

No spell had compelled the theft. No secret history of chicken crime explained it.

An open door had smelled good.

The people had moved.

The chicken had become available.

Mahnuh learned something.

Unfortunately, so did Chris.

The following morning the dog returned along the stream with a duck hanging from his mouth.

Chris saw the wrapping first.

Then the duck.

Then Mahnuh stopped directly in front of him.

Chris glanced toward camp. Daynuh was farther up the bank, working a split handle loose from their pan. Arthur sat nearby cleaning one revolver.

Chris crouched.

“Where'd you get that?”

Mahnuh lowered the duck beside his boots.

Billy leaned toward the monitor.

“My goodness.”

“Quiet.”

“I haven't accused anyone of anything.”

Chris opened the paper.

He smelled juniper, browned skin, warm fat.

For a moment, professional respect overcame every remaining obligation.

“That bastard put the aromatics beneath the skin.”

Mahnuh sat.

“Don't look at me like that.”

The dog continued looking.

Chris pulled off a small boneless piece.

Billy laughed softly.

“Careful, Christopher. You're approaching a decision.”

“He brought it over here.”

“Yes. In his mouth. A method of acquisition you've apparently accepted as a receipt.”

Chris gave Mahnuh the piece, then ate one himself.

“Look at you,” he murmured. “Face like somebody stuffed a sofa into a condom, and you got better taste than half the people I cooked for.”

Mahnuh leaned forward.

“Don't get emotional. There's bones.”

The duck was excellent.

That made the next several decisions considerably easier.

He removed the string and paper, wiped the dog's chin with a wet cloth, and pushed the remaining duck behind his knee as Daynuh called down the bank.

“He with you?”

“Right here.”

“Where'd he go?”

“Had a little walk.”

Mahnuh went back to Daynuh and lay down with his head on his paws.

Chris looked at the banjo.

Billy waited.

Chris waited.

Then Billy said, “A thigh.”

“What?”

“When I come outside. One proper thigh. Skin intact.”

“You're charging me for silence?”

“I'm joining the meal.”

“You didn't steal it.”

“Neither did you, a moment ago. And yet you've found a place at the table.”

Chris looked down at the duck.

He tore off another piece.

Now, I have watched villains negotiate over planets. I have watched men threaten entire civilizations because somebody disrespected their stupid little helmet.

These two had just formed a criminal partnership over poultry, while the only member taking actual risks had gone to sleep beside his owner.

By the next afternoon, Mahnuh knew the route.

The seller had learned too. Cooked birds no longer sat at the front counter. A hinged barrier blocked the loading entrance. Behind it, a worker carried food through an inner door.

Mahnuh came up to the barrier.

Stopped.

Sniffed.

The seller watched from inside.

“No.”

Mahnuh waited.

“No.”

A customer approached carrying a basket. He saw the dog and placed his basket on the ground so he could retreat with both hands available.

Mahnuh turned.

The customer retreated farther.

Chris arrived in time to see the dog picking up the abandoned basket.

“Nope. Put that down.”

Mahnuh brought it to him.

The basket held bread, eggs, smoked meat and a small pot of fat.

Chris took the handle.

Mahnuh sat.

The seller came outside.

“You again.”

Chris held out the basket.

“Found this.”

“It was beside him.”

“Hell of a coincidence. Saved us a search.”

The customer peered around the corner.

“That's mine.”

Chris gave it back.

The man accepted it without approaching Mahnuh.

The dog watched the basket leave.

Billy spoke from Chris's belt.

“A disappointing end to a promising assortment.”

Chris lowered his voice.

“You said a thigh. You don't get to expand the arrangement.”

The seller looked at him.

“What arrangement?”

“Talking to the banjo.”

“Why?”

“Because it won't shut the fuck up.”

Chris took Mahnuh home without a reward.

He made it halfway before the dog stopped at a puddle, lowered his head, and presented his greasy chin.

Chris stood over him.

“Oh, you motherfucker.”

Mahnuh waited.

Billy's delight was audible.

“He remembered the service.”

Chris wiped the dog's face.

From the road above them, unseen behind the bank, Daynuh watched the last part.

He didn't call out.

He watched Chris fold the cloth and put it away.

That evening Mahnuh refused the last of his ordinary food.

Daynuh looked at the bowl.

Looked at the dog.

Then looked at Chris, who had developed an extraordinary interest in keeping a clean cloth beside his knee.

“He sick?”

Chris looked up.

“Doesn't look sick.”

“Didn't ask how he looks.”

“Could be tired.”

“From what?”

Billy made a small sound through the banjo.

Chris covered it with a cough.

Daynuh waited.

Chris reached for a spoon.

Daynuh took the spoon before he could start cleaning it.

“Leave that alone. It's cleaner than your fucking answer.”

Arthur lowered his revolver.

Kaze, returning from the yard with no new name to follow, stopped beside the fire.

“What'd he do?”

“Don't know yet,” Daynuh said.

Chris spread his hands.

“That's a hell of a way to introduce an investigation.”

“Then help me finish it.”

Mahnuh rolled onto his side.

His belly made a low, satisfied noise.

Everybody looked at him.

Billy said, very softly, “An unreliable witness.”

Daynuh turned toward the instrument.

“You're enjoying this.”

“I have a limited view and an excellent seat.”

“Keep it.”

Daynuh got up and went toward town.

Chris watched him leave.

Billy said, “We may need to reconsider the thigh.”

The next morning, Chris found Mahnuh beside the road.

The dog wasn't carrying food.

He was staring at a two-wheeled delivery cart.

A worker had stopped outside the poultry shop. Behind the cart's raised slats sat closed tins of cooked meat, cooling on racks. The seller stood in the doorway, checking each one as it came down.

The goods were covered. The loading gate was shut. Customers kept their baskets in their hands.

For several minutes, everything worked.

Chris stood beside Mahnuh.

“See? That's it. Business is over.”

The dog sniffed toward the cart.

“Don't.”

Mahnuh looked at him.

Chris pointed back toward camp.

“Come on.”

Then a lid lifted.

Steam crossed the yard.

Mahnuh's head followed it.

Inside BELLY, Billy sat up so sharply that the monitor became a window worth occupying.

“Turn a little to the left.”

“No.”

“Christopher.”

“What?”

“I am trying to see what they have done with the skin.”

Chris looked.

Billy sighed.

“There. Look at that. A bird should shine a little. Not wet. Not varnished like a dead uncle in a cheap coffin. Just enough to make a man reconsider his commitments.”

“You don't know a fucking thing about roasting.”

“I know what encourages travel.”

The worker carried the tin through the inner door.

Mahnuh moved to follow.

Chris caught his attention.

“No. Here.”

The dog stopped beside him.

The seller noticed.

For the first time all morning, he smiled.

“That's better.”

Chris nodded.

“Yeah.”

“Keep doing that.”

Chris looked at him.

“Doing what?”

“Keeping him away from the food.”

The seller went inside.

Chris watched the door.

Then looked down at Mahnuh.

Billy said, “You've been mistaken for the solution.”

“I heard.”

“There are opportunities in a misunderstanding.”

Chris took a slow breath.

The worker returned for another tin.

Chris pointed toward the cart's rear wheel.

“Your pin's walking out.”

The worker looked.

It was.

Chris crouched, braced the wheel and pushed the loose retaining pin back into place. He checked the split end, bent it farther, then stood.

“Road'll shake it loose again if you leave it straight.”

The worker thanked him.

An ordinary useful thing had happened.

Then Chris asked, “Anything getting thrown out?”

The worker looked toward the shop.

“A few ends.”

“Dog's behaving.”

Mahnuh sat.

“Could take those off your hands.”

The worker brought him a small bundle of scraps.

Chris accepted it.

Billy watched through the headlamp.

“Well. Honest success. How unsettling.”

Chris gave Mahnuh one piece.

Kept another.

The dog followed him home.

He had not stolen anything that morning.

Chris told Daynuh that part with the confidence of a man who had discovered the most convenient six minutes of the truth.

Daynuh listened.

Then he asked, “Where's the duck?”

Chris stopped chewing.

Behind him, Arthur leaned back against a tree.

Kaze looked at the banjo.

The instrument remained silent.

Chris swallowed.

“What duck?”

“The one the shop lost the other morning.”

Chris looked toward Mahnuh.

Mahnuh had lain down.

Daynuh nodded.

“You can stop checking whether he's gonna tell me.”

“I'm aware.”

Daynuh did too.

“And you.”

Chris looked at the paper.

He tried a bad one anyway.

“Could've blown.”

“Under your fucking knee?”

Chris pointed at it.

“He wanted a thigh.”

Daynuh slowly turned his head.

Billy waited.

“A modest interest,” the fish said.

Daynuh folded the wrapping.

“You knew I was looking for him.”

“First time, no,” Chris said.

“After, yeah.”

He wasn't stupid. He also wasn't going to build a jail around a dog because two grown men had discovered poultry with the lights turned low.

“Yeah.”

Billy said, “Entirely reasonable.”

“You don't get to climb onto the good side of this conversation.”

“So who got the duck?”

He stopped bringing cooked birds through the yard during the busiest hour. The tins traveled in a closed box secured across the cart. Two men unloaded it. One stayed with the mule. The other carried the tins directly through the loading gate.
