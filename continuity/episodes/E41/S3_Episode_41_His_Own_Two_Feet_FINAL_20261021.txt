Earlier that day, at the port, Brian discovered that the pain at his waist eased when Tracy's shoulder began to throb.

This was the fourth day since the ground had broken. The three nights Dutch had bought were over. He paid two more silver to keep the back room until the following morning, leaving seventy, then went to examine the harbor with the stolen cargo ledger.

Tracy stayed behind with Clifford.

“My arm's asleep,” he said.

Clifford looked up from the wheel he was cleaning.

“You been lying on it?”

“I been lying next to it. This motherfucker lives on it.”

Brian glanced down at the place where his miniature torso joined Tracy's shoulder. Under the skin, something moved against the direction of Tracy's pulse.

He had felt movement there before. He had also spent a great deal of time claiming it meant progress without knowing what the fuck was progressing.

Now he could see a narrow ridge running toward his own waist.

He pressed it with his little palm.

Tracy flinched.

“Don't do that.”

Brian let go.

The ridge relaxed. A cramp passed through his own stomach. Beneath him, in tissue that had never contained a separate lower body, something pulled tight.

He pressed again.

Tracy slapped his hand away.

“I said don't.”

Brian looked offended enough to hide how interested he was.

“I'm trying to understand our situation.”

“Understand it with your hands in your lap.”

“I don't have a lap.”

“Then that's a problem for the pants department. Leave my fucking shoulder alone.”

Clifford turned his wheel another quarter rotation and picked grit from the axle.

“If it hurts, stop doing it.”

Brian waited until neither man was looking.

Then he touched the ridge again, more gently.

The organism beneath their joined skin contracted around his fingers. It wasn't answering a command. It had found pressure, warmth and an open route into living tissue.

Brian held the route open.

By afternoon, he had a knee.

It lay folded beneath the skin at his waist, pale and small, pushing against Tracy's shoulder as though someone had packed a doll into a sausage casing.

Tracy stood in front of the room's little mirror and pulled his collar down.

“What is that?”

Brian twisted to look with him.

“Development.”

“It got a joint.”

“Yes.”

“Why does my shoulder got somebody else's joint?”

“Technically, it's mine.”

Tracy looked at Brian's face in the glass.

“Then why am I paying for it?”

He pushed away from the mirror. His right hand shook. Brian could feel that tremor through the tissue between them; his own tiny hands had become steadier.

That was the point when he understood the exchange.

Not all of it. Not the organism's history, what it wanted or what it might do after this. Enough.

What was growing on Brian's side was coming out of Tracy's.

He covered the swelling with a fold of Tracy's shirt.

“We should rest.”

Tracy pulled the cloth back down.

“We should find somebody to get this shit out of me.”

Brian's little fingers tightened.

“We don't know what cutting it would do.”

“I know what leaving it is doing. I got a goddamn ankle in my armpit.”

He went toward the door.

His knee buckled.

Clifford locked his chair and caught Tracy's forearm before he hit the floor. The brace beneath Clifford's suit took his weight as he stood long enough to get Tracy against the bed.

“Sit.”

“I'm sitting. The floor applied early.”

Brian slid his hand under the collar.

“Don't touch it,” Clifford said.

Brian brought the hand back out.

Tracy was looking at him now.

“What did you do?”

“I didn't put it there.”

“I didn't ask who put it there.”

For a moment, the room was very quiet.

Dutch came back carrying the ledger beneath his arm. He stopped at the sight of Tracy against the bed.

“What happened?”

“He keeps fucking with it,” Tracy said.

Brian pointed at his own waist.

“I'm growing. That's not an accusation. That's an observation.”

Dutch came closer. He saw the swelling, the little foot-shaped bulge and the red branching marks spreading down Tracy's chest.

“Can you make it stop?”

Brian looked down.

“I've been trying.”

He had been trying several things. Stopping wasn't one of them.

Dutch put the ledger aside and went back out to look for help. Clifford stayed by the bed, where he could see Brian's hands.

Tracy kept himself awake by talking.

“When I get you off me, I'm putting you in a bowl.”

Brian watched the door.

“You don't have to be like that.”

“With a little lid. Air holes. A fucking raisin. You can tell the raisin about distribution.”

Clifford moved his chair closer.

“You thirsty?”

Tracy nodded. Clifford held the cup while he drank.

Brian looked away from the water running down Tracy's chin.

He could feel two feet now.

Not guesses. Toes that moved when he tried to move them. A minute ago his whole life had still depended on somebody else's decision to stand up.

He flexed them again.

Tracy felt it.

His hand closed around Brian's little wrist.

“You enjoying that?”

Brian looked at him.

“I want to get down.”

“Then get down without taking me with you.”

Brian didn't answer.

Clifford saw Tracy's grip tighten. He put the cup aside.

“Brian. Whatever you're doing.”

“I stopped.”

The ridge beneath Brian's other palm was still taut.

Tracy followed Clifford's eyes.

Then he grabbed Brian around the middle.

“Get your fucking hand off it.”

The little man screamed as Tracy pulled. The skin at their joining opened just enough to expose something dark and wet stretched between them.

It recoiled from the air.

Tracy let go, gasping. Clifford caught his wrist before he could tear at it again.

“Hold still. Dutch is coming.”

Brian's new legs kicked inside the thin membrane at his waist.

The organism began pulling them back toward the shared mass.

He felt the freedom receding before he'd used it.

“No.”

He pressed both hands against the moving ridge and pushed his body away from Tracy's shoulder.

Tracy arched against the bed.

“Stop.”

Brian pushed harder.

The thing between them tightened into a living cord. Under Tracy's chest, something dragged toward it. His next breath came out in a wet, unfinished cough.

Clifford seized Brian's wrists.

“Let go.”

For one instant Brian did.

Tracy drew air.

Brian's feet curled back beneath him.

He put his mouth against the cord and bit.

The organism convulsed.

The split spread down Tracy's shoulder. A dark sheath peeled away with Brian's forming hips, hauling red tissue out through the opening. Clifford grabbed the shirt and pressed it against the wound while Brian pulled clear onto the bed.

His legs came with him.

Tracy tried to say his name.

He never got past the first sound.

Clifford kept the pressure on. He shouted for Dutch, then shouted again. Brian lay beside them, kicking free of the collapsing membrane, while the large body that had carried him went still.

When Dutch came through the door, there was no one behind him yet.

He took one look at the bed and went to Clifford's side.

Clifford didn't take his hand away.

Brian sat up.

Below his waist were two bare little legs. A twitch ran through the dark seam at his hip, independent of anything he had asked it to do.

He put one foot on the blanket.

Then the other.

“Don't step over him,” Clifford said.

Brian froze.

His foot hovered above Tracy's arm. He brought it back down on the blanket.

Dutch pulled a clean section of the blanket over Tracy's face. Clifford stayed beside the bed until Dutch touched his arm.

Then he sat back in his chair.

Brian climbed down by the bedframe. His new foot found the floor. His other foot followed. He stood there, completely separate, no taller in spirit and considerably more naked than he had prepared for.

He took a step.

His knee folded.

He struck the floor chin first.

No one helped him up.

Now, I know. Some of you were waiting for the triumphant little walk. Brass section. Soft light. A man finally free to pursue his dreams and inconvenience a different shoulder. It took him a very long time.

By morning, the port had acquired something worse than a rumor.

At first it was a line of white water beyond the damaged outer quay. Then the water swelled, lifted a fishing float and rolled it backward against its rope.

A black curve rose beneath it.

The harbor bells began again.

Dutch stood at the quay with Clifford, who had put his helmet back on. They had left Tracy's body in the net-mender's room under the proprietor's care. Brian sat on the closed cargo ledger in Clifford's lap, wrapped below the waist in a strip cut from the room's blanket.

He had tried walking the whole way. Clifford had watched him take seventeen furious steps before saying they would all be dead of old age.

He had not called it forgiveness.

The submarine surfaced beyond the broken berth.

Water streamed from a long metal hull. A raised command tower climbed clear of the sea; behind it, more black steel kept arriving where there had been open water. Ropes snapped taut on the nearest moored boats. Men on the quay ran to free them before the wash crushed them against the pilings.

Dutch backed Clifford's chair away from the edge.

The vessel slowed. Water churned beneath its stern, holding it off the damaged stone. A hatch opened. Crew in gray came onto the deck and began preparing lines.

No cannon fired. Nothing needed to. A functioning piece of another world's industrial arrogance had just made the entire harbor rearrange its afternoon.

Morning. Sorry. The penis submarine threw me. I can be professional about a lot of things, but a Bond villain arriving in a horizontal architectural erection while our smallest executive is wearing a blood-adjacent napkin—give me a second.

He had a scar beside one eye and the expression of somebody who had paid a great deal of money to enter a room last.
