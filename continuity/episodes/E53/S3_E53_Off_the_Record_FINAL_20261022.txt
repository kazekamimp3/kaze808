Season 3, Episode 53: “Off the Record”

Arthur had made the fire small enough to deny it was a camp.

Chris had put a pan over it, which weakened the argument.

They were above the old works, on a shelf of ground screened from the wheelwright's sheds by a broken stone wall. Daynah sat with Mahnah's enormous head against his knee. Beyond the wall, the last work crews were coming down from the damaged road.

The foreman knew where they were. Arthur had made sure of that before coming up, and the man had said the shelf was theirs until morning. Kaze wouldn't have to go hunting for his own fucking family after spending an afternoon investigating how other people had killed it.

A landing cracked the slope below them.

Chris looked over the pan.

“That'll be our delicate little flower.”

Kaze came around the wall with dust on his shoes and the wrapped weapon still at his waist.

Arthur looked past him.

“Just you?”

“That's what I took.”

“And?”

Kaze took the hilt out. Held it toward him.

Arthur accepted it, checked the wrapping and put the same dead man's weapon back with his own gear.

“He knew Maul's face,” Kaze said. “Fought a machine that could look like him. Says he never met the real one.”

Chris stopped turning the food.

“Man went all that way to find somebody getting jumped by a DVD.”

“Training machine.”

“Yeah. You put the disc in. Bruce Lee comes out the television and beats your stepdad.”

An educational product with a very aggressive fucking refund policy.

Chris nodded.

“See, he gets it.”

Arthur looked between them.

“Who gets it?”

“Wade.”

Arthur looked at the empty ground beside the fire.

Then back at Kaze.

“I was hoping that would stop while you were gone.”

“He was there too,” Kaze said.

Arthur rubbed his jaw.

“Of course he was.”

Daynah scratched behind Mahnah's ear.

“You can hear whatever the fuck you like. Somebody tell me what the man actually said.”

Kaze did. The Sith. Vader. The Emperor. Killing one master and getting rebuilt by the other. A man looking for a way off the world without giving him a useful connection to either grave.

Arthur listened through the whole account.

“You believe him?”

“I believe he wants out.”

“That wasn't the question.”

Kaze looked at him.

Then laughed once.

“Motherfucker. He did that too.”

“Must be catching.”

“He see your face and go, oh, that's the man I should lie to?”

“He didn't see my face and become my fucking employee.”

“I didn't say employee. People lie recreationally. Charles does it like stretching.”

From the small assembly beside Chris's retracted mask came Billy's banjo-shaped voice.

“Christopher, Charles has never stretched in his life.”

Inside, Billy had his own instrument across his lap and Chris's headlamp view on the exterior monitor. One ancient catfish, full-sized, sitting in a protected living room inside the man cooking supper. Yes. We are continuing to make that your problem.

“He tried to tell me he pulled a muscle job hunting,” Chris said.

“Perhaps he reached for an application.”

“He can't read.”

“That would explain the strain.”

Daynah gave a short laugh.

Mahnah opened one eye, decided nobody had dropped anything worth getting up for, and closed it again.

Arthur moved closer to Kaze, away from the pan.

“And Tai Lung?”

The name changed the conversation without anybody needing to announce that the conversation had changed.

“Nothing from him,” Kaze said.

“You ask your other friend?”

Kaze looked up.

“Where is he?”

I don't have his current address.

“You had Starkiller's.”

I looked at Starkiller. He was standing on a fucking breakwater. Tai Lung has not helpfully nailed himself to a post and waited for your revenge itinerary.

Kaze's jaw shifted.

“But you knew he killed Daniel.”

Yes.

“Whole time.”

Yes.

Chris set the pan down on a flat stone.

“What'd he say?” Arthur asked.

Kaze kept looking upward.

“He knew.”

Arthur watched him for a moment.

“And now?”

“Doesn't know where he is.”

“All right.”

There was no accusation in it. That almost made it worse.

“We know who,” Arthur said. “We don't know where. Tell me when that changes.”

He went back to the fire.

Kaze stayed by the wall.

“You were gonna let us keep guessing?”

You were guessing before you remembered I could answer. You asked. I told you. I'm not going to improve that by inventing a leopard's fucking postal code.

“You could've said something.”

I say things constantly. I spent my entire time up here thinking I was talking into the world's most violent dead fucking headset.

Chris looked up from the food.

“Don't start that poor-me shit.”

Thank you. A listener with standards.

“I mean you. You sound like a landlord nobody visits.”

Kaze's mouth moved despite himself.

Arthur held out a plate.

“Whenever the three of you finish.”

Daynah took his own portion.

“Four. Billy's in there.”

“Wonderful,” Arthur said. “A committee I can't hear.”

He sat against the wall.

Then Chris heard Billy say something through the headgear and stopped with the next bite halfway to his mouth.

“What do you mean, admission?”

“Admission to the screening.”

“Whose screening?”

“Charles and the little fellow's. They have arranged an evening.”

“An evening of what?”

“You will be relieved to learn they have not sold the television.”

Nobody liked the distance Billy had left between that sentence and the answer.

“What are they charging?”

“Refreshments.”

Chris stood.

Kaze leaned back against the stone.

“There goes the homeowner.”

“Watch my fucking pan.”

“I'll tell it you love it.”

Chris stepped beyond the fire, where there was room for the larger body.

His jaw opened too far. The seam continued down through his chest, flesh drawing inward as the enormous catfish shape rolled through the opening. Hands became green, thick-fingered claws. Boots vanished with the human legs. Billy's weight settled into the dirt, leaving Chris full-sized inside the living domain.

Billy drew his banjo through the last of the opening, cradling it against one forearm before the human shape disappeared.

“Still a fucked-up way to change shifts.”

“I don't think I should.”

“Christopher once attempted to retain a sandwich throughout the process.”

“We disagreed on the definition.”
