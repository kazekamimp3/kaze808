Season 3, Episode 46: “The Upper Road”

Before Brian's cart reached the coast, before Dr. Evil began subtracting gravy from Brian's breakfast, K.K. went looking for the place the water came from.

He stood above the camp's pear tree with his walking stick in both hands. Twenty feet of living timber, narrow enough to make his height feel like something the world had forgotten to finish.

“How far?” Daynuh asked.

“Until something interests me more.”

“That ain't a distance.”

“No. It's how I choose one.”

Chris was getting ready to go into town. The banjo rested at his belt; Billy watched through the headlamp from inside BELLY.

K.K. lowered his face toward him.

“I'd like to tell you what I find.”

“Town first,” Chris said. “Then I'll be here.”

Arthur pointed upstream.

“Road bends east after the old crossing. People work up there.”

Kaze watched the tree straighten.

“If somebody tells you what you are, ask why they need you to be it.”

K.K. tilted his head.

“You told me I was interesting.”

“Yeah.”

“Why did you need that?”

Kaze opened his mouth.

K.K. waited.

“Go find your fucking water.”

The leaves moved. K.K. stepped across the stream and went.

Several ridges upstream, an active lime works had arranged itself around the heat inside a long brick furnace. Limestone waited on the terrace. Fuel lay beneath a shed. An iron shutter hung over the deep feeding passage.

At the damaged crossing, a worker was trying to move a barrel across two inadequate boards.

He saw K.K.'s arms.

K.K. saw him seeing them.

“You're about to ask me to become furniture.”

“Only for a minute.”

“That's how it begins.”

They reached an agreement. K.K. wanted to look at what the men were doing with the hill. The man wanted his barrels across the gap.

K.K. rooted one hand into usable soil behind him and extended the other across the crossing. Living fibers stiffened under the first barrel. His fingers spread against the far stone.

“Not on the thumb,” he told the worker.

The barrel moved.

“There.”

When the last load crossed, K.K. withdrew the growth and stood. The worker showed him the furnace from the safe side. Heat trembled above the brick.

“Don't put anything in there you want back.”

K.K. examined his fingers.

“I was going to keep these.”

Then a cart slipped off the upper approach.

Its driver jumped, caught his leg, and began going over with it. The mule fought the harness. Barrels struck the tailboard.

The whole cart stopped over empty air.

Across the gap stood a man with a red-and-black face, short horns and yellow eyes. One hand extended. The other held a long, unlit hilt.

He turned his wrist.

The harness tore free. The mule stumbled onto surviving ground. The cart moved sideways through the air and settled on the terrace with its driver still caught against the seat.

The stranger lowered his hand.

“You have a map.”

The driver dragged his leg free.

“Take it.”

The chart had been strapped beneath the seat. The stranger unrolled it, studying the marked breaks in the local roads.

“Who caused this?”

“People say a man. Downriver. Kaze.”

K.K. became still.

The yellow eyes lifted.

“You know him.”

“You saved a man so you could ask his paper a question.”

“And you made yourself a bridge.”

“Briefly.”

The stranger crossed the narrow footing.

“My name is Maul.”

“K.K.”

Maul watched the last small roots withdraw into his heels.

“You could be considerably more useful.”

“There it is.”

They moved onto the stone apron above the furnace. The workers returned to their tasks, carefully finding things to do below the conversation.

Maul wanted a route back to the crisis he'd been torn from on Mandalore. Sidious's design was still unfinished in the last world he remembered. Here, he had neither his people nor his ships. He had a map, an injured driver's rumor, and something that knew the man named in it.

“Bring me to Kaze,” he said. “Help me learn what broke this land. You could have room to become what you are.”

K.K. rested his stick against one shoulder.

“What am I now?”

“Strength spent beneath other men's carts.”

“They asked.”

“They will always ask.”

That interested him. So did the way Maul looked at his hands while pretending not to measure them.

“I could introduce you,” K.K. said. “You'd have to ask him yourself.”

“Naturally.”

“He might say no.”

“Then I would persuade him.”

K.K.'s narrow face lowered.

“Would you like to practice?”

Maul looked up.

“No,” K.K. said gently. “There. That's the part you'll need.”

He enjoyed the silence that followed. He knew what he was doing with it.

Maul's fingers tightened around the hilt.

“I am offering you a purpose.”

“I had one.”

“Looking at water.”

“Yes.”

The walking stick flew from K.K.'s hand.

Maul caught it.

K.K. looked at his empty fingers.

“Give it back.”

“Tell me where he is.”

Roots entered the soil beyond the apron.

The worker came up the steps with an iron wedge. K.K. turned toward him.

“Go down.”

The man obeyed.

Long wooden fingers unfolded around the stolen stick. A second hand grew from K.K.'s forearm and closed around Maul's wrist.

Maul's saber ignited at both ends.

Red light filled the brick apron.

K.K. opened a real gap through the gripping arm. The first blade passed through it. The other took off two fingers.

The grip closed again.

“You should have kept asking.”

K.K. shoved him backward.

Maul turned with the force, struck the wall feet-first and sprang away, cutting through the wrist that held him. He landed beside the fuel shed.

K.K. recovered his stick.

Sap sealed the cut. New fibers pressed outward.

Maul drove his free palm forward. The cart slammed sideways into the rooted legs and broke around them.

K.K. caught a length of the frame and drove it at him.

Maul cut it in half.

The piece behind it struck his shoulder and sent him into the shed.

K.K. put his walking stick carefully beside the wall.

Then grew three more arms.

The first fist broke brick where Maul's face had been. The second caught his weapon wrist. Maul twisted the opposite blade through the grabbing wood and drove the other end toward the narrow chest.

K.K.'s torso parted around it.

Hardening ribs closed on the hilt.

Maul released it before they caught his hands. Under his outstretched palm the weapon jerked sideways, carving free of the growing cage.

He caught it and rolled.

The fist that followed cracked the furnace apron above the feeding passage.

Heat breathed through the widened opening.

Maul looked at it.

K.K. saw him look.

The tree withdrew from the furnace, reaching for the wall. Overhead growth spread, hardened and came down across Maul's route.

It crushed the shed. A branch struck his metal leg before he landed.

Maul came up badly, cutting through the roots reaching toward usable earth. A shove of the Force drove K.K. against the retaining wall.

His shoulder split. Hardened splinters answered, opening Maul's upper arm and side. He staggered behind the cart bed, using its broken frame against the pieces his saber couldn't intercept.

K.K. climbed.

An iron poker tore through his reaching arm and struck brick behind it.

He shed the pinned wood and dropped, compressing his long torso around the blade coming for him. One hand closed around Maul's damaged leg.

Metal screamed.

Maul cut the gripping wrist. Another fist carried him into the shutter's counterweight chain.

The iron shutter fell halfway, caught on wreckage, and held there.

Maul rose bleeding.

The broken cart bed struck K.K.'s folded middle and drove him toward the furnace.

Four hands braced against the stone.

One reached Maul's throat.

His feet scraped forward as the fingers tightened.

The red blade severed the reaching forearm.

Maul tore the disconnected grip away, cut the remaining braces and drove the cart forward with the Force.

K.K.'s compressed body struck burning fuel inside the chamber.

He opened himself. Flattened. Reached around the frame.

Maul held it across the mouth with one hand extended and cut the reaching growth with the saber in the other.

Fresh wood formed. Blackened. A narrow arm caught the iron edge and began dragging the body toward the opening.

Maul's damaged leg bent as the frame shifted against him.

He shouted and pushed it back.

The furnace roared around K.K.'s folded ribs.

“You wanted something beneath your feet.”

Maul kept holding.

Another hand reached stone outside.

The red blade came down.

It fell away.

Maul stayed at the opening while heat reached the protected inner folds. The living tissue stopped repairing itself. The last essential heartwood charred through.

He lowered the frame only after the body inside collapsed.

The ordinary walking stick stood unburned beside the wall.

Maul took the map. The stick stayed where K.K. had put it.

At the first switchback, his damaged leg locked. He caught the retaining wall, then lowered himself into an abandoned road shelter.

Below him, the furnace kept burning.

He bound his arm with torn cloth and pulled a bloody splinter from above his collar. The chart showed three routes into the valley. Two were marked beside collapsed road sections. The third passed the works.

The tree's people might return there. He could watch the approach while the leg carried less weight.

Afternoon light moved across the map on his knees.

Eventually he stood, found he could move if he kept the knee nearly straight, and began the slow traverse above the works.

The worker from the crossing had taken the longer road downriver.

By the time he reached the poultry shop, the light was failing.

The seller had begun closing up. The cart from Tampon Pandemic Productions was gone. Kaze and the others had taken the road back toward their camp only a little earlier.

The worker had to ask before anybody understood him.

“The tree. The tall one. His people.”

The seller came out from behind the counter.

“What happened?”

The man put a hand against the doorframe.

“Where did they go?”

The seller pointed.

The worker went after them.

He caught them at the turn where the road descended toward the stream.

Arthur heard his stumbling steps first and turned.

The man stopped when he saw the red eye under the hat.

Then looked past him at Kaze.

“You know the tree?”

Chris came closer.

“K.K.?”

The worker nodded.

“He helped us. At the upper works. There was a man.”

Chris stopped moving.

“What man?”

The worker described the red-and-black face. The horns. The two red blades on one handle. The invisible force that had held a cart over empty air.

Arthur's gaze moved toward the hills.

Kaze listened without speaking.

“Did you see him leave?” Chris asked.

“I ran when they started. I came for—”

“Did you see K.K. leave?”

The man shook his head.

Chris looked upstream.

Inside BELLY, Billy leaned toward the headlamp feed.

Kaze asked, “Which road?”

The worker pointed past the ridge.

“Old crossing. Then the kiln. It's far by the road.”

Kaze turned to Arthur.

“Bring him.”

Arthur nodded.

Kaze put one foot onto the stone beside the road and launched.

The stone split beneath him.

He crossed the first ridge in a low, hard arc. The next contact broke a shelf of rock and sent him over the upper valley toward the glow the worker had described.

He crossed the valley fast enough for the second impact to arrive before the first dust had settled.

When he landed on the terrace, the abandoned tools jumped.

He looked at the smashed cart.

The scored stone.

The blackened branches.

Then at the walking stick beside the wall.

The noise inside him went very quiet.

The furnace was still hot. He approached the damaged mouth and looked through it.

There was enough of the narrow shape left to understand what had been trapped there. Not enough left alive to answer him.

Kaze stood without moving.

For once, the place had nothing he wanted to destroy that would make the thing he wanted happen.

He picked up the stick.

On the terrace, drops of Maul's blood marked the direction he'd taken. A damaged mechanical foot had dragged through the dust between them.

Kaze crouched.

Blood and scorched cloth held a scent distinct from the furnace smoke. Kaze followed it past a scrape where a metal foot had caught the road.

He followed the marks to the upper road.

Then he saw a figure moving along the pale switchback beyond the works.

Dark clothes. One uneven step. A long hilt in one hand.

Kaze put the walking stick down beside the road, where it wouldn't be under the next impact.

He jumped.

Maul felt danger before the hillside in front of him broke.

He stopped and drew the saber as Kaze landed between him and the next bend.

Both red ends lit.

Dust moved around Kaze's bare shoulders. His gold upper grills showed for an instant as his mouth opened.

“You meet a tree?”

Maul looked at the visible name across his stomach, then at his face.

“Kaze.”

“I asked you something.”

Maul adjusted his grip. Blood had made one hand slippery.

“It chose to attack me.”

Kaze's head tilted.

“You always start the story where you look better?”

Maul moved.

The saber swept toward Kaze's neck while a thrust of the Force struck his body from the other side. It was a real attempt to control his position and cut through the space he'd be forced into.

Kaze saw the attack begin. His danger sense had already fixed his attention on it.

He drove against the displacement, broke the stone under his feet, and moved inside the blade's arc.

His right hand closed around Maul's weapon wrist.

The red end passed behind his shoulder without reaching his neck.

Maul kicked for his knee.

Kaze took the strike without losing the grip.

Then he bent the wrist backward until the bones broke through the skin.

The saber dropped.

Maul reached for it with the Force.

Kaze drove him into the rock face before the hilt could return.

The impact broke a deep bowl in the hillside. Maul's back hit first; his ribs collapsed under the hand that followed. Blood came out of his mouth onto Kaze's forearm.

He was still trying to lift the saber when Kaze caught his face.

“No,” Kaze said.

He pulled Maul away from the wall and slammed him down through the stone at their feet.

The road burst outward.

His skull broke beneath Kaze's palm. The upper body folded into the new crater, flesh and shattered bone driven against the mechanical frame below it. Red blood splashed Kaze's chest and face. The reaching hand stopped reaching.

The saber struck the ground beside them, still lit.

Kaze stood over what he'd made.

The red blade hummed against loose stone. Nothing in the crater moved.

Kaze looked down at his bloody hand.

Then back toward the works.

He hadn't gotten an answer he could use.

He walked away from the crater, leaving the weapon where it lay. He collected the stick where he'd left it and went back down to the furnace.

By the time Arthur reached the upper terrace with the others, the worker was struggling to keep up with him. Daynuh had come with Mahnuh. Chris stayed exterior, the banjo quiet at his belt.

Kaze was sitting beside the furnace with the walking stick across his knees.

Chris looked at the red blood on him.

“Where?”

Kaze pointed toward the upper road.

“Done.”

Arthur looked in that direction, then at the kiln.

He didn't ask for the man's name yet.

The worker stopped at the shattered cart.

“He was here.”

Chris walked past him.

He saw the wood inside the furnace.

Stopped.

Billy's voice came through the banjo, low enough that only the people near Chris heard it.

“Christopher.”

Chris didn't answer.

He moved closer, looking for something that still moved. Something green. A living fold protected from the heat. He knew K.K. could grow back from injuries; he'd seen wood close where another person would have kept a scar.

There wasn't anything left doing it.

The worker began explaining the crossing.

How K.K. had helped.

The conversation above the shed.

The demand for the walking stick.

“He told me to go,” the man said. “I thought if I found you—”

Chris turned.

“You did.”

The worker looked at him.

Chris swallowed.

“You found us.”

That was all he could give him.

Arthur waited until the worker had caught his breath.

“Start where you were standing.”

The man pointed toward the steps.

“He asked the driver about the broken ground. Then your friend said something to him.”

“What?”

“About saving a man to talk to his paper.”

Chris shut his eyes for a moment.

“Yeah. That sounds like him.”

“They moved up here. I went back for the wedge. When I came up, the stranger had his stick.”

Kaze looked at the timber across his knees.

“Took it?”

“I didn't see that part. Your friend said to give it back. The stranger wanted to know where you were.”

Arthur glanced toward Kaze, then returned to the worker.

“He give a name?”

“Maul. Before they went up the steps.”

“And you left when?”

“Your friend told me to. I saw the red thing come on. Then the first hit. After that I ran.”

He looked past Arthur toward the furnace.

“I should've stayed.”

“Where?” Chris asked.

The man pointed helplessly at the terrace.

Chris looked at the broken brick, the iron driven through the wall, the deep cuts across the stone.

“Which fucking bit?”

The worker's hand dropped.

Billy's voice came through the banjo.

“Did he have the stick when you last saw him?”

“I didn't see. It was there when I came back.”

Billy asked Chris to turn toward the wall. Through the headlamp he examined the little clear space beside it, protected from the cart's first broken timbers.

Then he asked to see the rest of the terrace.

Chris gave him the view slowly. The fish took in the damage without pretending he could watch backward through it.

Arthur wrote Maul's name on the back of a loose scrap. Beside it he put the worker's account, stopping at the point where the man had stopped seeing.

Daynuh put a hand on Mahnuh's shoulder. The dog had reached the scattered branches and was smelling along them, following the familiar scent into something he couldn't complete.

“Here,” Daynuh said.

Mahnuh came back to him.

Arthur stood beside Kaze.

“You see all of it?”

Kaze shook his head.

“Saw enough.”

Arthur looked toward the upper road again.

“We should get that weapon off the road.”

Kaze's fingers tightened around the ordinary stick.

“Then get it.”

Arthur went up the switchback. At the broken section, he found the body, the scorched stone beside the still-active blade, and the long hilt lying clear of Maul's hand.

He approached from the side, keeping out of both red ends. He'd just watched Clifford use a similar kind of weapon. That didn't make every control identical. He studied the hilt before touching the recessed switch with his gloved hand.

The blades went out.

He wrapped the hilt in a spare piece of cloth from his satchel and brought it back, keeping the control beneath a fold where a careless grip wouldn't press it.

The survey map lay torn beside the body. Arthur recovered that too.

Down at the works, Chris had found the workers' long iron tongs. He waited until they could safely reach what remained in the mouth of the furnace, then lifted the larger pieces into an empty stone trough away from the heat.

Nobody pretended the pieces would start talking if they were arranged correctly.

Billy asked to see the stick.

Chris turned toward it so the headlamp gave him the view.

Kaze held it up.

For a while the fish said nothing.

Then, “He put that aside.”

The worker looked at it.

“Had it when he came here.”

Kaze looked at the unburned timber in his hands.

He had taken care of the thing he was carrying.

That detail hurt in a place the blood on Kaze's chest couldn't reach.

The workers returned to the upper works one at a time after the noise stopped.

Arthur told them which stretch of road was broken now. He didn't tell them they had witnessed justice or try to sell Kaze as the man who made the country safer.

The foreman looked toward the furnace, then at the red blood drying on Kaze's skin.

“That one did all this?”

“Some,” Arthur said.

The man understood enough to stop asking that question.

He showed them an intact path back downriver. It went farther around the hill than the damaged road, but nobody needed to risk the new crater to reach the stream.

Arthur asked him to keep travelers off the upper bend until somebody had marked the damage.

“There's a body there too.”

The foreman's eyes moved toward Kaze.

“I gathered.”

“Weapon's with me. Don't let somebody go digging through the wreckage in the dark.”

The man called one of the returning workers over. Together they dragged an unbroken beam across the upper approach, well short of the cracked section. Arthur waited until it was in place before coming down the steps.

Before they left, the worker from the crossing approached Kaze.

“He didn't charge us.”

Kaze looked at him.

“For the bridge.”

“He wanted to look.”

The worker nodded.

“I said he could.”

Kaze stood.

For a second the man thought he'd done something wrong.

Then Kaze handed him the walking stick.

The worker took it automatically.

Kaze looked at the hand holding it.

He took it back.

“No.”

The worker let go immediately.

Kaze stared at the stick as though it had asked him a question in somebody else's voice.

Then he went down the steps.

The others followed.

Chris carried the cooled remains they could recover, wrapped in a work cloth the foreman had given him. He held the bundle carefully without pretending it was a patient.

Billy stayed quiet through the banjo.

That evening, they placed the remains in the ground near the pear tree K.K. had bought.

Chris lowered the bundle himself. He waited beside the open earth until Arthur asked him for room to finish.

Daynuh settled Mahnuh away from the open earth while Arthur worked. Chris stepped back. Kaze put the ordinary stick across the fresh ground.

The place where their guest had stood was empty.

Chris stood looking at the pear tree.

“He was gonna tell me what he found.”

Arthur leaned on the tool he'd borrowed from the works.

“I know.”

Chris rubbed his mouth.

“I planted a fucking vegetable.”

From the banjo came Billy's voice.

“And met somebody.”

Chris nodded once.

Nothing clever followed it.

Later, Kaze sat beside the stream washing Maul's blood from his hands. The water took what it could. Red remained beneath his nails and in the creases around his knuckles. His pants were still stained.

Arthur put the wrapped double-ended hilt on a dry stone beside him.

“Keeping it?”

Kaze looked at the shape beneath the cloth.

“You found it.”

“Didn't ask who found it.”

The water moved around Kaze's fingers.

“Not tonight.”

Arthur picked it up again.

Across the camp, Chris had put a little of the evening's food aside without looking at the empty space where K.K. usually stood.

Then he'd noticed what he'd done.

He left it there.

Daynuh sat with Mahnuh's head against his leg. Billy watched through the headlamp when Chris turned toward the tree, and did not supply a history lesson.

Kaze dried his hands on the grass and went back to the fire.

He stopped beside Arthur.

“That map.”

Arthur looked up.

“Got it.”

“Tomorrow.”

Arthur nodded.

Kaze looked toward the upper hills, where the damaged roads ran deeper into Noxus.

Then he sat down with the people who were still there.
