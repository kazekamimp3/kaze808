Season 3, Episode 56: “Somewhere Mine”

Outside, Billy stood perfectly still.

“Christopher.”

For once, he didn't get an answer immediately.

“Christopher. Tell me.”

“He's gone.”

The banjo gave him those two words in the same voice that had spent the morning insulting his summer house.

Billy lowered himself to one knee.

“Bring him out,” Chris said. “Careful.”

Billy opened his mouth. He released Frylock into his waiting hands and eased the ruined body onto the clear grass beside him.

Mahnah came forward to sniff.

Billy held up a palm.

“No, sir.”

The dog stopped.

Billy kept the hand there until Mahnah backed away. Then he looked down again.

Inside, Meatwad stayed where Frylock had been.

Chris stood slowly.

Shake had gone to the far side of the table.

“I want out.”

Chris looked at him.

“What?”

“I'm leaving.”

“Now?”

Shake glanced at the floor.

“I can't stay here.”

Chris took a step toward him.

“What happened?”

“I told you.”

“Tell me again.”

“He went over the side.”

“How?”

Shake spread his hands.

“I don't know what to tell you. He was there. Then he wasn't.”

Meatwad turned.

“He could fly.”

“I know that.”

“Then why'd he fall?”

“I don't know, Meatwad.”

Chris looked at the burn on Shake's rim.

“And that?”

“He came out shooting. I got hit.”

That was something Chris had heard. The flash. Shake shouting. It didn't tell him what had happened before it.

Outside, Billy turned toward his banjo.

“What is he asking?”

“Shake wants to leave.”

“Did he say why?”

Chris kept his eyes on Shake.

“Says he can't stay.”

Billy looked down at Frylock.

Then up the road, where Daynah had gone.

“Does he have things to collect?”

Chris looked at the table. His camera was still recording.

He stopped it and picked it up.

Shake's eyes followed it.

“You taking anything?” Chris asked.

“No.”

“Not even the silence bottle?”

Shake didn't answer.

Chris put the camera at his own station, away from Shake's hands.

“He says he's going like he is, Billy.”

Billy was quiet for a moment.

“Tell him this is not a walk. If he wishes to leave this household, I will permit it. I am not promising to collect him later.”

Chris repeated it.

“Fine,” Shake said.

Meatwad looked at him.

“You're just going?”

“Somebody has to.”

“That ain't an answer.”

Shake looked toward Charles.

Charles had moved away from the doorway. He stood beside his own banjo station, one hand on the back of the chair.

“You got something to say?” Shake asked him.

“No.”

“Good.”

“Didn't say it was good.”

Shake turned back toward Chris.

“I'm ready.”

Chris told Billy.

Billy released him onto the road below the camp. Shake landed on his feet, steadied himself and looked up at the enormous catfish kneeling beside Frylock's body.

For a moment, neither spoke.

“You have been allowed to leave,” Billy said. “Do not mistake that for my knowing what happened.”

Shake's hand moved toward his burned rim, then dropped.

“I didn't ask you to know.”

He started down the road in the other direction, back toward the old works.

Billy watched until he passed out of sight around the bend.

No thunderbolt. No cosmic hand dragged him back into the cup holder. He walked away from a place that had fed him, and he kept walking.

Inside, Meatwad looked at the mark Frylock had left on the floor.

“He'll come back.”

Chris said nothing.

“Shake. I mean Shake.”

“He better ask first.”

Meatwad looked up at him.

Chris turned toward the rag he'd left in the workshop.

“Don't step in that. I'll get it.”

By the time Daynah returned with the others, Billy had moved Frylock out of the road's sightline.

Arthur stopped beside him.

Kaze came up on the other side.

“What the fuck?”

Billy looked at him.

“The acid.”

“How?”

“I don't know.”

Kaze looked from Billy to the banjo.

“Chris?”

“I wasn't in the room.”

Arthur took his hat off.

Daynah got a hand under Mahnah's collar and brought him farther back.

Nobody needed the dog investigating this.

Kaze crouched beside the body. He studied the damage, then looked away toward the empty road.

“Where's the big fucking drink?”

“Left,” Chris said.

“You let him?”

Billy's eyes moved to him.

“Yes.”

Kaze stood.

He looked up.

“You see what happened?”

I heard the splash when Chris did. I saw what he ran back to.

“Before that.”

I didn't see it.

Kaze looked at Billy's banjo again.

“Somebody was in there with him.”

“I know,” Chris said.

“We can—”

“I know.”

Chris's voice scraped on the second word.

Kaze stopped.

Billy looked toward the higher ground.

Arthur looked at the higher patch of earth behind the camp.

“Up there?”

Billy followed his gaze.

“Yes. Above where the water runs.”

Kaze walked to it first.

He knelt and drove his hands into the ground. The soil gave way under his fingers. He tore it out in heavy clumps and threw them aside, opening the grave without making anybody go shopping for a fucking shovel.

Arthur cleared the loose earth away from the edge.

Daynah stayed with Mahnah.

Billy carried Frylock up and laid him in the hole. He stayed kneeling beside it while Kaze brought the first earth back in. Arthur helped cover what remained.

There was no useful joke waiting in the dirt. The dirt offered no fucking notes.

At the harbor, Brian had found a useful joke.

At least, that was the diagnosis he'd given it.

Clifford sat on the edge of the quay, feet above the water, helmet beside him. Brian approached with the journal held against his chest.

“Do you have a moment?”

Clifford looked over.

“For what?”

“Constructive feedback.”

“On what?”

Brian opened the book.

“Recent professional experiences have inspired a short piece.”

Clifford looked toward the submarine.

The plank behind Brian remained cruelly empty.

“All right.”

Brian cleared his throat.

“Started five-ten, now the world's on pause,
Still the bigger man, that's a metaphor, because—”

He stopped.

Clifford waited.

“That isn't part of it,” Brian said.

“Which bit?”

“The explanation.”

“Oh.”

Brian started again from the next line.

“Head of my field when I had no torso,
Shoulder-to-shoulder, except I was more so.
Built my own body, self-made man,
Morgan acquisition, strategic plan.”

He looked up.

Clifford's face had changed.

“You ate that man.”

“I'm using professional language.”

“That's a man you ate.”

Brian looked down at the page.

“The point is resilience.”

“You rhymed man with plan.”

“Yes.”

“I got that part.”

Brian turned the journal slightly, shielding the rest.

“Perhaps the setting isn't conducive.”

Clifford looked at the water.

“I don't think the water did that to it.”

Brian shut the book.

“Thank you.”

“You asked.”

Brian held the closed book against his chest.

“I don't intend to remain the person everybody finds amusing.”

Clifford looked over.

“I wasn't laughing.”

“I am aware.”

“You want me to lie?”

Brian took a step away, then stopped.

“Kaze remembers what I was.”

Clifford stayed quiet.

“Not this. Before this. I had a life. I had a career. I was five foot ten.”

“Yeah. You said.”

“Because it's relevant.”

Clifford rubbed a thumb along the edge of the helmet.

“You think he thinks about it?”

Brian looked at him.

“What?”

“What he did to you.”

“He will.”

“I keep thinking about those other two. They probably don't think about me at all.”

Brian looked at the old damage on Clifford's neck, then back at the journal.

For once, he didn't offer a rhyme.

“That doesn't make it less,” he said.

Clifford nodded toward the book.

“Put that in there.”

Brian looked down at it.

“It's in a different register.”

Clifford blinked.

“It's a sentence.”

“I know it's a sentence. I'm saying it lacks the musical framework.”

“Then say it with a fucking drum.”

Brian opened the journal just enough to look at the last verse.

“The conclusion addresses the relevant party directly.”

“Don't.”

“It's only four lines.”

“I heard the first ones.”

Brian looked down anyway.

“Kaze on the page, rage in the margins,
You took the inches, I'm taking the bargains—”

He stopped himself.

Clifford looked out over the harbor.

“What's that mean?”

“Negotiating from an improved position.”

“You think he'll know that?”

“He'll know what I mean.”

“I don't. And you're right here explaining it.”

Brian pressed the journal shut.

“The point isn't merely to threaten. It's to demonstrate I remain capable of producing something.”

Clifford looked back at him.

“You talked to that guy yesterday. You had an idea. The Doctor listened.”

“Yes.”

“That was something.”

Brian rubbed the corner of the cover with his thumb.

“So is this.”

“Brian.”

“I'm not asking you to perform it.”

“Please get the fuck away from me with the book.”

Brian stared at him.

Then he tucked it under his arm, straightened his shirt and nodded as if Clifford had confirmed a reservation.

“Understood.”

He took another step.

Stopped again.

“For the record, the third line is better.”

Clifford put both hands over his face.

Brian opened the journal and added a note.

“Strong emotional response,” he said as he wrote. “Further review unnecessary.”

Then he looked at Clifford again.

“Anyway. We've both grown.”

Clifford looked at him.

Brian took one sharp breath through his nose.

“Artistically.”

He went back toward the submarine.

Doctor Evil was near the plank.

“How did it go?”

Brian stopped.

“How did what go?”

“The assessment. You took the book.”

“A productive exchange.”

The Doctor held out a hand.

Brian hesitated, then handed him the journal.

The Doctor opened it at the marked page. He read a few lines without changing expression.

Then he lowered the book.

“Brian. Have you considered making this your resignation letter?”

Brian reached for it.

The Doctor kept it where he was holding it. Too high.

Brian's fingers paused in the air.

The Doctor looked down at them, then at the book, as if this small piece of geometry had just occurred to him.

He bent and returned it.

“Keep writing,” he said. “I'm sure someone will stop you.”

Brian tucked the journal under his arm.

“I appreciate the encouragement.”

The Doctor watched him carry it onto the plank.

“Of course you do.”

Clifford stayed by the water.

He didn't put the helmet back on.

Inland, the grave was filled before Chris came outside.

He and Billy changed places beside the disturbed earth. The banjo went into the mouth with its owner; Chris straightened in the grass, and Billy arrived full-sized inside with the same instrument.

Meatwad waited near the lounge monitor.

Charles stood behind him.

They watched Chris kneel at the grave through his headlamp's view. They could hear him breathe. The camera on the gaming station was off.

“You want to say something?” Arthur asked.

Chris pressed his palm against the loose soil.

“He wanted to look through my parts.”

Kaze glanced at him.

“This morning. Wanted to figure something out.”

Chris rubbed the dirt off his palm.

“I told him later.”

Arthur put his hat back on.

Daynah looked down at Mahnah, keeping the dog beside him.

From inside, Billy spoke through Chris's headgear.

“We both did.”

Chris stayed there a moment longer.

Then he stood.

“I'm not putting the bottle on his grave.”

Kaze looked at him.

“Wasn't gonna suggest it.”

“I know how you get.”

Kaze's mouth moved.

It wasn't quite a smile.

“Yeah.”

Chris looked toward the road.

“Give me a minute.”

Arthur nodded and took the others down toward their gear.

Chris stayed by the grave until Billy said his name.

“What?”

“Charles has a question about the camera.”

Chris looked down at his own hands.

“Tell him to ask.”

Inside, Billy turned toward Charles.

Charles looked at the camera on Chris's station.

“What's on it from today?”

“The interview,” Meatwad said.

“After that.”

Meatwad was quiet.

“Shake turned it on,” Charles said. “I saw him when we went out.”

Billy looked at the camera.

Then at the place on the floor Chris had cleaned.

“Christopher.”

Outside, Chris's jaw tightened.

“Yeah. I know.”

They changed again.

Billy returned to the grave's side with his banjo. Chris arrived inside and went straight to the station.

Meatwad followed him.

Charles came as far as the couch.

“You all right to look at this?” Chris asked Meatwad.

“I want to see.”

Chris brought up the newest clip on the camera's own display. He held it low enough for Meatwad. Charles moved closer to see over his shoulder.

The picture began with Shake's face too close to the lens.

“This is what happens when you let amateurs make a documentary.”

He backed away into the couch shot.

Frylock hovered at the left edge of the frame. The bottle was still beside the couch.

Shake began explaining the show.

His version had one host.

It wasn't either of the two people who'd started it.

On the little screen, Frylock looked toward the passage Charles and Meatwad had used.

“They'll be back.”

“Good. They can listen.”

“Turn it off until they agree.”

Shake glanced toward the camera.

“They agreed by participating.”

“They aren't participating. They're in another room.”

Shake reached for the Pepsi.

Two of Frylock's fries curled around the neck of the bottle first.

“And leave this.”

“You already had some.”

“That doesn't make it yours.”

Shake took a step closer.

Frylock drifted sideways through the open doorway, keeping the open bottle away from him. He stayed in the air over the raised edge, with the acid below and behind him.

Shake stopped at the lip.

“I wasn't going to take it.”

“Then this should be very easy.”

He held the bottle back toward the couch.

Shake slapped at it.

Pepsi flew across the armrest. The bottle struck the seat and rolled out of sight.

Frylock turned toward the spill.

Shake lunged.

Both hands struck Frylock high on the carton. He drove forward and down, shoving him out of the air and below the edge.

The splash hit the living lip.

Shake stayed bent over it, one hand braced on the top of Frylock's carton, pressing him below the surface.

The splash speckled his glove. He kept pressing.

“How's that for—”

A beam flashed up from below the frame.

Shake jerked back, hit the couch and shouted. His upper rim smoked.

At the far edge of the picture, Frylock rose through the splash under his own power. He was still flying. The damage had already been done.

He crossed the lip badly and struck the couch.

Chris came into the shot.

The real Chris stopped the playback.

Meatwad stared at the darkened display.

Charles had backed away.

Nobody needed me to name the man whose fucking hands they'd just watched.

Outside, Billy's fingers tightened on the banjo.

He wasn't beside the camera, but he'd caught the picture the audience had just been shown. One of his old tricks, coming back with something he didn't want.

“I saw that, Christopher.”

Chris took a breath.

“Shake pushed him. I got it on the camera.”

“He was holding him down,” Billy said.

“Yeah. Frylock came back up. He got himself out.”

Chris looked toward the cleaned floor.

“That's what the burn was.”

Billy stayed on one knee.

Down the slope, Arthur looked up at the banjo's voice. Kaze turned toward it. Daynah remained beside Mahnah.

“He lied to me,” Billy said.

Inside, Meatwad turned toward the couch.

“He said he fell.”

Charles looked at him.

“He said a lot.”

“You didn't see?”

Charles shook his head.

Meatwad stayed there for another moment.

Then he went to get his stone.

Chris set the camera down.

“Where you going?”

Meatwad held the stone against himself.

“Outside.”

“You need some air?”

“No. I want to go.”

Chris watched him.

Charles came off the couch.

“He's already gone. You ain't gotta go because he did.”

“I ain't going with him.”

“Then stay.”

Meatwad looked at the place where Frylock had hovered that morning.

“I want a place that's mine.”

Chris rubbed his face.

“We can put your shit somewhere, man. We talked about it.”

“I know.”

“Shake ain't getting back in here.”

“I know that too.”

Meatwad looked up at him.

“I still want to go.”

Charles opened his mouth.

Closed it.

He looked toward his banjo station.

“You taking the camera?”

Meatwad looked at Chris.

“It ain't mine.”

“You can take a picture before you leave,” Chris said.

Meatwad thought about it.

Then shook his head.

“Don't want one today.”

Chris bent down.

“You got somewhere picked out?”

“Not yet.”

“You know what's out there?”

“I been out there.”

“Not all of it.”

Meatwad looked around the lounge.

“I ain't seen all of this either.”

Chris sat back on his heels.

For a moment, the only sound was the live road outside coming through the monitor.

Then he nodded.

“Billy. He wants to leave too. His own choice.”

Billy listened while Chris told him what Meatwad had actually said.

When Chris finished, Billy addressed the banjo.

“Tell him I would prefer he stayed.”

Chris repeated it.

Meatwad pressed the stone closer.

“Tell him thank you.”

Chris repeated that too.

Billy looked at the grave.

Then toward the open country on the other side of the road.

“Very well.”

Inside, Charles came down to Meatwad's level.

“You want me to come?”

Meatwad looked at him.

“You want to?”

Charles glanced at his station again. At the couch. At Chris.

“I got things here.”

“I know.”

“I'm making something.”

“I know, Charles.”

Charles touched the stone with one finger.

“Don't lose that.”

“That's why I'm taking it.”

Charles nodded.

Meatwad rolled toward the place Chris indicated, keeping hold of the small piece of Death Mountain he'd chosen to keep long before anybody wanted to tell him where he belonged.

Billy released him onto clear ground beside the camp.

Daynah brought Mahnah back a pace. The dog watched the small, living thing arrive but stayed with his man.

Meatwad looked up at Billy.

Billy removed his hat.

“I hope you find a good door.”

“Me too.”

Meatwad looked at the grave.

He went to it first.

Nobody followed him up the little slope.

Daynah waited at the bottom with Mahnah sitting close against his leg.

When Meatwad came down, he looked toward the road.

“You ain't gotta go that way,” Daynah said.

“I wasn't.”

Daynah nodded toward the lower ground beyond the grass.

“Wheels won't see you easy.”

Meatwad looked up at him.

“You see me.”

“I'm looking.”

Meatwad held the stone a little tighter.

“All right.”

Mahnah leaned forward to smell him.

Daynah touched the back of the dog's head and stopped him short.

“Let him go.”

Meatwad looked at the huge muzzle. Then at Daynah's hand resting on it.

“Tell him bye for me.”

“He heard you.”

Meatwad faced the open grass again.

He turned away from the road Shake had taken and crossed the short grass toward a low line of scrub, moving at his own pace with his own stone.

Billy watched him go.

Arthur came to stand beside him.

“You all right?”

Billy put his hat back on.

“Not especially.”

Arthur stayed beside him anyway.

Inside, Chris found Charles standing by the camera.

His hand rested near it, without touching.

“Leave that clip,” Chris said.

“I wasn't taking it off.”

“I'm telling you.”

Charles nodded.

“What about the rest?”

Chris looked at the little display.

All those ordinary minutes. Bad questions. A couch. People getting in each other's fucking way.

“Leave that too.”

Charles moved his hand.

He stayed beside the station.

Chris waited.

“You want something?”

Charles glanced at the camera.

“We didn't finish.”

“I know.”

“He was gonna ask me. Then I was gonna ask him.”

Chris turned the camera so it no longer faced either of them.

“You got the other stuff.”

“That was before.”

“Still happened.”

Charles worked his jaw.

“You gonna let somebody see it?”

“What somebody?”

“Anybody.”

Chris looked toward the couch.

“Not today.”

Charles nodded.

Then he pointed to the camera again.

“Don't put that part first.”

Chris's eyes came back to him.

“What part?”

Charles pointed toward the cleaned floor.

“That.”

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then Chris said, “I ain't making a fucking trailer out of it.”

“I didn't say you was.”

“What are you saying?”

Charles looked at the couch.

“He was here before that. We was all here.”

Chris leaned back in his chair.

“Yeah.”

Charles touched the back of the couch.

“You start a picture there, that's all somebody sees.”

“I ain't deleting him.”

“I'm telling you.”

Chris looked at him for a moment.

Then he turned back toward the camera.

“You want the show to still have his name?”

Charles rubbed his thumb along its worn covering.

“It's his picture too.”

“All right.”

Charles nodded once, without looking pleased.

He went to the couch and straightened the cushion beside his own place. Then he stopped and left it there.

Chris watched him.

“You don't gotta sit there waiting.”

Charles looked over.

“I ain't waiting.”

“Okay.”

“I just moved it.”

“I saw.”

Charles walked past him toward his banjo station.

At the passage, he paused.

“You gonna be in here?”

Chris looked up.

“For a while.”

“All right.”

Charles went on.

Chris stayed where he was. He didn't start the game.

Outside, Kaze came up the slope.

“Chris. You want him brought back?”

The question came through the exterior feed into the lounge.

Chris looked toward it.

“Meatwad?”

“The other one.”

Chris looked past the two monitors toward the clear living wall beyond them.

“No.”

Kaze watched Billy's banjo.

“You sure?”

“He ain't coming back into my home.”

“That's not what I mean.”

“I know what you mean.”

Chris looked at the camera again.

“I'm not spending the day chasing that motherfucker. If we see him again, I'll have something to say.”

Kaze scratched his jaw.

Then nodded once.

“All right.”

He went back down to the road.

Inside, Charles moved toward the couch, then stopped by the armrest.

The Pepsi bottle lay on the floor behind it, empty except for a dark little pool below the neck.

He picked it up.

Chris looked over.

Charles held it out.

“Where you want this?”

Chris stared at it for a moment.

“Put it on the table. I'll get it.”

Charles did.

He went to his own station and sat down without taking the camera.

Billy followed the others onto the inland road. His banjo hung quiet against him.

Behind them, the grave stayed above the waterline.

Ahead, Meatwad had already disappeared into the scrub.

Inside, the little red recording light stayed off.
