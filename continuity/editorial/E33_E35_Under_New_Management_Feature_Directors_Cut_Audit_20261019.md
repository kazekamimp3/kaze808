## The highest-value latent spine: INDEPENDENCE VERSUS SOMEONE ELSE'S MANAGEMENT

The three exact manuscripts already share **one developing argument** without needing a new premise: Wade *fires* Mimir but cannot erase the original narrator's history or Kratos's rescue; Chris *grows* K.K. but cannot claim to own his choices; Shake *buys Chris's chair with Charles's stolen Pepsi* and demands domestic control; Frylock calls an invasive child/Meatwad prank a personhood experiment; Brian tries to brand and manage the conjoined adult Tracy; Dutch and Cartman fight over TPP direction; Clifford regains chosen assisted mobility while other people present him as their cheap archvillain; K.K. openly calls out Kaze for waiting to watch him respond to injury. K.K. planting his **single** pear independently at E35's stream, Meatwad refusing another false death, and Timmy opening the door despite Cartman's instructions all enact the theme differently.

**Do not have Wade lecture that the theme is freedom.** Let those desires repeatedly cause insulting, dangerous or touching events; allow direct disagreement and irony rather than crowning one group's moral argument correct.

### Strongest extra structural insight: REAL vs COUNTERFEIT miracles

E33 enacts two real developments: actual 20-foot, autonomous K.K. born from one rare seed in Chris's garden; actual low-budget assisted walking for Clifford after Sava's consent-based reconstruction. E33 also literally drops/reconnects the ex-narrator Mimir to Kratos. The surgical presentation intentionally withholds Clifford's identity for the cheap Vader-style reveal, **not** a new Star Wars character/power.

E35 is an atrocious parody of E33's real creations: Frylock and Shake stage a child made of offal/wood/string with **existing Meatwad concealed inside**, and Brian stages a false head-without-body exhibit while still conjoined to the irate, full-sized actual Tracy Morgan. In both fake “miracles,” the supposed participant has true independent agency (Meatwad and Tracy); their fraud humiliates the people who tried to manage them. That pattern creates elegant delayed recognition if cues and consequences are fairly planted.

**E34 is the bloody hinge between the genuine marvels and staged counterfeits:** K.K.'s killing is real, deliberate, morally uncomfortable; TPP earns real money through an actual deal that Brian's boast converts into armed coercion. The contrast should carry emotional residue into E35 without turning violence into a “they secretly deserved it” excuse.

The current three scripts largely have these events already; the repair is to make motive → action → transformation of consequence unmistakable, not replace established beats with bigger invented spectacles.
