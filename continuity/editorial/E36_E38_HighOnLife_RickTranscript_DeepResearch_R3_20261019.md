## High on Life: precise, transferable comedy mechanisms

### A. Each Gatlian is both tool AND independent relationship
Robbins said talking guns were designed as RPG companions, each with distinct voice/personality reactions that vary by weapon equipped. As an example, Gus (voiced by J.B. Smoove) was an approachable ally; Creature (Tim Robinson) volatile/wild. Writers sometimes authored four versions of one reaction because any of several weapons could be present. Weapon identity thus changes actual exchange, not merely its accent. Applications:
- In S7NS, give different responses to the same bizarre fortune based on Kaze, Chris, Arthur, Daniel, Dana, Mana, K.K. respectively, including refusals and physical changes.
- BELLY's Billy, Frylock, Shake, Meatwad and Charles are independently motivated; one interior microphone must not produce interchangeable heckling lines from all five.
- No NEW talking Black Marrow. The current sword has nonverbal integrated awareness and only one formed weapon. `High on Life` is COMEDY RESEARCH, not authority to give Black Marrow a mouth or second body.
- Preserve legitimate access: Billy full-sized interior while Chris sole exterior, audio reaches outside only through proven routes, not by sheer writer convenience.

### B. Character action BEFORE speech demands a branch
Meyr documents a playtest where an attack blew up the Mac and Cheese Brothers before introductions. Instead of making the scene play anyway, they wrote a valid response to being preempted. The broader lesson is that a character can insult the host, walk away, fire, hide a cookie, or refuse an absurd premise BEFORE the camera expects them to, and the scene must be rearranged in response. In the restaurant:
- No evenly spaced queue of seven cookie-read speeches if people would break into disagreements, inspect the printing, pocket their slips, or strike first.
- Chris recognizes ACTUAL skim on the very first sip; his reaction interrupts everything else. The rest of the scene must respond to that early decisive choice, NOT obediently wait for storybeats.
- Kaze's locked fortune **“Your greatest enemy is five peaceful minutes, Kaze. And somehow, that motherfucker keeps beating you.”** can sting during a truly tranquil setup; Kaze's own conscious later catastrophic choice proves it privately and to viewers. The paper does not send time-jumping intelligence or invent a new prophecy.
- TPP's juvenile titles and contradictory actions interrupt Dutch's otherwise serious heist before it can become 2,000 words of planning exposition. The payoff may be a win, a worsened injury or witnesses, not a new forced joke.

### C. A functional “dummy script” makes improv coherent
Alec Robbins describes outlining bare navigational dialogue first, layering jokes/character flavor and repeated rewrites, keeping exact instructions when needed. S7NS analog: set scene wants, actual positions, equipment, routes and outcome FIRST, then build wild associative back-and-forth *from* each character's psychological reasons. This is compatible with the existing `Season3_Scene_Decision_Doctrine_20261010.md`, NOT a second mandatory creative protocol.

### D. Comedy can live in physical mechanics, not monologues
Squanch's combat design makes a large-eyed grunt's eye pop from a headshot and temporarily blindly attack others. The gag alters danger and targeting, a real outcome not narration wallpaper. Here:
- For the restaurant, Kaze's world-scale fracture must have physically sensible local-to-Bastion progression, genuine environmental interference and aftermath. Do not pretend an actual tectonic rupture is a throwaway visual.
- For TPP, one member's accidental useful rescue enables a morally ugly theft, which then creates conflicting actual witness testimony. Punchline material arises from mutually incompatible perceptions of the same verifiable act.

### E. Long-form riff must earn listening, and silence matters
`High on Life` allows changing incidental weapon chatter frequency, and source-critical reviewers note boredom from constant tangents, repeated fourth-wall remarks and interchangeable personalities. `High on Knife` writers REMOVED jokey dialogue to let the abandoned warehouse frighten the player. An actual title like the S7NS revised cut can be wildly obscene, while still varying dialogue density. Don't force Wade to crack jokes on each planetary feces chapter or a narrator moral thesis after each confirmed death; let the catastrophic impact at the Bastion register from human perspective before the next joke. The graphic cruelty's dramatic seriousness can amplify, not censor, the absurdity of its mundane trigger.

### F. “80% grounded, 20% strange” is an aesthetic preference, not a fiction law
Art director Mikey Spano said this about grounded vs odd **visual art direction**, not a required 80/20 humor quota for S7NS and certainly not a limit on the bizarre literal planetary childhood. Practical interpretation only: a normal precise kitchen, milk temperature, awkward sturdy chairs and *actual busy people* give the eventual cosmic catastrophe contrast. Do NOT quantify an 80/20 rule or downgrade literal history.

## Rick and Morty transcript findings by episode

### 1. Night Family (S6E4) — tiny refusal becomes a two-state household war
A small labor request (rinse dishes before night people wash them) meets Rick's arrogant refusal. Overnight workers destroy dishes, ask again and seize leverage over bodies/behavior. It is funny because the ask is tiny and a legitimate human dignity argument appears in an absurd automatic-labor technology; Rick protects ego at catastrophic cost. S7NS adaptation: Kaze's “five peaceful minutes” fortune could cause a petty deflection during genuine tranquility rather than invoking an unexplained power; the motive makes it worse. BELLY's disagreements about cooking, noise or who owns food can become actual environmental decisions, but NEVER grant Shake authority over Billy's body or override the sovereign.

### 2. Analyze Piss (S6E8) — ridiculous villain branding masks shame, with a moral reversal
Rick tries refusing an endless parade of self-named attackers; Jerry battles a urine-themed stranger; harm, embarrassment and a devastating death occur. Rick's attempt to fix another's reputation causes renewed misunderstanding. Named absurdity coexists with human consequence and deliberate misunderstanding. Application: TPP's “heroes” cannot be treated as mere costume fantasy; Tracy/Jimmy/Timmy must actually rescue people while Dutch exploits the incident, and real witnesses disagree on culpability. Cartman's outfit may make the public's perception *worse* instead of legitimizing him.

### 3. That's Amorte (S7E4) — delicious meal, monstrous true ingredient, then an economic cascade
The family enjoys perfect spaghetti; Morty learns what it literally is. A well-meant intervention reorders a society toward industrializing the behavior, which produces grotesque unintended social results. The final personal story restores a recognizably human emotional scale. The humor is not just disgusting food. It is emotionally motivated objections causing structural, political and economic change after an absurd dietary rule is normalized. Application: Chris's true skim knowledge coexists with true culinary excellence and agency; a murder over milk sets off real Noxian response through Kaze's fault, not a reaction montage from nowhere. Fate of the innocent restaurant staff is a recorded upcoming R2 selection, not something that already occurred in the old delivered cut.

### 4. Unmortricken (S7E5) — sincere intimacy hidden inside sprawling science-fiction violence
A major continuing obsession resolves through physical risk, then the episode leaves room for a void/ambiguity where triumph should feel complete. Comedy within the action does not require a new gag after every line. Wade post-2024 can step aside for real consequence, not flatten himself into Rick.

### 5. Rickfending Your Mort (S7E6) — meta showmanship becomes antagonist, consequences adjudicated from actual behavior
A cosmic observer uses humiliating “clips” to force characters into a spectacle, then encounters unexpected actual vulnerability and litigation/counterargument. Character objections alter the fake framing. Never import a cosmic observer, flashback device or memory-editing power into S7NS as a free effect. The relevant trick is a fortune that opens a character's exposed nerve without narrator fabricating their thoughts. Wade alone can refer to audience; his framing must not rewrite evidence.

### 6. Fear No Mort (S7E10) — escape itself is a false finish, the target is dependency
A banal Denny's restroom hides a fear experience with repeated supposed exits; the ultimate revelation concerns the central relationship, not just bigger monsters. For S7NS, a sound echoing from Chris's REAL sparse bathroom may create a false emotional “we're done with the planets” timing cue, but the bathroom itself is real and no nested reality loop is imposed without author selection. The early payoff could be Chris's returning voice continuing the gruesome childhood monologue while no one wants to hear more.

### 7. The Ricklantis Mixup (S3E7) — ensemble montage expands a specific setting without losing social causality
Ordinary but morally monstrous institutions (advertising, voting, labor) operate in the Citadel under a single overarching science-fiction invention. The Simple Rick wafer commercial converts a trapped father's precious memory into product; the eventual revolt is itself commodified. S7NS parallel: the Sins' massacre and TPP's contradictory public heroism give contrasting institutions real evidence. Worldbuilding should be shown via people affected and responses, not four-page legislative process.

### 8. The Vat of Acid Episode (S4E8, revisited) — a criticism of one stupid idea drives obscene engineering overkill
Morty correctly critiques Rick's theatrical escape plan and Rick's inflated ego turns the argument into an elaborate revenge teaching mechanism. For Kaze's locked fortune, think *behavioral accusation*, not a spell: he'd rather create chaos than accept five tranquil minutes; the choice that breaks the Bastion is his own. For TPP, Dutch's “plan” could work then provoke somebody else to humiliate him, driving a costly unnecessary flourish. Do not copy the acid-vat or time-reset mechanics.
