### 1. Millennium Falcon operational burn and maintenance ledger
Every operation must track, at minimum: propulsion reserve, hyperdrive readiness, coolant integrity, electrical load, life-support load, hull/pressure integrity, consumables, damage, parts used, and the next required inspection. For consistent forward staging, use a normalized **Falcon Reserve Point (FRP)**: 1 FRP equals 1% of a gauge-verified usable reserve for the system being tracked. Calibrate each tank/system separately; never compare fuel FRP to coolant FRP as if they were the same substance.
- Occupied idle/systems-on: propulsion 0 FRP/hour; auxiliary electrical reserve 1 FRP per 8 hours; life-support consumables 1 FRP per 8 occupied hours. These are staging accounting rates, not a claim about a film’s published technical manual.
- Sustained sublight cruise: propulsion 2 FRP per 30 minutes.
- Hard acceleration, evasive maneuvering, or combat thrust: propulsion 3 FRP per 10 minutes; add observed heat/stress and inspect the affected system afterward.
- Hyperspace entry: 8 propulsion FRP per jump, plus 2 propulsion FRP per hour in hyperspace. A jump cannot begin if the post-entry reserve would fall below 20 FRP or if the cooling/drive/pressure gates are not passed.
- Reserve below 30 FRP triggers a route/mission decision before any discretionary detour. Below 20 FRP, no hyperspace entry. A reserve value is not invented when the gauge has not been read.

### 2. Bearer-warrant cipher ledger — locked dual-layer authentication

1. the physical original physically present and intact;
2. a live biometric/liveness match for the warrant’s currently designated bearer;
3. the locally paired hardware key at the ledger’s authorized terminal; and
4. a successful signature/authorization check against the original’s embedded core.
The bearer’s identity and terminal location are not invented unless an existing source establishes them. No remote biometric spoof, ordinary scan, copied file, or Hondo-held data substitutes for the live bearer and local key.
