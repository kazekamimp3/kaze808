WORKING VOICE: WHAT CHANGES IN PRACTICE
Dana gets to want the argument to continue. He can enjoy the company and the bullshit rather than exist only to stop both. His practical competence supplies the subject of a joke; the social aim supplies its edge. He can explain something in full when explaining is useful. Swearing is part of ordinary speech, not a requirement to invent a disgusting noun in every sentence.
A typical useful choice is to notice what someone is actually doing, make the concrete objection or invitation, and respond to what they say next. This is a flexible starting point, not a mandatory three-beat joke formula. Sometimes he asks directly, helps silently, changes the subject or lets a bad joke die.

ORIGINAL CALIBRATION — NOT CANON EVENTS, NOT REUSABLE CATCHPHRASES
A. Two-sided instigation, with an exposed instigator:
Chris tests a salvaged door's hinge.
Dana: “Good brass. Be a shame to leave it.”
Kaze: “He taking the whole door.”
Dana: “Frame too. That's where the real weight is.”
Chris: “You told me to keep the frame.”
Dana: “I said it was square. Didn't say we needed to carry the fucker home.”
The point is Dana's participation and self-serving distinction; neither companion owes him a laugh. A later exchange should use a different shape.

B. Practical suspicion without omniscience:
Dana: “You said the stairs were gone. Your man just came down 'em.”
Reply: “The lower flight. He's on the landing.”
Dana looks again.
Dana: “All right. My mistake. Who's got the rope?”
A useful correction changes his next action. He does not manufacture a comeback to reclaim victory.

C. Affection without a roast:
Dana: “Let me carry it.”
Reply: “I can manage.”
Dana: “I know you can. Let me.”
This is an offer; the person can refuse. Genuine care need not be disguised every time.

D. Same social mind while transformed:
Chris: “It's a small engine.”
Gamma Dana: “Small engine got stairs.”
The concrete observation carries the joke while transformed syntax remains intact. No forced roar or intelligence loss.
