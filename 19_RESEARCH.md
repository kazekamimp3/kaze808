## How a subplot earns its place

A subplot needs an independent desire and a consequence outside itself. It does not need every character to meet.

For each thread, track:

- What its people want before the main cast arrives.
- What pressure comes from another thread.
- What decision they make rather than simply endure.
- What resource, relationship, public belief or obligation changes.
- Who must respond next, and how they learn enough to respond.

A thematic resemblance is useful but is not itself a causal connection. Heisenberg and Viktor can both seek control without sharing a plan. Their stories become causally connected when a particular patient's recovery changes an employment relationship, or when a discovered experiment changes who will supply either man. That connection needs an actual person, event and information route.

Likewise, simultaneous action is not automatically convergence. An exchange and a theft happening in the same episode need not share a contrived security failure. The existing fragment-transfer proposal already has a separate institutional cause; preserve it.

## The enhancement arcs should change relationships

Frank and 47 now have selected heavyweight packages. Preserve those choices. They are original crossover developments, not retroactive feats of their source versions.

Their parallel should be about agency and dependence:

- Frank deliberately chooses enhancement. Chris alone develops both the biological and cybernetic work, independently of Heisenberg. Frank's participation in decisions is part of the arc, not an inconvenience skipped between fights.
- 47's package is funded through Heisenberg. His greater capability changes the employer's expectations and his own bargaining position. The two fighters do not need symmetrical procedures, matching injuries or an obligatory evenly matched duel. Both can be very strong while having different perception, movement, repair and approach. Chris's expanded role also needs a cost in time, access or divided responsibility. Do not let his new job consume Jesse's independent competence, Heimer's local work or Arthur's established identity. ## Scene and act design

Use this compact card for each major sequence:

| Field | Required answer |
| --- | --- |
| Desire | What does the viewpoint character want here? |
| Pressure | Which earlier decision created the problem? |
| Choice | What do they decide that another character might refuse? |
| Immediate result | What concretely changes in this scene? |
| Displaced cost | Who elsewhere pays for that result or gains from it? |
| Information path | How and when does that person learn enough to respond? |
| Next action | What must happen differently because this scene occurred? |

An act ends when a meaningful problem has changed form, not merely when three episodes have elapsed. Three-episode groupings remain a working presentation convention for this project. Do not copy Arcane's episode count or force all factions into every act ending.

Preserve useful quiet moments by giving them a job. A brief disagreement can establish a boundary that later costs someone a victory. A repair can establish both competence and a relationship.

Dialogue checks remain character-specific. Kaze notices street incentives; Daniel tests manipulation; Frank judges a concrete decision through his experience and values. None should announce the entire plot because the audience needs a briefing.

### The proposed Season 3 progression

**Arrival and reward.** Deadpool briefly steals the narration and explains his own time in Noxus. Black Mimir is bewildered, then regains the narration. The account may be biased, but observable consequences should let the audience orient itself. It does not disclose everyone else's private history or turn prior Season 2 victories into Deadpool's secret work.

The Sins then choose a confrontation that interests them. A useful opening pattern is a clash with a locally celebrated or protected dangerous person whose reputation they decide to challenge. Their motives can include pride, appetite, loyalty and profit; they need not arrive as humanitarian rescuers. Their choice brings reward and attention. Paid work emerges from demonstrated value afterward, not an employer handing them their reason to enter the country.

**Established:** An expansionist ruler who seized power after mutilation in the Ionian wars. His demonic hand and ravens are integral to his position; the biography describes fragments of insight gathered from corpses, not complete knowledge. He also commands at the front. [N1]

**Writing implication:** Evaluate newcomers through their usefulness, independence, and political cost. He can learn from a failure without it secretly having been his plan. Calm strategic speech is a sensible provisional direction, but exact cadence still needs performance evidence.

**Do not:** Grant access to crossover memories on sight, make every ordinary raven his familiar, or have him correctly predict every private choice. Establish what information reaches him and when.

**New scene evidence:** Principles of Strength gives him courteous, politically loaded questioning. Katarina issue 5 explicitly limits his sight: Marcus's magic obscured Marcus, while Katarina remained visible to him. His intervention and explanation are evidence of a particular operation, not proof that he controls every outcome. [N17–N19]

**Unresolved:** Exact branch date, broader limits of demon-derived information, and audio delivery. Textual conversation has now been checked; it is no longer merely inferred from his biography.

### LeBlanc

**Established:** A longstanding manipulator whose identities and magical doubles obscure her actions, including from members of the Black Rose. [N2]

**Writing implication:** Influence should come through believable offers, intermediaries, and conflicting accounts. Her opponents need opportunities to notice inconsistencies. A failed operation is not automatically another successful layer of her scheme.

**Do not:** Treat a broad biography's language about appearing anywhere as proof of unlimited reach, effortless possession, or knowledge of every Elsewhere. Do not declare her the Faceless.

**New scene evidence:** Everywhere, and Everyone was read in full. Its shifting disguises, intermediaries and private observations show information gathering as work. The narrator regrets underestimating Swain; this is especially important evidence against making her infallible. The story's many selves and competing claims to being the true LeBlanc preserve uncertainty about identity rather than establishing a simple, all-knowing hive mind. Its separated historical vignettes are not one simultaneous operation. [N26]

**Further dialogue evidence:** The Demon's Hand shows adaptive interrogation: assumed familiarity, competing suggestions, pressure and an objective she still needs the captive to disclose. Her statements are evidence of what she wants him to believe, not automatically impartial historical narration. This supports a patient, responsive manipulator—not an author surrogate who already knows every secret. Its prepared containment remains specific to that encounter. [N30]

**Unresolved:** Which visual/performance presentation governs the branch. Riot released a visual update in patch 25.07; older art and later presentation need deliberate reconciliation, not a mixture of unrelated skins. [N16]

### Darius

**Established:** Rose from humble origins to become a feared commander of the Trifarian Legion, with strong conviction in his cause and willingness to confront enemies within Noxus. [N3]

**Writing implication:** Respect for demonstrated ability need not mean friendship, recruitment, or tolerance of insubordination. Give him military and civic convictions, not merely a desire for a worthy duel.

**Do not:** Write an unintelligent brute who mistakes destructive power for dependable service.

**New scene evidence:** Blood of Noxus places imperial loyalty beside personal affection and loss. He protects Draven, recognizes practical military pressure and remains committed to Noxian cohesion. Principles of Strength gives him a rougher, less ceremonious voice than Swain. He is neither a witless brute nor an uncomplicated benevolent commander. [N18, N20]

**Visual extension:** Welcome to Noxus now supplies sampled combat evidence; consult C1 in the cinematic ledger for timestamps and the distinction between visible actions and interpretation. This is a bounded reference, not universal power scaling. [N31]

**Unresolved:** Continuous axe choreography, exact timing and vocal delivery. The comic's flashback dates do not select this branch's present. Suggested parentage in the comic is not promoted to an explicit confirmed statement.

### Draven

**Established:** A former soldier whose identity now centers on spectacle, spinning axes, and public acclaim in the reckoning pits. [N4]

**Writing implication:** A newcomer threatens or enhances his audience, not just his physical standing. Rivalry can begin over attention without becoming a fatal feud immediately.

**Do not:** Reduce him to a recurring third-person catchphrase or make showmanship proof that he cannot fight.

**New scene evidence:** Blood of Noxus shows showmanship, recklessness and costly disregard of advice. His need for help after injury does not make his adult arena persona incompetent. Distinguish the comic's earlier periods from his later public position. [N20]

**Unresolved:** Arena rules, status at the chosen date, and audio samples. No assumption that every selected crossover enters his arena.

**Do not:** Treat all Du Couteaus as politically interchangeable, or import every later comic development into an earlier date.

Her commitment to Noxus does not mean automatic submission to family authority. ## What can be estimated about the offscreen interval

The source clock says total elapsed time is unknown. Its recorded seventeen-day interval is only one segment. No supported total emerged from the X.1/X.2 time-marker check. Therefore do not announce that the newcomers have spent a fixed number of months building empires.

Use conditional stages rather than invented dates:

1. **Initial adjustment:** work out immediate survival, what arrived with them, whether home is reachable, and what local authorities can actually observe.
2. **Repeated contact:** establish a small set of encounters, resources, debts, grudges, or local reputations. Characters do not need to meet every native champion.
3. **Earned position:** only if the elapsed interval and on-page setup allow it, propose a patronage offer, independent operation, rivalry, or public identity. None is committed merely by this estimate.

Possible research-led questions—not assigned subplots: Does Thor seek a way back before accepting any purpose here? Does Heimdall treat useful locals with contempt while searching for Odin? Does Magneto see a genuine refuge or conditional acceptance? Does Dutch call a bargain freedom while making his family dependent? These require source-specific psychology before decisions are written as facts.
